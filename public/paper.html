Genovy: a case-intelligence platform for rare-disease workups

Genovy is being built as a patient-to-clinician rare-disease case layer. The platform combines a source-auditable knowledge backbone with a structured case-intelligence direction designed to turn fragmented records into a coherent workup, a clinician-ready brief, and a clearer next-step view.

Author: Ahmed Elmorshedy Status: active working paper Scope: product, data architecture, and clinical workflow direction

Abstract

Rare-disease care is slowed less by a lack of raw data than by a lack of case structure. Patients arrive with years of reports, imaging, labs, genetics, pathology, and specialist notes spread across disconnected systems and file formats. Clinicians inherit a workup burden before they can even reason about the disease. Genovy is designed to address that failure mode. It combines a normalized rare-disease knowledge network with a case layer intended to reconcile fragmented records into an auditable clinical narrative. The live platform already exposes the knowledge backbone: diseases, phenotypes, genes, variants, trials, canonical concepts, evidence relationships, and source refresh state. The next product layer is focused on case ingestion, longitudinal timelines, clinician briefs, and guided next-step workup support. This paper describes the current system, the architectural rationale behind it, and the workflow direction it is being built to support.

1. Problem

Rare-disease patients often spend years between specialists while the underlying case remains operationally illegible. Existing tools tend to cover only one fragment of that journey: document storage, phenotype search, variant interpretation, or trial discovery. Very few systems serve the handoff between the patient record and the clinical workup itself. That gap creates repeated history taking, repeated interpretation, and avoidable diagnostic delay.

Genovy is aimed at the workup bottleneck: the point where fragmented evidence has to be turned into a clinically usable case view.

2. System overview

Knowledge layer

The live backbone stores diseases, phenotypes, genes, variants, trials, evidence, source metadata, canonical concepts, aliases, memberships, and typed relationships in a Postgres-backed graph-oriented schema.

Case layer

The planned case layer turns uploaded records into a structured patient timeline, normalized findings, unresolved workup questions, and a clinician-ready brief with traceability back to source documents.

Product layer

The output surface is intended to be readable and operational: case summaries, referral guidance, workup recommendations, future trial matching, and clinic-facing workflows.

Public API layer

The platform already exposes a versioned read-only API and public platform overview so the backbone can be inspected without exposing admin controls or write paths.

3. Data architecture

Genovy separates entities, relationships, and evidence instead of flattening everything into a single undifferentiated store. That distinction matters in rare disease because the same disease-gene link can have different evidentiary strength than a variant-disease link, and clinical findings may be supporting, excluding, age-dependent, or uncertain.

  • Entity families include diseases, phenotypes, genes, variants, trials, and source records.
  • Canonical resolution sits above raw source entities so noisy source vocabularies can be reconciled without losing provenance.
  • Structured clinical evidence keeps phenotype support, exclusions, natural history, and validity assertions typed instead of implicit.
  • Source sync state is preserved so refreshes, backfills, and auditability remain first-class.

4. Source integration and provenance

The current platform integrates a set of refreshable rare-disease and clinical sources, including MONDO, HPO, Orphadata, ClinGen, ClinVar, and ClinicalTrials.gov. These sources are not treated as static imports. Each connector has sync state, lineage, and a path into canonical concepts and downstream evidence layers. This design keeps the graph inspectable and makes future reprocessing possible without losing the original record of what was ingested and when.

5. Current live capabilities

The live platform already provides a public website, a platform explorer, a read-only API, a source catalog, canonical concept summaries, knowledge search, typed clinical evidence, and a first phenotype-only diagnostic ranking layer. In parallel, the repository includes ML export paths for gene-disease link discovery and read-only novelty analysis workflows.

The important boundary is that the knowledge backbone is live now, while the patient case ingestion and clinician brief layer is the next product stage rather than something already finished.

6. Product direction

The intended operating model is patient-to-clinician, not patient-only and not researcher-only. Patients should be able to bring years of records into one place. Clinicians should inherit a workup surface that is already structured, source-linked, and legible. Downstream modules such as trial matching, eligibility workflows, therapy guidance, and clinic copilots depend on that case layer being correct first.

7. Conclusion

Genovy is being built to solve a specific rare-disease infrastructure problem: the lack of a durable case layer between scattered patient records and meaningful clinical action. The live system already establishes the knowledge backbone and provenance model needed for that goal. The next stage is to turn that backbone into a practical case-intelligence workflow that reduces diagnostic friction and makes hard rare-disease cases easier to reason about, communicate, and move forward.
