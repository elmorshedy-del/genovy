# VWAP and Volume Analysis for Intraday Trading (US equities/ETFs and CME index futures), as of October 2026

Scope: every major VWAP variant (formulas, anchoring, practitioner use), the broader volume-analysis toolkit, and what rigorous evidence says about VWAP/volume as predictive signals vs. execution benchmarks. Instruments: SPY, QQQ, large caps; ES/NQ. Labels used below: **[RIGOROUS]** = peer-reviewed or well-specified academic work; **[WORKING PAPER]** = non-peer-reviewed but specified study; **[PRACTITIONER]** = vendor, trader, book or blog claims; **[DATA]** = exchange/industry statistics. All market-structure figures are flagged with their date.

## 1. VWAP variants: formulas, computation and anchoring

### Takeaway
All VWAP variants share one formula, VWAP = Σ(P·V)/ΣV, and differ only in (a) which trades/bars are included (the anchor and window), (b) the price proxy (true trade price vs. bar "typical price" HLC/3), and (c) which session/venue/trade types are included. The design choice that matters most for an algorithm is the *window definition* (session reset vs. anchored vs. rolling) and *data hygiene* (RTH-only vs. extended hours, odd lots, auction prints, Globex vs. RTH for futures), not the formula itself.

### Cited Findings

**Session (daily) VWAP**
- Standard bar-based formula: `VWAP_t = Σ_{i=1..t} TP_i·V_i / Σ_{i=1..t} V_i`, where typical price `TP_i = (H_i + L_i + C_i)/3`; the first value equals the first bar's typical price; "VWAP calculations start fresh at the open and end at the close"; StockCharts computes it on 1/5/10/15/30/60-min bars, not ticks, and states "VWAP is not defined for daily, weekly, or monthly periods" (in its implementation). [PRACTITIONER] — [StockCharts ChartSchool: VWAP](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap.md)
- The same HLC/3-on-1-minute-bars definition, restricted to regular trading hours (pre/post-market excluded), is what Zarattini & Aziz use in their VWAP backtests ("HLC_t is the average between High, Low and Close of the security in minute t"). [WORKING PAPER] — [Zarattini & Aziz (2023), SSRN 4631351, PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Volume-Weighted-Average-Price.pdf)
- Academic/institutional definitions (Madhavan 2002, Exhibit 1): **Full VWAP** = dollar volume / share volume over the horizon "including all transactions" (the standard, "unambiguously defined"; may be intraday, full-day, or multi-day for orders worked over several days); **VWAP excluding own transactions** (corrects bias when the trader is a large fraction of volume, but can be misrepresentative); **Non-block VWAP** (excludes upstairs/block trades; "common to exclude trades of 10,000 or more shares as a proxy"); **VWAP proxies** (e.g., simple average of open, high, low, close, used where tick data are unavailable); **Value-weighted average price** (weights by dollar value rather than shares). [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan, "VWAP Strategies," ITG / Transaction Performance, Spring 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)

**Anchored VWAP (AVWAP)**
- AVWAP uses the identical formula but "the chartist chooses the first bar to use in the calculation" and "the last bar is always the most recent bar available"; anchors can be "a significant high or low, an earnings announcement, a gap, or any point of interest"; rationale: price action before a regime change "should be excluded from the calculations since it doesn't reflect the same market psychology"; "Where multiple Anchored VWAP lines converge, that can indicate an especially strong area of support or resistance." StockCharts' AVWAP material features Brian Shannon of Alphatrends. [PRACTITIONER] — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Anchored VWAP](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/anchored-vwap.md)
- Brian Shannon (CMT) popularized AVWAP in *Maximum Trading Gains with Anchored VWAP* (100+ charts; builds on his *Technical Analysis Using Multiple Timeframes*); endorsements frame it as identifying "price levels being targeted by large institutional money." [PRACTITIONER] — [Alphatrends: Anchored VWAP book](https://alphatrends.net/anchored-vwap-book/)
- Public implementations commonly auto-anchor to trend highs and trend lows (three lines: session VWAP, VWAP from swing high, VWAP from swing low). [PRACTITIONER] — [TradingView: Anchored VWAP Auto High/Low](https://www.tradingview.com/script/72stxVxI-Anchored-VWAP-Auto-High-Low)

**VWAP standard-deviation bands and percentage bands**
- Volume-weighted variance around the running VWAP: `σ²_t = Σ V_i·(P_i − VWAP_t)² / Σ V_i`, equivalently via running sums `σ²_t = (Σ V_i·P_i²)/(Σ V_i) − VWAP_t²` (guard against negative values from rounding); bands `VWAP_t ± k·σ_t`, k typically 1, 2, 3. [PRACTITIONER] — [NexusFi: VWAP standard deviation bands](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/vwap-standard-deviation-bands); [LuxAlgo: VWAP bands](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vwap-bands/)
- Early-session band estimates are unstable because few observations exist ("the bands whipsaw before settling"); some platforms instead offer fixed-percentage or constant-offset bands, i.e., `VWAP·(1 ± p%)`. [PRACTITIONER] — [LuxAlgo: VWAP bands](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vwap-bands/); [Linn Software: VWAP indicator options](https://www.linnsoft.com/techind/volume-weighted-average-price-vwapi)
- No standards body defines the band formula; implementations differ slightly across platforms. [PRACTITIONER] — [NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/vwap-standard-deviation-bands)

**TWAP**
- TWAP (time-weighted average price) as an execution style slices an order evenly through clock time; Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara state "TWAP is highly predictable and should be avoided," while "VWAP joins the herd, however in a predictable way." [RIGOROUS, JPM 2012] — [Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara, "The Volume Clock," SSRN 2034858](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2034858) (full text: [PDF copy](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf))

**VWAP computed on ticks vs bars**
- Odd-lot trades are included in consolidated volume but excluded from last-sale, high and low calculations (CTA plan change approved 2013); a tick-level VWAP that includes all volume-eligible trades can therefore sit *outside* the bar's displayed high/low. [RIGOROUS/REGULATORY] — [SEC release 34-70898 (NYSE CTA odd-lot filing)](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyse/2013/34-70898.pdf); practitioner report of VWAP above bar high: [Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/historical-vwap-data-very-wrong/14272/2)
- Madhavan lists OHLC-average "VWAP proxies" as a fallback where tick data are unavailable, implying that bar-proxy VWAP is an approximation rather than the benchmark definition. [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)

**Futures VWAP across Globex vs RTH**
- ES trades on CME Globex Sunday 6:00 p.m. – Friday 5:00 p.m. ET with a daily 5:00–6:00 p.m. ET maintenance halt. [DATA] — [CME E-mini S&P 500 contract specs](https://cmegroup.com/markets/equities/sp/e-mini-sandp500.contractSpecs.html)
- ES daily settlement is itself a VWAP: the volume-weighted average of lead-month Globex trades between 14:59:30 and 15:00:00 CT, rounded to the nearest 0.25 point (midpoint of bid/ask in that window if no trades); an older 2018 CFTC filing listed a 15:14:30–15:15:00 CT window. [DATA] — [CME daily settlement procedure (ES/MES)](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/E4DaBw); [CFTC filing 2018](https://cftc.gov/filings/orgrules/rule012618cmedcm002.pdf)

**VWAP of specific participants**
- Madhavan's "VWAP excluding own transactions" is a formal participant-conditioned VWAP; Berkowitz, Logue & Noser (1988) justify VWAP as "a weighted average of transaction prices on both sides of the trade" representing prices facing a non-strategic trader. [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf); [Berkowitz, Logue & Noser (1988), JF 43(1):97-112](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1988.tb02591.x)

### Inferences
- **Rolling/moving VWAP** (N bars or N minutes): `RVWAP_t = Σ_{i=t−N+1..t} P_i·V_i / Σ_{i=t−N+1..t} V_i`. **VWMA** is the same object computed on closes: `VWMA_t(N) = Σ C_i·V_i / Σ V_i` over the last N bars; it differs from a session VWAP only by having a fixed lookback rather than an anchor. **Multi-day/weekly/monthly VWAP** = anchored VWAP with the anchor at the first bar of the week/month (Madhavan explicitly mentions multi-day VWAP for orders worked over several days). These definitions follow directly from the general formula above; I did not fetch a separate primary source for each.
- **"VWAP of the buyers/sellers"** (aggressor-signed VWAP): `VWAP_buy = Σ_{buyer-initiated} P·V / Σ_{buyer-initiated} V` and analogously for sells; requires trade-side classification (exchange aggressor flag in CME data; tick/quote rule such as Lee–Ready in equities). The spread `VWAP_buy − VWAP_sell` is a crude realized-spread/impact proxy. I found no rigorous source testing it as a signal (see Gaps).
- **Tick-vs-bar error**: HLC/3 assumes volume is spread symmetrically within the bar; when volume clusters at bar extremes (breakouts, sweeps) the typical-price VWAP is biased toward the bar midpoint. For 1-minute SPY/QQQ bars the error is likely small relative to spread on most minutes but largest exactly on the high-volume breakout minutes a VWAP strategy trades on. Best practice for a production system: compute VWAP from trades filtered by sale condition (volume-eligible) and keep a separate HLC/3 version only for backtest comparability with published papers.
- **Futures**: there are at least three defensible ES/NQ "daily" VWAPs: (a) Globex-session VWAP reset at 6:00 p.m. ET, (b) RTH VWAP reset at 9:30 a.m. ET (matching the cash-equity session and the SPY/QQQ VWAP), (c) a settlement-anchored VWAP starting from the 15:00 CT settlement. Because the ES settlement itself is a 30-second VWAP, (c) is natural for multi-day anchoring. The overnight session's thin volume means (a) is dominated by RTH volume anyway, so (a) and (b) converge by late morning on normal days but diverge on overnight-news days.
- Practitioners describe AVWAP as the "average cost of everyone who traded since event X," which is literally true only if every share traded since the anchor is still held. It is an accounting identity, not a mechanism; whether that "cost basis" acts as support or resistance is an empirical question no rigorous source I found has tested.

### Gaps
- No primary-source definition was retrieved for Shannon's specific anchor taxonomy (his book was not accessed). The anchors listed above (open, prior high/low, earnings, gaps, swing points) come from StockCharts and TradingView, not from the book.
- I found no quantitative study of HLC/3-bar VWAP vs. tick VWAP error for SPY/QQQ/ES.
- No formal source was retrieved for the conventional ES "RTH" definition used by charting vendors (8:30–15:00 CT vs. 8:30–15:15 CT); the 15:15 CT close relates to the pre-2021 settlement window per the 2018 CFTC filing, but current vendor defaults were not verified.

## 2. VWAP as an execution benchmark and the intraday flows it creates

### Takeaway
VWAP became the dominant execution benchmark because it is cheap to compute, hard to beat by accident, and (Berkowitz-Logue-Noser) an unbiased proxy for the price a non-strategic trader faces. But it is gameable, it encourages passive volume-proportional slicing, and it leaves predictable footprints: 1-minute VWAP/TWAP slicing is visible in ES second-by-second volume, and theory (Choi-Larsen-Seppi) and evidence (Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka) both point to predictable intraday price-pressure patterns. That is the economic basis for any VWAP trading signal.

### Cited Findings
- Berkowitz, Logue & Noser (1988), "The Total Cost of Transactions on the NYSE," provide the theoretical justification for the VWAP benchmark as an unbiased estimate of the prices facing a non-strategic trader during the day of the trade. [RIGOROUS] — [JF 43(1):97-112](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1988.tb02591.x); characterization per [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Madhavan (2002, ITG): "The uncritical use of VWAP as a benchmark can promote trading behavior that actually increases costs and risk. VWAP is a reasonable benchmark for smaller trades that lack urgency and when traders do not have discretion over timing or execution." [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Madhavan's three ways to obtain VWAP: (1) **guaranteed principal VWAP bid** (broker guarantees the day's VWAP for a fixed commission and bears the tracking risk; most certainty, but true cost hidden in the principal risk premium); (2) **forward VWAP cross** (buyers and sellers matched at a future VWAP; potentially lowest cost but non-execution and adverse-selection risk); (3) **VWAP trading** via agency or automated participation strategies that trade proportionally to the forecast volume pattern (cancelable, low cost, but VWAP not guaranteed). [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Behavioral effects of the benchmark (Madhavan): "Daily VWAP benchmarks encourage traders to spread their trades out over time to avoid the risk of trading at prices that are at the extreme for the day," which entails delay/opportunity costs; VWAP benchmarks "underestimate costs relative to pre-trade benchmarks when the stock itself is trending"; for large blocks in illiquid names "VWAP essentially reflects the trade itself"; VWAP is especially subject to gaming. [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Madhavan (citing Cushing & Madhavan 2000): prices are more sensitive to order flow at the close, and returns "exhibit significant reversals on days with published imbalances (100 basis points or more)" — liquidity demanders at the close pay a premium. Microsoft share volume by time of day (Apr–Jul 2001) is U-shaped. [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- Konishi (2002), "Optimal slice of a VWAP trade," JFM 5(2):197-221: derives a **static** optimal VWAP execution schedule minimizing expected squared execution error (VWAP tracking error), reducing a multivariable optimization to a single-variable problem; the static approach limits applicability for small orders/low liquidity. [RIGOROUS] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v5y2002i2p197-221.html); [summary](https://alphatrade.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_vwap_2002_slice.html)
- Białkowski, Darolles & Le Fol (2008), JBF 32(9):1709-1722: decompose intraday volume into a market-wide component (common factor) and a stock-specific component modeled with ARMA/SETAR dynamics, reducing VWAP execution risk relative to static historical volume curves. [RIGOROUS] — [Working paper PDF](https://ourarchive.otago.ac.nz/bitstream/handle/10523/1540/ImprovingVWAPStrategiesADynamicalVolumeApproach2006.pdf?sequence=3); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/p/hal/journl/hal-01393121.html)
- Humphery-Jenner (2011), "Optimal VWAP trading under noisy conditions," JBF 35(9):2319-2329: introduces **Dynamic VWAP (DVWAP)**, letting the VWAP schedule respond to intraday news/noise rather than a fixed historical volume curve. [RIGOROUS] — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v35y2011i9p2319-2329.html)
- Later stochastic-control VWAP execution work includes Frei & Westray (2015), Cartea & Jaimungal (2016, closed-form VWAP-targeting strategy) and Barzykin & Lillo (2019, VWAP under transient impact). [RIGOROUS] — [Frei & Westray PDF](https://www.math.ualberta.ca/~cfrei/CFNW2015.pdf); [Cartea & Jaimungal summary](https://alphatrade.readthedocs.io/en/latest/_vwap_2016.html); [Barzykin & Lillo, arXiv 1901.02327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1901.02327)
- **Predictable footprints in ES**: Using ES trades Nov 2010–Nov 2011, the largest within-minute volume concentrations occur in the first few seconds of each minute, especially 02:00–03:00 GMT (European open), 13:00–14:00 GMT (US open) and 20:00–21:00 GMT (US close); "This is the result of TWAP algorithms and VWAP algorithms that trade on 1 minute slots. A mildly sophisticated HFT algorithm will evaluate the order imbalance at the beginning of every minute ... thus front-running VWAPs and TWAPs." Also: ~51.56% of ES trades were for one contract; size-100 trades up to 17× more frequent than 99 or 101 (round-number GUI traders are detectable). [RIGOROUS, JPM 2012] — [Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara, The Volume Clock (PDF)](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)
- Same paper: "A smart VWAP algo would incorporate a feedback mechanism that adjusts the execution horizon in real time"; advice to low-frequency traders includes "Join the herd. Trade with volume bursts, like at the opening and closing." [RIGOROUS] — [The Volume Clock](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)
- **Theory of benchmark-induced price pressure**: Choi, Larsen & Seppi build a continuous-time equilibrium model with dynamic TWAP and VWAP targets; TWAP targets create predictable intraday price-pressure patterns, VWAP targets add randomness to them, and "TWAP and VWAP trading both reduce market liquidity" and raise volatility relative to terminal-only targets. [WORKING PAPER, arXiv 1803.08336 v6, 2020] — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08336)
- **Empirical periodicity**: Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010) find return continuation at half-hour intervals that are exact multiples of a trading day, lasting ~40 trading days; volume, order imbalance, volatility and spreads show similar periodicity but do not explain the return effect; motivated by systematic institutional trading/flows. [RIGOROUS] — [JF 65(4):1369-1407](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x); [arXiv version](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535); [Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns)

### Inferences
- **Why price "respects" VWAP (reversion toward it):** VWAP-benchmarked agency algos are measured on execution vs. VWAP; a buy algo ahead of schedule when price is above VWAP tends to slow down, and one behind schedule when price is below VWAP tends to accelerate (price-sensitive participation is standard "VWAP-or-better" logic). Aggregated across many such algos, this yields demand that increases as price falls below VWAP and decreases above it — a soft mean-reverting force. This is a plausible mechanism consistent with Madhavan's incentive discussion, but I found no paper that measures it directly.
- **Why price trends away from VWAP:** a large one-sided VWAP program spread over the day (Madhavan: benchmarks "encourage traders to spread their trades out") is persistent, predictable order flow; Choi-Larsen-Seppi show such targets create predictable price pressure. When the net imbalance of benchmarked flow is large, price drifts and stays on one side of VWAP — consistent with Zarattini & Aziz's trend result (Section 3) and with the "Join the herd" observation that footprints are least visible at the open and close.
- Net: whether VWAP is support/resistance or a trend filter on a given day depends on the *net size and sign of benchmarked institutional flow relative to liquidity*, which is unobservable directly but correlated with relative volume, gap size and news. This argues for regime-conditioning (e.g., RVOL, opening gap, day type) rather than a single unconditional VWAP rule.
- For ES/NQ, the minute-boundary volume spikes Easley et al. document mean that 1-minute bar boundaries are not neutral sampling points; signals computed at :00 seconds are measured at the moment of maximum algorithmic slicing.

### Gaps
- I did not access Kissell's *The Science of Algorithmic Trading and Portfolio Management*; no figures from it are included.
- Implementation-shortfall and POV algorithm specifics (Perold 1988; Almgren-Chriss) were not researched here beyond Madhavan's comparison of benchmark types; presumably covered in the trading-costs notes.
- No current (2024–2026) data on what share of US institutional equity flow is VWAP-benchmarked; Madhavan notes VWAP benchmarks are "prevalent outside the U.S., especially in Japan and continental Europe," but gives no US share.
- Białkowski et al.'s exact sample (believed to be French CAC40 stocks) and quantitative tracking-error improvements were not verified from the full text.

## 3. Evidence on VWAP as a trading signal

### Takeaway
The best-known public evidence is a working paper by Zarattini & Aziz (2023): a 1-minute "long above VWAP / short below VWAP" rule on QQQ, Jan 2018–Sep 2023, reported Sharpe 2.1, 671% total return, 9.4% max drawdown, but with **zero slippage**, a $0.0005/share commission, ~22,000 trades, no out-of-sample test, and authors with a commercial interest. Independent QuantConnect replications range from roughly similar (Sharpe ~1.75) to "far lower." I found no peer-reviewed paper that tests VWAP-deviation mean reversion; the evidence for "VWAP as signal" is weak relative to the evidence for "VWAP as benchmark."

### Cited Findings
- **Zarattini & Aziz (2023), "Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP): The Holy Grail for Day Trading Systems?"** [WORKING PAPER, SSRN 4631351; co-author Aziz runs a trading-education firm (Bear Bull Traders); Zarattini runs Concretum Group] — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4631351); [full PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Volume-Weighted-Average-Price.pdf); [Concretum summary](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/)
  - Data/rules: 1-minute bars, RTH-only VWAP on HLC/3; at 9:31:00 ET go long if price is above VWAP, short if below; reverse when a 1-minute candle closes on the other side of VWAP; flat overnight; 100% of equity per trade, no leverage (QQQ version). "Apart from the first minute of the trading session ... the strategy always implied an open position."
  - Costs: commission $0.0005/share; "we assumed no slippage in our order fills"; authors say it is not suitable for "multi-million-dollar funds."
  - Pre-strategy diagnostic: ~56% of 1-minute QQQ candles closed above VWAP; summed 1-minute changes after closes above VWAP ≈ +$320/share vs. ≈ −$280/share after closes below VWAP over the sample (i.e., short-horizon continuation on both sides).
  - Results (Jan 2, 2018–Sep 28, 2023; $25k start):

    | Strategy | Total return | Avg yearly | Vol | Sharpe | MDD | # trades |
    |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
    | VWAP trend, QQQ | 671% | 43% | 18% | 2.1 | 9.4% | 21,967 |
    | VWAP trend, TQQQ | 8,242% | 116% | 54% | 1.7 | 36.1% | 22,399 |
    | Buy & hold QQQ | 126% | 15% (17% in Table 5) | 25% | 0.7 | 35.6% | 1 |
    | Buy & hold TQQQ | 192% | 21% | 45% | 0.6 | 81.7% | 1 |
  - Regression on daily returns: annualized alpha 38% (t > 5) for QQQ, 93% (t > 4) for TQQQ; beta not statistically different from zero.
  - Trade stats: hit ratio ~17%, average gain:loss ≈ 5.67 (QQQ); worst trade −1.4%, worst day −5.1% ("likely ... a mean-reverting day with numerous false VWAP breaks"); QQQ version traded 13,094,444 shares, commissions $6,547.
  - "Placebo" comparison on QQQ 1-minute data: SMA9 Sharpe 1.3 (107,067 trades, MDD 41%), SMA20 0.5, SMA100 0.7, SMA200 0.9 vs. VWAP 2.1. The authors attribute VWAP's edge to inclusion of volume and the daily reset.
- **Replications**: a QuantConnect "config-matched" LEAN reproduction reports net profit 592.9%, Sharpe 1.754, drawdown 9.1%; that page says several other public QuantConnect replications (e.g., Seth "Algo_dude" Lingafeldt) "reported far lower numbers and concluded 'there's no holy grail here.'" [PRACTITIONER] — [QuantConnect backtest page](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_b115d0894231d55994b1068202f6c0ae.html); [QuantConnect forum 16706](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/16706)
- **Relative-volume "stocks in play" + ORB** (Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, Feb 2024, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy For The U.S. Equity Market"): universe all US stocks 2016–2023, price > $5, 14-day ADV ≥ 1M shares, 14-day ATR > $0.50; RVOL = first-5-minute volume / average first-5-minute volume over the prior 14 days, require RVOL ≥ 100% and keep the top 20; 5-minute opening-range breakout in the direction of the first 5-minute candle; stop = 10% of ATR; exit at close; risk 1% of capital per trade, 4× leverage cap; commission $0.0035/share. [WORKING PAPER] — [CXO Advisory summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/); [SSRN 4729284](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4729284)
  - Reported performance figures disagree by source: a secondary write-up cites 1,637% total return, Sharpe 2.81, near-zero beta; QuantConnect's recreation reports Sharpe ~2.4. Neither was verified against the paper's own tables. [PRACTITIONER] — [LetsDataScience summary](https://www.letsdatascience.com/news/traders-adopt-orb-strategy-for-stocks-in-play-19394da2); [QuantConnect research 18444](https://www.quantconnect.com/research/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)
- **VWAP mean reversion**: No peer-reviewed test found. A TradingView practitioner post ("Everyone Uses VWAP Wrong," ES) claims many Bonferroni-significant mean-reversion results vs. none for VWAP-crossover rules; methodology unverifiable. [PRACTITIONER] — [TradingView/EdgeTools](https://www.tradingview.com/chart/ES1!/tVJcD92K-Everyone-Uses-VWAP-Wrong/)
- Practitioner claims that ±2σ VWAP-band touches are "statistically overextended" and revert are unsourced. [PRACTITIONER] — [LuxAlgo: VWAP mean-reversion vs trend regimes](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/vwap-mean-reversion-vs-trend-regimes/)

### Inferences
- **The cost assumption is the crux.** QQQ version: ~21,967 trades over ~1,445 trading days ≈ 15 position changes per day; 13.09M shares traded; gross profit ≈ $167.7k. If QQQ's quoted spread is ~$0.01 (typical, not verified here), crossing half the spread on every share traded costs ≈ 13.09M × $0.005 ≈ $65k (~39% of profit); a full cent per share ≈ $131k (~78% of profit). Signal-on-close / fill-at-same-price also ignores latency. The edge is real enough to survive commissions but **fragile to slippage**. Any production version must model fills explicitly (e.g., marketable-limit at next bar open, or the futures equivalent).
- In ES/NQ the per-trade cost structure differs (1-tick spread = 0.25 pt ES ≈ $12.50/contract, plus exchange/clearing fees), and the futures session/VWAP definition matters (Section 1). A port to ES/NQ should test RTH-VWAP vs. Globex-VWAP versions separately.
- The 17% hit ratio / 5.7 gain:loss profile means returns come from a small number of trend days (the authors say "exceptionally effective during periods of extreme fear or exuberance"); 2018–2023 contained COVID and the 2022 bear market, so the sample is favorable to intraday trend rules. An honest test needs 2024–2026 out-of-sample data and a pre-2018 period (e.g., 2010–2017 low-vol regime).
- The SMA "placebo" comparison is suggestive but not a clean control: VWAP resets daily (anchored at open), whereas the SMAs have rolling lookbacks across days. A better control is a daily-reset *equal-weighted* average of HLC/3 (i.e., "TWAP since open") to isolate the volume-weighting contribution from the daily-anchor contribution.
- Taken with Section 2, a coherent model is: VWAP is a reasonable *state variable* (who is "winning" today relative to benchmarked flow), best used as a regime/trend filter combined with volume/RVOL conditioning, rather than as a standalone mean-reversion level.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed, cost-aware, out-of-sample study of VWAP-trend or VWAP-reversion rules on SPY/QQQ/ES was found.
- The original Zarattini–Barbon–Aziz tables (Sharpe, drawdown) were not accessed (CXO paywall); the 2.81 vs. 2.4 Sharpe discrepancy is unresolved.
- No evidence was found on anchored-VWAP (earnings/gap anchors) as a predictive signal.

## 4. Volume profile / Market Profile

### Takeaway
Market Profile (Steidlmayer, CBOT, publicized c. 1985) organizes price-time (TPO) or price-volume distributions into a point of control (POC), a 70% value area (an analogy to ±1σ of a normal distribution) and high/low volume nodes. The concepts are well defined and widely used in futures, but **I found no rigorous, peer-reviewed evidence** that POC/VA/HVN/LVN levels predict returns or act as support/resistance beyond chance.

### Cited Findings
- Market Profile was devised by J. Peter Steidlmayer, a CBOT trader, and "introduced to the public in 1985 as a part of a CBOT product"; "the central seventy percent of trading activity about POC defines the value area," following the normal-distribution analogy. [PRACTITIONER/encyclopedic] — [Wikipedia: Market profile](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile)
- Conflicting dating: one document says CBOT Market Profile was developed in 1982 by Steidlmayer and Mike Boyle. [PRACTITIONER] — [Scribd document](https://www.scribd.com/doc/76073092/MP-2)
- Computation (CQG): POC = the price with the maximum number of TPOs in the period; value area = expand outward from the POC until 70% of TPOs are covered. [PRACTITIONER/vendor] — [CQG: Market Profile Value Areas](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/marketprofilevalueareasmpva.htm)
- Some implementations define the value area as 68–70% of volume around the POC (volume profile rather than TPO profile). [PRACTITIONER] — [TradingView: Market Profile script](https://www.tradingview.com/script/KKSZOsN6-Market-Profile)
- Dalton codified Market Profile trading in *Mind Over Markets* (1990). [PRACTITIONER, secondary attribution] — [TradingView: Market Profile script description](https://www.tradingview.com/script/KKSZOsN6-Market-Profile)
- Volume-by-price (horizontal volume histogram) is presented by StockCharts as a way "to efficiently identify key support and resistance zones." [PRACTITIONER] — [StockCharts ChartSchool: Volume-by-Price](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/volume-by-price.md)
- Practitioner claims: HVNs "often serve as future support or resistance zones"; the value area acts as a reference for the next session; "80% rule" (if price re-enters the prior value area and holds, it traverses it ~80% of the time) — none supported by published statistics in the sources found. [PRACTITIONER] — [LuxAlgo HVN/LVN](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/indicator/pojTuzmM-volume-profile-with-hvn-lvn-detection-low-tf-safe); [NexusFi: 80 percent rule](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/80-percent-rule); [ATAS Market Profile guide](https://atas.net/volume-analysis/basics-of-volume-analysis/how-to-improve-trading-using-the-market-profile/)
- The closest academic work found tests generic technical rules across 23 developed and 18 emerging equity markets (not Market Profile), reporting in-sample outperformance in most markets. [RIGOROUS, but off-target] — [Financial Markets and Portfolio Management (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s11408-023-00433-2)

### Inferences
- **Formulas for implementation**: TPO profile — for each 30-min period p and price bucket k, TPO(k) += 1 if price traded in bucket k during p; POC = argmax_k TPO(k). Volume profile — VP(k) = Σ volume traded at bucket k; POC = argmax_k VP(k). Value area (standard auction-market algorithm): start at POC, repeatedly add the larger of the two adjacent rows above/below (often compared in pairs) until cumulative volume ≥ 70% of total; VAH/VAL are the bounds. HVN/LVN = local maxima/minima of a smoothed VP(k) (e.g., kernel density over price).
- There is a plausible microstructure rationale: high-volume prices are where two-sided liquidity previously cleared, and resting limit orders/inventory cost bases cluster there; LVNs are prices traversed quickly (one-sided). This makes HVN/LVN testable features (distance-to-POC, inside/outside prior VA) for an ML model, but their value must be established in-house with proper controls (random-level placebo, same distance distribution).
- Because the 70% value area is a quantile of an empirical distribution, it is mechanically similar to VWAP ±1σ bands computed over the same window; expect high correlation between VA bounds and VWAP ±1σ on unimodal days and divergence on bimodal (double-distribution) days.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study testing POC/VA/HVN/LVN or the "80% rule" as predictive was found in this search.
- No source retrieved for the "initial balance" definition (conventionally the first hour of RTH, i.e., the first two 30-minute TPO periods) or for Dalton's day-type/profile-shape taxonomy (normal, trend, double-distribution, P/b shapes); these are practitioner conventions from *Mind Over Markets*, which was not accessed.
- Composite (multi-day) profiles: no evidence found on predictive content.

## 5. Classic volume indicators (OBV, A/D, CMF, MFI, VPT, Klinger, EMV, Force Index, volume-weighted MACD, RVOL)

### Takeaway
These indicators are deterministic transforms of OHLCV; their formulas are standard. Rigorous evidence is thin and mostly at **monthly** horizons (e.g., OBV moving-average rules in Neely-Rapach-Tu-Zhou 2014 forecast the equity premium in- and out-of-sample). Intraday evidence for OBV/CMF/MFI etc. is essentially practitioner backtests. The best-supported intraday volume feature is **time-of-day-normalized relative volume (RVOL)**, which is used as a conditioning variable in the (working-paper) "stocks in play" results.

### Cited Findings
**Formulas (standard definitions)**
- **OBV**: `OBV_t = OBV_{t−1} + V_t` if `C_t > C_{t−1}`; `− V_t` if `C_t < C_{t−1}`; unchanged otherwise. [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: OBV](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/on-balance-volume-obv)
- **Accumulation/Distribution Line**: money-flow multiplier `MFM_t = ((C−L) − (H−C))/(H−L)`; money-flow volume `MFV_t = MFM_t·V_t`; `ADL_t = ADL_{t−1} + MFV_t`. [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: A/D Line](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/accumulation-distribution-line)
- **Chaikin Money Flow**: `CMF_n = Σ_n MFV / Σ_n V` (bounded oscillator; n commonly 20–21; some vendors 13). [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: CMF](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/chaikin-money-flow-cmf); [Stock Doctor: CMF](https://help.stockdoctor.com.au/hc/en-us/articles/360000099635-Chaikin-Money-Flow)
- **Chaikin Oscillator**: `EMA_3(ADL) − EMA_10(ADL)`. [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: Chaikin Oscillator](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/chaikin-oscillator)
- **Money Flow Index** ("volume-weighted RSI"): raw money flow `RMF = TP·V`; positive/negative flow summed over 14 periods by sign of ΔTP; `MFR = ΣRMF⁺/ΣRMF⁻`; `MFI = 100 − 100/(1 + MFR)`. [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: MFI](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/money-flow-index-mfi)
- **Price Volume Trend (VPT/PVT)**: `PVT_t = PVT_{t−1} + V_t·(C_t − C_{t−1})/C_{t−1}` (percentage-change-weighted, unlike OBV's sign). [PRACTITIONER] — [TA-Lib: PVT](https://ta-lib.org/functions/pvt.html); [WealthCharts PVT formula](https://www.wealthcharts.com/kb/category/charts/indicator-formulas/Price-Volume-Trend-PVT-Indicator-Formula/)
- **Klinger Volume Oscillator**: trend `T = +1` if `(H+L+C)_t > (H+L+C)_{t−1}` else −1; `dm = H − L`; `cm = cm_{t−1} + dm` if trend unchanged else `dm_{t−1} + dm`; volume force `VF = V·|2(dm/cm − 1)|·T·100`; `KVO = EMA_34(VF) − EMA_55(VF)`, signal = `EMA_13(KVO)`; variants differ on the absolute-value term. [PRACTITIONER] — [TradingView support: Klinger](https://ru.tradingview.com/support/solutions/43000589157/); [LuxAlgo: KVO](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/klinger-volume-oscillator.md)
- **Ease of Movement**: distance moved `= ((H+L)/2 − (H_{t−1}+L_{t−1})/2)`; box ratio `= (V/scale)/(H−L)`; `EMV = distance/box ratio`, smoothed by a 14-period SMA. [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: EMV](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/ease-of-movement-emv)
- **Elder's Force Index**: `FI_t = (C_t − C_{t−1})·V_t`, typically smoothed with a 13-period EMA (2-period for short-term). [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: Force Index](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/force-index)
- **Percentage Volume Oscillator**: `PVO = (EMA_12(V) − EMA_26(V))/EMA_26(V) × 100` (a MACD of volume). [PRACTITIONER] — [ChartSchool: PVO](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-indicators/percentage-volume-oscillator-pvo)
- **Relative volume (time-of-day normalized)**: Zarattini-Barbon-Aziz define RVOL as volume in the first 5 minutes of today divided by the average volume in the same 5-minute window over the prior 14 days. [WORKING PAPER] — [CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)

**Evidence**
- Neely, Rapach, Tu & Zhou (2014): 14 technical indicators, including moving-average comparisons of **OBV** (short vs. long OBV MAs), display "statistically and economically significant in-sample and out-of-sample predictive power" for the **monthly** US equity risk premium, matching or exceeding macro predictors; technicals capture declines near cyclical peaks, macro variables capture rises near troughs. [RIGOROUS] — [Management Science 60(7):1772-1791](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1838); [St. Louis Fed WP 2010-008](https://files.stlouisfed.org/files/htdocs/wp/2010/2010-008.pdf)
- Blume, Easley & O'Hara (1994): in a rational-expectations model, volume conveys information about the *quality/precision* of information that price alone cannot, so traders who condition on volume do better — a theoretical justification for volume-based technical analysis. [RIGOROUS] — [JF 49(1):153-181](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1994.tb04424.x)
- Lee & Swaminathan (2000): past trading volume predicts both the magnitude and persistence of price momentum (monthly horizons; low-volume stocks earn higher future returns; high-volume winners reverse faster). [RIGOROUS] — [JF 55(5):2017-2069](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00280)
- No peer-reviewed study testing OBV or CMF as intraday predictors was found; the closest is a trader-magazine comparison (Katsanos, S&C July 2011) of seven money-flow indicators using two mechanical systems, which reported best performance on small/mid caps and worst on financials. [PRACTITIONER] — [Traders.com: Katsanos, Comparing Seven Money Flow Indicators](https://traders.com/Documentation/FEEDbk_docs/2011/07/Katsanos.html)

### Inferences
- **Volume-weighted MACD** (not separately sourced): replace the EMAs of price in MACD with VWMAs, i.e., `VWMACD = VWMA_12(C) − VWMA_26(C)`, signal = `EMA_9(VWMACD)`. The same caveat applies as for all of these: they are smooth, correlated functions of the same OHLCV inputs. In an ML pipeline they add little beyond (signed) returns, volume z-scores and close-location-in-range `(2C−H−L)/(H−L)`, which is the shared core of A/D, CMF and Klinger.
- For intraday use, **raw volume must be time-of-day normalized** before any of these indicators (Section 7: the U-shape and closing auction dominate raw volume). A useful family: `RVOL_{d,b} = V_{d,b} / mean_{d−N..d−1}(V_{·,b})` per bin b, and cumulative `cumRVOL_{d,t} = Σ_{b≤t} V_{d,b} / mean(Σ_{b≤t} V_{·,b})`; and a volume spike flag `z = (log V_{d,b} − μ_b)/σ_b`.
- OBV-type evidence at monthly horizons (Neely et al.) and Lee-Swaminathan momentum-volume interactions do not transfer automatically to 1–5-minute horizons; treat intraday versions as unvalidated features.
- For ETFs like SPY/QQQ, "money flow" indicators conflate creation/redemption-related arbitrage flow with directional flow; for ES/NQ the CME aggressor flag allows true signed volume (order-flow imbalance), which dominates close-location proxies like A/D (see the order-flow notes).

### Gaps
- No rigorous intraday evidence on OBV, A/D, CMF, MFI, VPT, Klinger, EMV, Force Index or VW-MACD for SPY/QQQ/ES/NQ was found.
- Neely et al.'s individual OBV-rule statistics were not extracted from the full text.

## 6. Volume–return and volume–volatility relationships

### Takeaway
The robust facts: (1) volume and absolute returns/volatility are strongly positively correlated, explained by a common latent information-arrival process (mixture-of-distributions hypothesis: Clark 1973; Tauchen & Pitts 1983; survey by Karpoff 1987); (2) the number of trades, not their size, carries most of the volatility information (Jones, Kaul & Lipson 1994); (3) volume conditions return autocorrelation: high-volume returns reverse when trading is liquidity/hedging-driven and continue when it is information-driven (Campbell, Grossman & Wang 1993; Llorente, Michaely, Saar & Wang 2002); (4) abnormal volume predicts subsequent returns at monthly horizons (Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin 2001). Most evidence is daily-to-monthly; intraday analogs are plausible but less tested.

### Cited Findings
- **Clark (1973)**: price changes follow a subordinated process; conditional on the information flow (proxied by volume) price changes are approximately normal with finite variance, while the unconditional (time-sampled) distribution is leptokurtic. [RIGOROUS] — [Econometrica 41(1):135-155](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913889)
- **Tauchen & Pitts (1983)**: bivariate mixture model in which daily price-change variance and volume are both driven by the number of information arrivals, generating the positive volume–price-variability relation. [RIGOROUS] — [Econometrica 51(2):485-505](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912002)
- **Karpoff (1987)** survey: volume is positively related to the magnitude of price changes and (in equities) to price changes themselves (an asymmetry). [RIGOROUS] — [JFQA 22(1):109-126](https://doi.org/10.2307/2330874)
- **Jones, Kaul & Lipson (1994)**: the number of transactions, rather than trade size or total volume, drives the volume–volatility relation. [RIGOROUS] — [RFS 7(4):631-651](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/7.4.631)
- **Ané & Geman (2000)**: the cumulative number of trades is a better stochastic clock than volume, producing near-normal returns; a comment by Murphy & Izzeldin reports the normality result is not reproducible (Li 2004 and Izzeldin 2005 could not reproduce it) and that higher moments of the latent information flow cannot be accurately recovered. [RIGOROUS, contested] — [JF 55(5):2259-2284](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00286); [Murphy & Izzeldin comment](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/staff/izzeldin/AG.pdf)
- **Admati & Pfleiderer (1988)**: discretionary liquidity traders concentrate trading at the same times, and informed traders follow, producing intraday clustering of volume and price variability (theoretical basis for the U-shape). [RIGOROUS] — [RFS 1(1):3-40](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.1.3)
- **Campbell, Grossman & Wang (1993)**: daily returns accompanied by high volume tend to be followed by stronger reversals (first-order autocorrelation declines with volume), consistent with liquidity providers being compensated for absorbing non-informational selling/buying. [RIGOROUS] — [QJE 108(4):905-939](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118454)
- **Llorente, Michaely, Saar & Wang (2002)**: model with hedging vs. speculative trades — "returns generated by risk-sharing trades tend to reverse themselves while returns generated by speculative trades tend to continue"; if speculative trading is minor, high-volume returns reverse next period; if significant, they are less likely to reverse and can continue. Empirical test on daily NYSE/AMEX stocks: regression `R_{t+1} = C0 + C1·R_t + C2·V_t·R_t + ε` (V = detrended log turnover); the cross-sectional variation in C2 tracks proxies for informed trading (more negative for large, liquid, low-asymmetry stocks; less negative/positive for small, high-spread stocks). [RIGOROUS] — [RFS 15(4):1005-1047](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/15.4.1005); [NBER w8312](https://img.nber.org/papers/w8312); [working paper PDF](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/bitstream/2451/26698/1/FIN-00-032.pdf)
- Replication on Taiwan data is consistent, with cross-sectional variation tied to information asymmetry. [RIGOROUS] — [NCCU thesis record](https://nccur.lib.nccu.edu.tw/handle/140.119/30048)
- **Gervais, Kaniel & Mingelgrin (2001)**, "The High-Volume Return Premium": stocks with unusually high (low) volume over a day or a week tend to appreciate (depreciate) over the following month, attributed to a visibility effect on subsequent demand; Kaniel, Li & Starks report the premium in almost all developed and many emerging markets; an alternative study concludes a volume-shock-induced liquidity change is the only explanation consistent with all findings among four candidates. [RIGOROUS] — [JF 56(3):877-919](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00349); [Wharton working paper](https://rodneywhitecenter.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/9901.pdf)

### Inferences
- For **SPY/QQQ/ES/NQ** (the most liquid, lowest-information-asymmetry instruments in the world), the LMSW/CGW logic predicts that high-volume moves are *more* likely liquidity/hedging-driven and therefore more prone to reversal at daily horizons — the opposite of the "volume confirms the move" rule of thumb. Intraday, however, the Zarattini-Aziz evidence suggests 1-minute continuation relative to VWAP. These are reconcilable: continuation at minutes (order-splitting, benchmarked flow persistence) and reversal at days (liquidity provision compensation). This horizon dependence should be tested explicitly with an LMSW-style regression on intraday bins: `r_{b+1} = c0 + c1·r_b + c2·RVOL_b·r_b`.
- The mixture-of-distributions result implies **volume (or trade count) is a better volatility predictor than a time-of-day dummy alone** — useful for sizing stops/targets in volatility units; Jones-Kaul-Lipson implies trade count may beat share volume for this purpose.
- The high-volume return premium is a monthly cross-sectional effect; it is not a direct intraday signal for index products, but it supports the use of abnormal volume as a "stocks in play" universe filter for large caps.

### Gaps
- No 2015–2026 re-tests of LMSW or CGW on intraday data for SPY/QQQ/ES were found in this search.
- Exact numerical coefficients (e.g., LMSW C2 magnitudes by size decile) were not extracted.

## 7. Intraday volume patterns, closing-auction share and volume forecasting

### Takeaway
Intraday volume is U/J-shaped and increasingly back-loaded into the closing auction. Current figures: the US closing auction is roughly **8–10% of total consolidated equity volume on normal days** (≈9% in 2024 per BMLL; 7.93% in July 2026 per Rosenblatt), around 20% on index-rebalance/expiration days, and over one-third on Russell reconstitution day. For ETFs the auction share is much smaller (~2% on NYSE Arca in 2024). Absolute auction records keep being set in 2026. Forecasting models progressed from historical average curves to CMEM (Brownlees et al. 2011) to pooled ML (out-of-sample R² ≈0.62 vs. 0.27 for CMEM on 15-minute S&P 500 bins in a 2025 study).

### Cited Findings
**Closing auction share and records**
- 2024: about $50bn of notional matched in the closing auction on a typical day, about 9% of total daily volume; on index-rebalance or options-expiration days, about 20% of daily volume; NYSE-listed equities ~9% of volume in the auction vs. ~2% for NYSE Arca ETFs. [DATA, published June 24, 2025] — [BMLL: Into the Close](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)
- Russell reconstitution (June 28, 2024): over 34% of total daily notional executed in the closing auction; Reg NMS volume 65% above the June average; over 220% higher in the final 30 minutes including the auction; NYSE traded $275bn and Nasdaq $103bn in the final moments including the auction. NYSE D-Orders were over 46% of NYSE closing auction executed volume as of Aug 2024. [DATA, 2024] — [BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)
- July 2026: closing auction = **7.93% of total equity volume** (−165 bps m/m), with 17.15% of closing volume executed off-exchange (−34 bps m/m); equity ADV 17.44bn shares; pre- and post-market = 10.84% of total volume (pre-market ADV 1.06bn, post-market 0.83bn); intraday block share 5.80%. [DATA, published Aug 7, 2026] — [Rosenblatt: US Securities Volumes, July 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-july-2026)
- May 2026: 18.10% of closing volume executed off-exchange. [DATA] — [Rosenblatt: US Securities Volumes, May 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-may-2026)
- NYSE Q1 2026: record average 605.5mn shares (>$43bn) per day in the NYSE closing auction; a record 3.57bn shares ($230.5bn) matched on March 20, 2026; ~18mn shares/day (~$530mn) of auction interest went unfilled for lack of contra-side, ~3.3% of executed closing volume (6.3% outside the Russell 1000). [DATA, published April 8, 2026] — [NYSE: Behind the Record Volumes](https://www.nyse.com/research/insights/behind-the-record-volumes-a-hidden-opportunity)
- Nasdaq Closing Cross, June 26, 2026 (Russell reconstitution): record 4,594,880,616 shares, $334.027bn, executed in about 1.6 seconds. [DATA] — [Nasdaq press release](https://ir.nasdaq.com/news-releases/news-release-details/record-trading-volume-nasdaq-closing-cross-during-june-2026)
- Longer-run trend: a JFQA paper on auction price impact reports closing-auction volume peaking at about 10% of ADV by the end of its 2012–2021 sample (per search summary; not verified in full text); a dated Traders Magazine piece reports S&P 500 closing auction volume rising from 4% to over 10% of daily volume over the prior eight years. [RIGOROUS/DATA, older] — [Cambridge JFQA article page](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-financial-and-quantitative-analysis/article/price-impact-in-closing-auctions-opening-auctions-and-continuous-markets-a-benchmark-for-cost-of-trading-on-anomalies/0F72910A79C5B42CF6E85F55164CE846); [Traders Magazine](https://www.tradersmagazine.com/departments/ecns_and_exchanges/closing-volume-discovery/)

**Intraday shape and models**
- Microsoft share volume by time of day (Apr–Jul 2001) is U-shaped; VWAP participation strategies trade to a projected volume pattern so that more is executed when volume is heaviest. [RIGOROUS] — [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- **Brownlees, Cipollini & Gallo (2011), CMEM**: intraday volume = daily component × intraday periodic (time-of-day) component × intraday non-periodic component, specified semiparametrically and estimated by GMM; applied to widely traded index ETFs, it "significantly outperform[s] common forecasting methods" and delivers more precise VWAP-trading predictions; proposes loss functions for volume-proportion forecasts. [RIGOROUS] — [J. Financial Econometrics 9(3):489-518](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbq024); [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/9/3/489/840918)
- Extensions: GAS models for volume shares (Calvori, Cipollini & Gallo) and MIDAS long-run components (Naimoli & Storti). [RIGOROUS/WORKING PAPER] — [DiSIA working paper 2014-01](https://labdisia.disia.unifi.it/wp_disia/2014/wp_disia_2014_01.pdf); [MPRA 93802](https://mpra.ub.uni-muenchen.de/93802/1/MPRA_paper_93802.pdf)
- **Cucuringu, Li & Zhang (2025)**, 469 S&P 500 stocks, Jul–Dec 2017 (LOBSTER), 26 fifteen-minute bins: J/U-shaped profile with a high final bin partly due to the closing auction and late limit-on-close orders; out-of-sample R²: CMEM 0.265 (static 0.240); single-asset XGBoost 0.532; DeepLOB-style CNN-LSTM 0.566; clustered XGBoost 0.600; pooled (universal) XGBoost 0.622 and DeepLOBv 0.624; average VWAP tracking-error reduction vs. benchmark 28.7%; CMEM components are the most important features; pooling across stocks helps. [WORKING PAPER, arXiv 2505.08180] — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08180v1)

### Inferences
- For a VWAP-based intraday algorithm in **single large caps**, roughly a tenth of the day's volume prints in one instant at 16:00; a "full-day" VWAP benchmark therefore includes a heavy closing-print weight that a 9:30–15:59 trading signal cannot trade against. Decide explicitly whether the session VWAP includes the auction (for benchmark comparison) or excludes it (for signal generation). For SPY/QQQ the effect is smaller (ETF auction share ~2% on Arca per BMLL).
- June 2026 closing-auction share can be inferred as ≈9.6% (7.93% + 1.65pp from the July m/m change), consistent with the Russell-reconstitution month lifting the figure.
- Calendar seasonality to model explicitly: monthly options expiration (third Friday), quarterly triple-witching + S&P/Nasdaq-100 rebalances (e.g., March 20, 2026 record), Russell reconstitution (late June), MSCI rebalances (NYSE cites them as major unfilled-liquidity days), and month-end — all shift volume into the close and distort time-of-day RVOL baselines. Build RVOL baselines excluding these days, and model them with event dummies.
- A practical forecasting stack: CMEM-style multiplicative decomposition (daily level × time-of-day profile × intraday AR term) as baseline, then gradient-boosted residual models pooled across the universe (per Cucuringu et al.), with event dummies.

### Gaps
- No 2026 market-wide closing-auction share by listing exchange (NYSE vs. Nasdaq) was found; the 2024 BMLL 9% and Rosenblatt July 2026 7.93% are the most current overall figures. Opening-auction share was not found.
- Month-end volume effects: no specific source retrieved.
- No source quantified SPY/QQQ-specific closing-auction share in 2025–2026.
- Białkowski et al.'s quantitative improvements and any CME futures intraday-volume forecasting literature were not retrieved.

## 8. Volume clocks, volume bars and "volume time"

### Takeaway
Sampling by volume (equal-volume buckets) rather than clock time removes most intraday seasonality, partially restores normality/IID behavior of returns, and handles asynchronous trading (Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara 2012), building on Clark's (1973) subordination idea. The strongest claim, near-perfect normality under a transaction clock (Ané & Geman 2000), is contested, and the related VPIN metric is disputed (Andersen & Bondarenko). Volume time is a sound sampling choice for features and labels, not a source of alpha by itself.

### Cited Findings
- Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara: "working in volume time presents significant statistical advantages. First, this time transformation removes most intra-session seasonal effects; second, it allows a partial recovery of Normality and the IID assumption; third, sampling in a volume-clock metric addresses the problem of random and asynchronous transactions." Example bucket sizes: 200,000 contracts or 20,000 shares; Exhibit 2 compares standardized ES price changes sampled every minute vs. every 1/50 of average daily volume (Jan 2008–Oct 2010), showing the volume-clock distribution is closer to normal. [RIGOROUS, JPM Fall 2012] — [SSRN 2034858](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2034858); [PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)
- Same paper: HFT market makers "must keep her inventory under control in volume time (e.g., by turning her inventory over for every 50,000 contracts exchanged)," and Mandelbrot's objection (local-time forecasts can't be translated back to clock time) loses force when decisions are made in event time. [RIGOROUS] — [PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)
- Intellectual lineage: Mandelbrot & Taylor (1967) proposed transaction time; Clark (1973) proposed a volume-based clock. [RIGOROUS] — [Clark 1973](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913889); discussion in [The Volume Clock](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)
- Ané & Geman (2000): transaction count is a better clock than volume (near-perfect normality); contested by Murphy & Izzeldin (not reproducible). [RIGOROUS, contested] — [JF 2000](https://doi.org/10.1111/0022-1082.00286); [IDEAS](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v55y2000i5p2259-2284.html); [Murphy & Izzeldin](https://www.lancaster.ac.uk/staff/izzeldin/AG.pdf)
- VPIN dispute: Andersen & Bondarenko argue VPIN is mechanically correlated with volume and volatility, its predictive content arising from trading intensity and from classification errors in bulk-volume classification; Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara dispute this. [RIGOROUS, contested] — [Kellogg: VPIN and the Flash Crash](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2014/vpin-and-the-flash-crash/); [Andersen & Bondarenko, CREATES RP 2013-42](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_42.pdf); [ELO comment, SSRN 2062450](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2062450); [Kellogg Insight: The Trouble with VPIN](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/the_trouble_with_vpin)

### Inferences
- **Implementation**: a volume bar closes when cumulative volume ≥ B (e.g., B = ADV/50 for ES per ELO's Exhibit 2; equivalently ~1/50 of the instrument's 20-day RTH ADV for SPY/QQQ). Dollar bars (B in notional) are preferable across price regimes and splits. Because 1/50-ADV bars arrive faster at the open/close and slower midday, features computed on them are naturally seasonality-adjusted — an alternative to explicit time-of-day RVOL normalization.
- For **futures across the Globex/RTH boundary**, volume bars are the cleanest way to treat the overnight session consistently: thin overnight trading produces few bars rather than many near-empty time bars.
- Volume time does not remove the need for transaction-cost modeling and does not make VWAP signals more predictive per se; it improves the statistical properties of the features/labels (closer-to-IID returns, more stable variance), which matters for model estimation and for the validity of significance tests.
- Mixing clocks is a common bug: VWAP itself is computed in clock-time windows (session), whereas the bars are volume-time; ensure the anchor/reset logic is defined on timestamps, not bar counts.

### Gaps
- No independent, recent (post-2015) quantification of normality/autocorrelation improvements of volume bars vs. time bars for SPY/QQQ/ES/NQ was retrieved (López de Prado's *Advances in Financial Machine Learning* (2018) presents such comparisons but was not accessed).

## 9. Pitfalls: venue, off-exchange volume, odd lots, auctions, extended hours, futures roll

### Takeaway
Off-exchange (TRF) volume exceeded half of US consolidated equity volume for full-year 2025 (50.6%, Cboe), and ~59% for the 20 most-active names in July 2026 (Rosenblatt). VWAP and volume features must therefore be computed from consolidated (SIP/direct-feed-aggregated) volume, not a single exchange's prints, and with explicit sale-condition filters for odd lots, auction prints and extended hours. Structural rule changes (round-lot redefinition on Nov 3, 2025; odd-lot quote dissemination slated for May 2026) change what "odd lot" means for high-priced names in your history.

### Cited Findings
- **Off-exchange share**: TRF share of total consolidated volume rose 361 bps to **50.6% in 2025**, the first time off-exchange exceeded half of US equity volume; of TRF volume, 18.7% on ATSs and 81.3% via principal dealers (wholesalers/single-dealer platforms); 2025 ADV 17.6bn shares (+44.6% y/y), $1.1tn notional; pre-market volume 5.91% of TCV; retail-attested volume on Cboe exchanges 3.2% of TCV. [DATA, published Jan 26, 2026] — [Cboe: 2025 U.S. Equities Year in Review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)
- November 2024 was the first month ever with more volume off-exchange than on; off-exchange share stayed above 50% in Dec 2024 and Jan 2025; dark-pool (ATS) share has been range-bound since at least 2019 — growth came from wholesalers and negotiated trades. [DATA] — [Nasdaq: Off-Exchange Trading Increases Across All Types of Stocks](https://www.nasdaq.com/articles/exchange-trading-increases-across-all-types-stocks)
- Bloomberg (Jan 2025) projected a record 51.8% off-exchange share for January 2025 (single-month, projected before month end). [DATA/press] — [Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/private-equity/wall-street-enters-darker-age-with-most-stock-trading-now-hidden)
- July 2026: 59.47% of volume in the top-20 most-active equities executed off-board (+146 bps m/m); off-board volume at the midpoint 24.82%; IEX at an all-time-high 4.43% market share; year-to-date ADV +14.44% vs. the same period of 2025. [DATA] — [Rosenblatt July 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-july-2026)
- **Odd lots**: odd-lot transactions were added to the consolidated tape (2013 CTA change) and "would be included in calculations of daily consolidated volume" but "would not be included in calculations of high and low prices." [REGULATORY] — [SEC 34-70898](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyse/2013/34-70898.pdf); [Federal Register 2013-30041](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2013-12-18/pdf/2013-30041.pdf)
- **Round-lot redefinition** (Reg NMS Rule 600(b)(93)), live **November 3, 2025**: round lot = 100 shares for stocks with average closing price ≤ $250; 40 shares for $250.01–$1,000; 10 shares for $1,000.01–$10,000; 1 share above $10,000; odd-lot information dissemination (best odd-lot orders) had a compliance date of the first business day of May 2026 (secondary sources; verify against SEC adopting release); tick-size/access-fee changes delayed to November 2026. [REGULATORY/secondary] — [NYSE filing 34-104209](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyse/2025/34-104209.pdf); [FlexTrade](https://flextrade.com/?p=16366); [dxFeed: round lot changes](https://dxfeed.com/support-for-round-lot-changes/)
- **Auction prints**: closing auction ≈ 8–10% of daily volume on normal days, ~20% on rebalance/expiry days, >34% on Russell day (Section 7). Cushing & Madhavan: prices more sensitive to order flow at the close; reversals after large published imbalances. [DATA/RIGOROUS] — [BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution); [Madhavan 2002](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)
- **Extended hours**: pre- plus post-market = 10.84% of total volume in July 2026; pre-market 5.91% of TCV in 2025 (up 111% y/y). Published VWAP strategy research (Zarattini & Aziz) excludes pre/post-market from VWAP. [DATA/WORKING PAPER] — [Rosenblatt July 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-july-2026); [Cboe 2025 review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review); [Zarattini & Aziz PDF](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Volume-Weighted-Average-Price.pdf)
- **Bar-data vs. trade-data mismatch**: practitioners report vendor VWAP lying above bar highs because VWAP includes all volume-eligible trades while highs/lows exclude odd lots. [PRACTITIONER] — [Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/historical-vwap-data-very-wrong/14272/2)
- **Futures microstructure**: >50% of ES trades are single-contract (51.56%, Nov 2010–Nov 2011); round-number size clustering reveals GUI traders. [RIGOROUS] — [The Volume Clock](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)

### Inferences
- **Primary-exchange vs. consolidated**: a VWAP computed from one venue's prints (e.g., an exchange-only feed) uses less than half of the volume; for stocks where off-exchange flow is retail/wholesaler-heavy, the venue mix shifts by time of day (retail clusters at the open), so a single-venue VWAP is biased in a time-varying way. Always use consolidated volume for VWAP/RVOL, and treat off-exchange share itself as a feature (high off-exchange share ≈ more retail/uninformed flow).
- **Round-lot change**: since Nov 3, 2025, SPY and QQQ (both priced in the $250–$1,000 tier in 2025–2026) and many mega-caps have a 40-share round lot; any history spanning that date mixes regimes for "odd-lot" sale conditions, and possibly for which trades update high/low/last. Validate how your data vendor applied the change before using tick-derived high/low or trade-size features across that boundary.
- **Auction-print handling**: for signal VWAP, exclude opening/closing cross prints (and their late-reported "official" prints) or at least flag them; for benchmark VWAP, include them per the benchmark's definition. Opening-cross prints can dominate the first VWAP value (StockCharts notes the first VWAP equals the first bar's typical price), making the 9:30–9:35 VWAP noisy; the Zarattini rules wait for the first 1-minute bar to close.
- **Late-reported and out-of-sequence trades** (TRF reports, average-price/VWAP-priced prints, derivatively-priced trades) carry sale conditions that should be excluded from last-sale-style VWAP; VWAP-priced block prints are themselves benchmarked prices and would double-count the benchmark if included.
- **Futures roll**: ES/NQ volume migrates from the front to the next quarterly contract over the roll week before expiration; continuous-contract volume/VWAP should either sum both months or switch at a volume-crossover rule, and prices must be back-adjusted consistently before computing multi-day anchored VWAPs across a roll (an anchored VWAP across a roll on an unadjusted continuous series is contaminated by the roll gap). (Roll-calendar specifics not verified here — see Gaps.)
