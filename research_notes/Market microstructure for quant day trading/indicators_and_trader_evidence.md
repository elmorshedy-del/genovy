# Technical Indicators, Intraday Anomalies, and Documented Day-Trader Performance (evidence as of Oct 2026)

Source-quality labels used throughout: **[PR]** = peer-reviewed journal; **[WP]** = academic working paper (SSRN/NBER/SFI); **[PRAC]** = practitioner / author-affiliated commercial research; **[BLOG]** = independent blog or community replication; **[REG]** = regulator data; **[SEC]** = secondary summary (CXO Advisory, Quantpedia, Alpha Architect, press). **IS** = in-sample, **OOS** = out-of-sample.

---

## 1. Classic indicators (RSI, Stochastics, StochRSI, MACD, Bollinger, ATR, OBV, VWAP): definitions and what rigorous tests find, especially intraday

### Takeaway
Rigorous tests that correct for data snooping and include realistic costs find essentially no robust, exploitable predictive power in standard price-based technical rules on mature US equity indices, daily or intraday (5-minute SPY: 163 rules "significant" at 5% before snooping correction, zero after). Early positive daily-data results (Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron 1992) weakened or vanished in later data, did not persist ex ante, and are wiped out by low transaction costs. Indicators should be treated as nonlinear transformations (features) of past price/volume, not as independent information.

### Cited Findings
**Definitions (for the feature set)**
- Stochastic RSI (Chande & Kroll, 1994, *The New Technical Trader*) applies the stochastic-oscillator formula to RSI values instead of price: raw %K = 100 × (RSI − min RSI over lookback) / (max RSI − min RSI over lookback); %D is a moving average of %K; TA-Lib notes the original unsmoothed version is reproduced by setting the RSI period equal to the %K period; when RSI is flat over the window the ratio is undefined (TA-Lib sets %K = 0) — [TA-Lib STOCHRSI](https://ta-lib.org/functions/stochrsi/); [LuxAlgo StochRSI concept](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/stochastic-rsi.md)
- Attribution of StochRSI to Chande & Kroll's 1994 book, and a claim that they suggested 80/20 overbought/oversold levels (vs. 70/30 for RSI); other sources call 80/20 merely a convention, so the book's exact recommendation is unverified — [Incredible Charts](https://incrediblecharts.com/indicators/stochastic-rsi.php); [CFI StochRSI](https://corporatefinanceinstitute.com/learn/resources/career-map/sell-side/capital-markets/stochastic-rsi-stochrsi)
- (Definitions of RSI, Stochastic %K/%D, MACD, Bollinger Bands, ATR, OBV and VWAP are standard and implemented in TA-Lib; I did not retrieve primary sources for Wilder's 1978 formulas — see Gaps.) — [TA-Lib functions](https://ta-lib.org/functions/stochrsi/)

**Daily-data evidence (foundation of the literature)**
- [PR] Brock, Lakonishok & LeBaron (1992, *Journal of Finance* 47(5)): moving-average and trading-range-break rules on the DJIA 1897–1986, bootstrap inference; buy signals produce higher, less volatile returns than sell signals, and returns after sell signals are negative; results inconsistent with random walk, AR(1), GARCH-M and EGARCH nulls — [IDEAS/RePEc BLL 1992](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html)
- [PR] Later DJIA data: profitability of the BLL rules "recently disappeared" (Ulku 2009), except a 22-day moving-average rule — [IDEAS Ulku 2009](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bor/iserev/v11y2009i41p25-44.html)
- [PR] Sullivan, Timmermann & White (1999, *JF* 54:1647–1691): applied White's Reality Check bootstrap to a large universe of rules on ~100 years of DJIA data to quantify data-snooping bias — [FMG discussion paper version](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf); on the subsequent 10 years (1987–1996) the best rule's mean return (~8.63%/yr before costs) was statistically insignificant [SEC citation of STW] — [AgEcon conference paper citing STW](https://ageconsearch.umn.edu/record/19039/files/cp05pa01.pdf)
- [PR] Bajgrowicz & Scaillet (2012, *JFE* 106:473–491): DJIA 1897–2011, false-discovery-rate (FDR) control for data snooping (more powerful than Reality Check); persistence tests show an investor "would never have been able to select ex ante the future best-performing rules"; "even in-sample, the performance is completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" — [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html); [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)
- [PR] Park & Irwin (2007, *Journal of Economic Surveys* 21(4):786–826) survey: many studies report positive profits but academic skepticism persists "mainly due to data snooping problems" (2004 working-paper version) — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jecsur/v21y2007i4p786-826.html); [2004 WP/conference version](https://farmdoc.illinois.edu/assets/meetings/nccc134/conf_2004/pdf/confp23-04.pdf)
- [WP] WIFO (2007) review: recent studies "all based on daily data – find that the profitability of technical analysis has strongly declined or even ceased to exist in the stock market" — [WIFO WP 2007/323](https://wifo.ac.at/wp-content/uploads/upload-8775/WP_2007_323.pdf)
- [WP] Hsu & Kuan (Reality Check + Hansen SPA): significantly profitable simple rules exist in "young" markets (NASDAQ Composite, Russell 2000) but not in "mature" markets (DJIA, S&P 500) — [IDEAS Hsu & Kuan](https://ideas.repec.org/p/sin/wpaper/04-a003.html)
- [PR] Hsu, Taylor & Wang (2016, *Journal of International Economics*): FX, not equities — 30 developed/emerging currencies, 45 years daily data, 21,000+ rules, stepwise SPA test; finds "substantial predictability and excess profitability," cross-validated OOS — [SUFE newsletter abstract](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/297845); a related Essex record (authorship not confirmed, 113,148 rules) reports virtually no traditional rule significant in 2006–2015, though Bollinger-Band and RSI rules remained robust in FX — [Essex repository](https://repository.essex.ac.uk/16362/)

**Intraday tests**
- [PR] Marshall, Cahan & Cahan (2008, *Journal of Empirical Finance* 15(2):199–210): 7,846 intraday rules from five rule families on 5-minute SPDR (SPY) data, 2002–2003; before data-snooping correction 42 rules significant at 1% and 163 at 5%; after bootstrap snooping correction, none significant [SEC summary] — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i2p199-210.html); [CXO Advisory summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/technical-analysis-anathema-to-the-academic-world)
- [PR] Duvinage, Mazza & Petitjean (2013, *Quantitative Finance* 13(7)): candlestick rules on 5-minute data for the 30 DJIA stocks; ~1/3 of rules beat buy-and-hold at Bonferroni level gross, only a few remain profitable after trading costs, and after SSPA data-snooping correction no single rule beats buy-and-hold net of costs — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v13y2013i7p1059-1070.html)
- [PR] Yamamoto (2012, *Journal of Banking & Finance* 36(11)): intraday technical rules plus order-flow / order-book-imbalance rules on Nikkei 225 stocks; short-term predictability for more than half of stocks, but no strategy (including limit-order strategies driven by book imbalance) beats buy-and-hold — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i11p3033-3047.html)

### Inferences
- Every indicator listed (RSI, Stochastics, StochRSI, MACD, Bollinger %B, ATR, OBV, VWAP distance) is a deterministic function of the same OHLCV history; stacking them adds collinear, re-parameterized views of recent return, range and volume — not new information. In an ML pipeline they should be treated as candidate features and subjected to the same multiple-testing discipline (FDR / Reality Check / SPA) that killed them as standalone rules.
- The pattern across the literature is consistent: gross "significance" appears when thousands of rules are scanned, disappears under snooping corrections, does not persist ex ante, and is smaller than round-trip costs — strongest evidence against standalone indicator rules on SPY/ES-type instruments.
- Where technical rules survive (young/less liquid markets, FX), the edge is plausibly compensation for illiquidity or slow information diffusion rather than chart geometry; US mega-cap ETFs/futures are the least promising venue for pure indicator rules.

### Gaps
- No primary source retrieved for Wilder (1978) RSI/ATR smoothing, Lane's stochastic, Appel's MACD, Bollinger, or Granville's OBV definitions; these are standard but uncited here.
- Did not find a rigorous, snooping-corrected intraday test specifically of RSI/StochRSI/MACD on SPY/ES post-2010; Marshall et al. (2002–2003 sample) is the closest and is old.
- Could not confirm Park & Irwin's commonly quoted tally (e.g., "56 of 95 modern studies positive") from the paper text — treat as unverified.

## 2. Documented intraday anomalies: intraday momentum, open/close patterns, overnight vs intraday, intraday seasonality, short-term reversal, ORB, VWAP, post-news drift, FOMC drift

### Takeaway
The best-documented intraday regularity is **market intraday momentum** (first half-hour / rest-of-day return predicts the last half-hour), peer-reviewed in multiple samples and markets, but with R² of only ~1–2%, a hit rate of ~54%, regime dependence (strong on high-vol/news days, weak otherwise) and one peer-reviewed OOS failure for a variant. **Intraday seasonality** in the cross-section (Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka) and **news drift** (Jiang-Li-Wang) are robust academic findings. ORB and VWAP "strategies" with Sharpe 2+ come from author-affiliated working papers with optimistic cost assumptions; an independent 2026 replication of the QQQ ORB rule finds the gross edge reproduces but nets to zero after spread/slippage. The pre-FOMC drift largely disappeared after ~2015.

### Cited Findings
**Market intraday momentum (SPY)**
- [PR/WP] Gao, Han, Li & Zhou (JFE 2018; WP version Dec 2015): SPY Feb 1993–Dec 2013; first half-hour return (prior close to 10:00) predicts last half-hour return with IS R² = 1.6%; 12th half-hour alone R² = 1.1%; both combined 2.6% — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf); [WUSTL listing of JFE article](https://profiles.wustl.edu/en/publications/market-intraday-momentum/)
- OOS R² = 1.2% (first half-hour), 0.7% (12th half-hour), 1.8% (both); higher (2.08% / 1.19% / 3.18%) if estimation starts after Jan 2004 — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)
- OOS timing strategy (sign of first half-hour; trade only last 30 minutes): 6.67%/yr, SD 6.19%, **Sharpe 1.08** (annualized ×252 despite being in the market 30 minutes/day); always-long last half-hour −1.11%/yr; random timing −0.68%/yr; buy-and-hold 6.04%/yr, Sharpe 0.29; success rate 54.37% vs 50.42% for always-long; strong positive skew (0.90), kurtosis 15.65 — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)
- Costs: using TAQ bid/ask at 3:30pm for entry and the closing auction for exit (commissions ignored), post-decimalization return falls from ~6.9% to 4.46%/yr; SPY proportional spread stabilized ~1.2 bps after 2005; post-2005 return 6.52% after vs 7.96% before costs — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)
- Regime dependence: low first-half-hour volatility tercile R² 0.6% with insignificant coefficient; recession-period Sharpe 2.10 vs expansion 0.66; FOMC-minutes release days R² 11.0% vs 2.5% on other days; MCSI release days 5.5% vs 2.6% — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)
- Effect also present in ten other heavily traded domestic/international ETFs; explanations: infrequent rebalancing (Bogousslavsky 2016) and late-informed trading near close — [SUFE newsletter summary](https://academicnewsletter.sufe.edu.cn/info/356150); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/2014/08/attention-prop-traders-the-first-half-hour-of-trading-predicts-the-last-half-hour/)
- [PR] Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens (2021, *JFE* 142:377–403): 60+ futures (equity, bond, commodity, FX) 1974–2020; return over rest of day positively predicts last 30 minutes, and the effect reverses over following days; mechanism = hedging by option market makers with negative gamma (and leveraged-ETF rebalancing), more negative gamma → stronger momentum — [Notre Dame WP PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf); [Erasmus repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/hedging-demand-and-market-intraday-momentum/); [Alpha Architect summary](https://alphaarchitect.com/hot-topic-does-gamma-hedging-actually-affect-stock-prices/)
- [PR] Li, Sakkas & Urquhart (2022, *Journal of Financial Markets* 57): 16 developed markets, high-frequency data; intraday time-series momentum economically sizable and significant both IS and OOS in most countries; stronger when liquidity low, volatility high, information discrete — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v57y2022ics138641812100001x.html)
- [PR] **Contrary OOS evidence**: Rosa (2022, *Journal of Futures Markets* 42(12):2218–2234): using the overnight return to forecast the last half-hour, predictability "vanishes in the out-of-sample period"; Markov-switching shows two regimes; threshold (signal-strength-filtered) strategies beat always-active ones — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v42y2022i12p2218-2234.html)
- [WP/PRAC] Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon, "Beat the Market: An Effective Intraday Momentum Strategy for SPY" (SFI RP 24-97): enters trend-following positions intraday when price exits a "noise area," dynamic trailing stops; 2007–early 2024 backtest: 1,985% total net-of-cost return, 19.6% annualized, **Sharpe 1.33**; 4th place Quantpedia Awards 2025 — [HSG Alexandria](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/entities/publication/71ef0a23-34e3-4b37-a8d7-d98285579662); [SFI listing](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy); [FSI-HSG award note](https://fsi.unisg.ch/de/newsdetail/news/quantpedia-awards-2025-fsis-barbon-and-team-secure-4th-place-with-intraday-momentum-strategy/); a follow-up (Maróy) reports parameter optimization and alternative exits "significantly improved returns" (an overfitting warning sign, not validation) — [Bear Bull Traders forum](https://forums.bearbulltraders.com/topic/4002-improvements-to-intraday-momentum-strategies-using-parameter-optimization-and-different-exit-strategies/)

**Opening Range Breakout (ORB)**
- [WP/PRAC] Zarattini & Aziz, "Can Day Trading Really Be Profitable?" (SSRN 4416622): 5-minute ORB on QQQ, 2016–2023 — trade in direction of the first 5-minute candle at the open of the second, stop at the first candle's opposite extreme, target 10R or close; reported ~33%/yr alpha net of commissions; using TQQQ 1,484% vs 169% for QQQ — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4416622); [Concretum page](https://concretumgroup.com/can-day-trading-really-be-profitable/); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/day-trading-with-an-opening-range-breakout-strategy)
- [BLOG] Independent replication (MQL5, 2026): paper reports 24% hit rate, +0.13R/trade, ~33%/yr with 4× leverage, commissions but **no spread/slippage and no OOS period**. Replication on NQ/SPX/Dow/DAX/FTSE CFDs, Jan 2015–Jun 2026 (~2,900 sessions each): gross NQ +0.131R (t=2.9, hit 23.2%), SPX +0.119R (t=2.4), DAX +0.116R, Dow +0.048R, FTSE +0.050R; **net** of spread+slippage (NQ 2.5 pts, SPX 0.8 pts, Dow 4.0, DAX 2.5, FTSE 1.5 ≈ 6–12% of risk per trade): NQ +0.002R (t=0.0), SPX −0.081R, Dow −0.081R, DAX −0.038R, FTSE −0.079R; directional edge vs random direction ≈ 0.1R ≈ round-trip cost; 2015–2017 negative in every market; 2021–2026 at most +0.06R net on NQ — [MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [WP/PRAC] Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz, "A Profitable Day Trading Strategy for the U.S. Equity Market" (SFI RP 24-98, Feb 2024): 5-minute ORB restricted to "Stocks in Play" (abnormal relative volume, usually news-driven) among 7,000+ US stocks, 2016–2023; screen: price >$5, ≥1M shares 14-day ADV, ATR >$0.50, top-20 by opening-range relative volume ≥100%; stop at 10% of ATR; top-20 portfolio >1,600% net, **Sharpe 2.81**, 36% annualized alpha vs 198% S&P 500 — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html); [HSG Alexandria](https://www.alexandria.unisg.ch/entities/publication/ab50f49b-4d5c-4aa8-aaa1-af7a4c131008); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)
- [BLOG] QuantConnect community recreation of the Stocks-in-Play ORB: Sharpe 2.4, beta ≈ 0 (1,000 most liquid stocks, >$5, ATR >$0.50, trade top 20) — [QuantConnect forum](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)

**VWAP**
- [WP/PRAC] Zarattini & Aziz, "VWAP: The Holy Grail for Day Trading Systems" (SSRN 4631351): long above VWAP / short below on QQQ, Jan 2, 2018–Sep 28, 2023; $25k → $192,656 (671%) net of commissions; reported max drawdown 9.4%, **Sharpe 2.1** vs QQQ buy-and-hold 126%, 37% max DD, Sharpe 0.7; TQQQ variant $25k → ~$2.09M — [Concretum page](https://concretumgroup.com/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap-the-holy-grail-for-day-trading-systems/); [Bear Bull Traders (Aziz's day-trading education site)](https://bearbulltraders.com/?p=2606339)
- [BLOG] Public QuantConnect replications reported much weaker results ("there's no holy grail here"); one QuantConnect backtest shows 592.88% net profit, Sharpe 1.754 — [QuantConnect backtest 1](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_0cab7e083e99d5a372e02fe882bbf078.html); [QuantConnect backtest 2](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_b115d0894231d55994b1068202f6c0ae.html)
- Note: one secondary summary attributes the identical "9.4% DD / Sharpe 2.1 vs 37% / 0.7" figures to the ORB paper; they match the VWAP paper's 2018–2023 benchmark (QQQ 126%), so the ORB attribution is likely a mix-up — [Retail Traders Repository summary](https://retailtradersrepository.substack.com/p/trading-research-paper-can-day-trading-really-be-profitable)

**Intraday seasonality and short-term reversal (cross-section)**
- [PR] Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010, *JF* 65(4):1369–1407): ~1,700 NYSE stocks, 13 half-hour intervals/day, Jan 2001–Dec 2005; return continuation at half-hour lags that are multiples of one trading day, lasting ~40 trading days (20 in an earlier draft); stronger (not restricted to) first and last half-hours; volume, order imbalance and volatility show similar patterns but don't explain returns; not explained by size, systematic risk, or S&P 500 membership; at shorter lags returns **reverse**, driven by liquidity imbalances lasting < 1 hour and bid-ask bounce; authors suggest using it to time rebalancing — [arXiv/ar5iv version](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535); [Kellogg listing](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/2010/intraday-patterns-in-the-cross-section-of-stock-returns); [Kellogg Insight "Picking Up Pennies"](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/picking_up_pennies_for_profit)

**Overnight vs intraday**
- [WP/SEC] Cliff, Cooper & Gulen: the equity premium is earned essentially entirely overnight (S&P 500, 1993–2003), with daytime returns near zero or negative; holds for stocks, indices, index futures and ETFs, and has continued since — [arXiv 2025 "Does Overnight News Explain Overnight Returns?"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04481); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/calendar-effects/buy-at-the-close-and-sell-at-the-open)
- [PR] Lou, Polk & Skouras (2019, *JFE* 134(1):192–213): across 14 cross-sectional strategies, profits are earned either entirely overnight (momentum, reversal) or entirely intraday (most others), often with opposite signs in the other component; institutions trade intraday against momentum, individuals overnight ("tug of war") — [LSE PDF](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf); [LSE Research Online](https://researchonline.lse.ac.uk/id/eprint/87481)
- [WP] Lou, Polk & Skouras (version June 2026), "The Day Destroys the Night, Night Extends the Day": smoothed past overnight market returns strongly negatively forecast quarterly close-to-close excess market returns — [LSE PDF 2026](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/POLK/research/LPS_ClientelePerspectiveOnEquityPremium_20260603.pdf); [FMG DP961](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2026-07/DP961.pdf)

**Post-news drift**
- [PR] Jiang, Li & Wang (2021, *JFE* 141(2):573–599): decomposing returns into news-driven and non-news components with high-frequency data, prices drift in the direction of the initial news reaction for several days without reversal; a strategy exploiting it earns high abnormal returns and remains profitable after transaction costs; stronger when investors are distracted; non-news returns reverse; strongest for overnight/weekend news and small firms (WP version) — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i2p573-599.html); [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2679614)

**Pre-FOMC drift**
- [PR] Lucca & Moench (original sample 1994–Mar 2011): equities rise almost 50 bps from the open of the day before to the 2:15pm announcement; no similar drift in Treasuries; similar in other developed equity markets. Update Apr 2011–Jun 2018: ~40 bps pre-announcement drift only at press-conference meetings (plus ~30 bps more by end of press conference); none at non-press-conference meetings; authors note early trading may erode it — [NY Fed Liberty Street 2018](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2018/11/the-pre-fomc-announcement-drift-more-recent-evidence.html)
- [WP] Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert: extending to Dec 2019, the drift "essentially disappeared after 2015" for meetings with and without press conferences — [Skidmore PDF](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf); contrasting: BIS WP finds post-2011 the drift shifted to the second day before the meeting and shrank to ~22 bps from ~45 bps — [BIS WP 1079](https://www.bis.org/publ/work1079.pdf)

### Inferences
Evidence scorecard for an SPY/ES/QQQ day-trading algo:

| Pattern | Evidence quality | Horizon | Strength | Survives costs / OOS? |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market intraday momentum (first 30m / rest-of-day → last 30m) | Multiple PR papers, multi-market | 30 min at close | R² 1–2% (up to 11% on FOMC days), hit ~54%, SR ~1 on a 30-min sleeve | Yes in Gao et al. (SPY spread ~1.2 bps); mixed OOS (Rosa 2022 fails); regime-dependent |
| Gamma-linked intraday momentum | PR (JFE 2021) | Last 30 min | Stronger when dealer gamma negative | Conditioning variable rather than standalone |
| Cross-sectional intraday seasonality (HKS) | PR (JF 2010) | Same half-hour on prior days | Small per-interval | Best used for execution timing |
| News drift | PR (JFE 2021) | Hours–days | Large abnormal returns | Authors say yes after costs (single stocks) |
| ORB on index ETF (QQQ/NQ) | Author-affiliated WP + one independent replication | Intraday | ~0.1R gross directional edge | Replication: net ≈ 0 after spread/slippage |
| ORB on "Stocks in Play" | Author-affiliated WP + community recreation | Intraday | SR 2.4–2.8 in backtest | Unverified by peer review; sensitive to fill/slippage on news stocks |
| VWAP cross | Author-affiliated WP; weak replications | Intraday | SR 2.1 claimed | Replications weaker |
| Pre-FOMC drift | PR + updates | 24h before 2pm | ~50 bps originally | Largely gone after 2015 |

- The 1.08 Sharpe for intraday momentum is on a sleeve exposed 30 minutes a day and with tiny per-trade edge (~2.6 bps/day average gross if 6.67%/252); it is highly sensitive to execution (entering at 3:30 quote, exiting at closing auction). Any extra slippage of ~1–2 bps per side materially erodes it.
- The Zarattini/Aziz/Barbon family of papers is the main source of "Sharpe 2+" intraday claims; authors are affiliated with Concretum (a commercial research firm) and Bear Bull Traders (a day-trading education business). Treat as practitioner backtests pending independent net-of-cost replication; the only independent net test found (MQL5) is negative.
- Overnight-vs-intraday evidence implies a flat-at-close day-trading algo structurally forgoes the bulk of the equity premium; intraday long-bias has no unconditional reward.

### Gaps
- Could not retrieve the exact magnitude (bps per half-hour) and cost-adjusted profitability of the Heston-Korajczyk-Sadka seasonality strategy.
- No peer-reviewed, independent net-of-cost replication found for the ORB or VWAP papers; no live track records.
- Did not find a post-2013 extension of Gao et al. on SPY in a peer-reviewed outlet (other than Rosa 2022's variant and the multi-country Li-Sakkas-Urquhart study); whether the effect decayed after publication (2014–2026) is unresolved.
- Did not verify the "noise area" construction or position-sizing rule in "Beat the Market" from the primary text.
- Jiang-Li-Wang sample period and exact cost assumptions not verified.

## 3. Mean reversion vs momentum at intraday horizons for SPY/ES (autocorrelation / variance-ratio evidence at 1-min, 5-min, 30-min)

### Takeaway
At the shortest horizons (seconds to ~15–60 minutes) index and stock returns show mild **negative** autocorrelation (bid-ask bounce, temporary liquidity imbalances), and order-flow predictability has compressed from ~30 minutes (1996) to ~5 minutes (2002) and likely less today; at the 30-minute-to-close horizon there is **positive** (momentum) dependence tied to rebalancing/gamma hedging, which reverses over subsequent days. Serial correlation itself is state-dependent, falling (more negative) when volatility is high (the "LeBaron effect").

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Chordia, Roll & Subrahmanyam (2005, *JFE* 76(2):271–292): NYSE/S&P 500 stocks; convergence to efficiency takes between 5 and 60 minutes; lagged order imbalances predict returns at intervals up to 30 minutes in 1996, up to 10 minutes in 1999, and only up to 5 minutes in 2002 — [CFA Institute digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2005/11/evidence-on-the-speed-of-convergence-to-market-efficiency-digest-summary)
- [PR] Chung & Hrazdil (2012, *Journal of Empirical Finance*): price adjustment to new information occurs within 5–15 minutes on average for large NYSE stocks, ~20 minutes longer for small firms — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v19y2012i5p702-720.html)
- [PR] Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka (2010): short-horizon reversal driven by liquidity imbalances lasting < 1 hour and bid-ask bounce — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1005.3535)
- [PR] Corsi & Renò (2009, PNAS): S&P 500 futures intraday; a strengthened LeBaron effect — serial correlation negatively related to volatility forecasts — [USiena repository](https://usiena-air.unisi.it/handle/11365/10662)
- [WP] Bianco & Renò: Lo-MacKinlay variance-ratio tests on S&P futures transactions (valid for scales < 20 minutes); unexpected (not predictable) volatility drives intraday serial correlation — [arXiv physics/0610023](https://arxiv.org/pdf/physics/0610023); companion variance-ratio study on S&P 500 futures 1993–2007 using 5-minute returns, q = 2–6 — [arXiv 0810.4912](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.4912)
- [WP] Baule, Schlie & Zhou (US stocks): average intraday autocorrelations mostly negative, strongest reversal at ~15-minute horizons; sub-minute returns sometimes show continuation, particularly in the first half-hour — [OptionMetrics research summary](https://optionmetrics.com/?p=2611)
- [WP, not peer-reviewed] Portnaya (arXiv 2026): SPY lag-1 autocorrelation −0.081, argued to be driven by magnitude effects such as bid-ask bounce rather than true directional reversal — [arXiv 2606.29591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.29591)
- [PR] At the 30-minute-to-close horizon: positive dependence (Gao et al. 2018; Baltussen et al. 2021), with reversal over following days (Baltussen et al.) — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf); [Notre Dame WP PDF](https://www3.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)

### Inferences
- Horizon map for SPY/ES: tick–1 min: negative AC largely mechanical (bounce) and not tradable for a spread-paying taker; 5–15 min: mild reversal from liquidity imbalances (a liquidity-provision edge, available mainly to passive/maker execution); 30 min into close: momentum, conditional on volatility, news days and dealer gamma; multi-day: partial reversal of end-of-day moves.
- Because the sign of serial correlation depends on volatility state, a fixed "mean-revert" or "breakout" rule is misspecified; conditioning on realized/implied vol and on dealer gamma is the evidence-based refinement.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed study found that directly reports E-mini S&P 1-minute and 5-minute return autocorrelations / variance ratios for a recent (post-2015) sample; magnitudes at 1-min and 5-min for ES are therefore not quantified here.
- Did not retrieve the numerical variance-ratio estimates from the arXiv S&P futures papers.

## 4. Predictable volatility patterns (clustering, HAR-RV, intraday seasonality) and their use for sizing rather than direction

### Takeaway
Volatility — unlike direction — is strongly and reliably predictable: it clusters, has long memory well captured by the simple HAR-RV model (daily/weekly/monthly RV), and has a pervasive intraday U-shape. The evidence-based use for a day-trading algo is sizing, stop/target scaling, and regime conditioning (intraday momentum and serial-correlation sign both depend on volatility), not directional prediction.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Andersen & Bollerslev (1997, *Journal of Empirical Finance* 4(2–3):115–158): intraday periodicity in return volatility is pervasive in FX and equity markets and must be modeled to recover true intraday volatility dynamics — [Kellogg listing](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1997/intraday-periodicity-and-volatility-persistence-in-financial-markets/); they estimated a U-shaped S&P 500 diurnal volatility pattern from absolute 5-minute returns [SEC citation] — [arXiv 2310.19992](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.19992)
- [PR] Corsi (2009, *Journal of Financial Econometrics* 7(2):174–196), HAR-RV: RV_t = β0 + β1·RV_{t−1} + β2·RV^(5)_{t−1} + β3·RV^(22)_{t−1} + ε; reproduces long memory, fat tails and self-similarity despite simple AR structure; good OOS forecasts and "workhorse" status in the literature — [Oxford Academic](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/7/2/174/856522); [Corsi PDF](https://statmath.wu.ac.at/~hauser/LVs/FinEtricsQF/References/Corsi2009JFinEtrics_LMmodelRealizedVola.pdf); [Macrosynergy HAR overview](https://macrosynergy.com/?p=42413)
- [PR] Moreira & Muir (2017, *JF*; NBER w22208): scaling exposure inversely to recent variance raises Sharpe ratios by 50–100% of the original factor Sharpe ratios across factors; for the market portfolio, alpha 4.9%, appraisal ratio 0.33, ~25% Sharpe increase; works because volatility changes are not offset by proportional changes in expected returns (monthly horizon) — [NBER w22208](https://www.nber.org/papers/22208); [Hedge Fund Alpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/volatility-managed-portfolios/)
- [PR] Intraday momentum is stronger on high-volatility days (Gao et al.: insignificant in low-vol tercile) and when volatility is high/liquidity low internationally (Li, Sakkas & Urquhart) — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf); [IDEAS Li-Sakkas-Urquhart](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v57y2022ics138641812100001x.html)
- [PR] Serial correlation is negatively related to volatility forecasts (LeBaron effect, S&P futures) — [USiena Corsi & Renò](https://usiena-air.unisi.it/handle/11365/10662)
- [WP/PRAC] Practitioner ORB designs size stops in ATR units (Stocks-in-Play ORB: stop = 10% of ATR; universe ATR > $0.50) — [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/intraday-trading-of-overactive-stocks-via-opening-range-breakout/)

### Inferences
- Practical stack: (1) forecast daily RV with HAR-RV (optionally add implied vol); (2) distribute it across the session with an intraday U-shaped seasonal factor (Andersen-Bollerslev style); (3) size positions to a constant risk budget per trade and set stops/targets in volatility units (ATR is a crude version); (4) use the vol state as a gate for regime-dependent signals (enable intraday momentum on high-vol/news days; expect more short-horizon reversal in high vol).
- Moreira-Muir evidence is at monthly rebalancing; its transfer to intraday sizing is plausible but not directly tested in the sources here.

### Gaps
- No source retrieved quantifying OOS gains of vol-targeted sizing specifically for intraday SPY/ES strategies.
- Did not retrieve critiques of volatility-managed portfolios' OOS performance (e.g., later replication studies) — evidence on robustness is therefore one-sided here.

## 5. How day traders actually perform (Taiwan, Brazil, US, Europe CFDs, India, retail options); fraction persistently profitable and what distinguishes them

### Takeaway
Across every large, complete-population dataset, the overwhelming majority of day/retail speculative traders lose money: in Taiwan fewer than 1% are predictably profitable after fees; in Brazilian mini-index futures 97% of persistent day traders lost money and ~1% earned more than the minimum wage; 74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts lose; SEBI finds ~70% of Indian intraday cash traders and ~88–93% of F&O traders lose. Retail options traders lose via wide spreads (avg 12.6%) and overpaying for volatility. The rare persistent winners are identified mainly by past performance, experience, concentration and willingness to short; older US evidence of profitable day traders relies on microstructure edges (stale quotes, inside-the-spread limit orders), not indicators.

### Cited Findings
**Taiwan (complete market data, 1992–2006)**
- [PR] Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean (2014, *Journal of Financial Markets*), "The Cross-Section of Speculator Skill": ranking day traders on year-y returns, the top 500 earn 61.3 bps/day before fees and 37.9 bps after fees in year y+1; bottom-ranked earn −11.5 / −28.9 bps/day; less than 1% of the day-trader population predictably and reliably earns positive abnormal returns net of fees (2011 WP figures: 49.5/28.1 and −17.5/−34.2 bps) — [eScholarship](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7k75v0qx); [2011 WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- Predictors of future profitability: past performance "by a large margin" the best; also experience, past day-trading volume, willingness to short, concentration in a few stocks. Profitable day traders do **not** appear to profit from liquidity provision: day traders' trades are 71–75% aggressive orders vs 67% for all individuals; even the top 500 are 71% aggressive; aggressiveness is at best a weak predictor; profits not concentrated around earnings announcements — interpreted as forecasting short-term moves and trading aggressively — [2011 WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- Aggregate losses of individual investors in Taiwan exceed 2% of GDP (Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean 2009, 1995–1999 data); in Taiwan 17% of volume is individuals' round-trip day trades — [2011 WP PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- [WP] Barber, Lee, Liu, Odean & Zhang, "Do Day Traders Rationally Learn About Their Ability?" (Oct 2017 version): >75% of day traders quit within two years; survival at 1/2/3 years 44%/24%/15%; unprofitable traders more likely to quit, but experienced unprofitable traders (50+ days) still have a 95.3% probability of day trading again in the next 12 months vs 96.4% for profitable ones; the fraction of experienced traders with positive lifetime net intraday profits is consistently ~5% (1995–2006); unprofitable traders generate 72% of day-trading volume overall and ~80% in later years; predictably profitable traders are < 3% of day traders on an average day and generate 9.81% of day-trading volume — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- Taiwan cost structure in those studies: commission capped at 0.1425% (discounts up to 50%), transaction tax on sales 0.3%; worked example shows ~40 bps round-trip (10 bps commissions + 30 bps tax) — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)

**Brazil (mini-Ibovespa futures)**
- [WP] Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti, "Day Trading for a Living?" (SSRN 2019/2020; FGV TD 525): all individuals who began day trading mini-index futures 2013–2015 (19,646), 1,551 persisted > 300 days; 97% of persisters lost money; only 17 (1.1%) earned more than the Brazilian minimum wage; 8 (0.5%; 0.4% in another version) more than a bank teller's starting salary; top earner averaged ~US$310/day with SD US$2,560; no evidence of learning — [SSRN](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101); [FGV repository](https://repositorio.fgv.br/items/e87d04fc-4b4c-4a56-ab5a-1c84c1afde1b/full); [CXO caveats (sample period, generalizability)](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/investing-expertise/day-trading-a-bust/)

**United States**
- [PR] Barber & Odean (2000, *JF*), "Trading Is Hazardous to Your Wealth": 66,465 discount-broker households, 1991–1996; most active traders earned 11.4%/yr vs market 17.9%; average household turnover ~75%/yr; overconfidence explanation — [Hedge Fund Alpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/common-stock-investment-performance-individual-investors/); [CXO summary](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/the-lure-of-trading/)
- [PR/WP] Garvey & Murphy: 15 proprietary day traders (96,000 trades, 3 months in 2000) profited primarily by placing limit orders on ECNs inside NASDAQ dealer quotes; follow-up on 1,386 day traders found about half profitable after commissions; profitable and unprofitable traders looked similar on timing, stocks and location; caveat — round-trip-only analysis biases performance upward — [Oxford "Active Traders" PDF](https://www.nuff.ox.ac.uk/Users/MurphyA/Active%20Traders.pdf); [IDEAS JEF 2005](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v12y2005i5p629-649.html); [Barber et al. lit review in WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)
- [PR] Harris & Schultz (1998, *JFE* 50(1)): "SOES bandits" profited by trading before NASDAQ market makers updated stale quotes; ~20,000 trades over three weeks, lose almost as often as they win but small average profit per trade — [IDEAS listing](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v50y1998i1p39-62.html); [GAO 1998 report](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GAOREPORTS-GGD-98-194/html/GAOREPORTS-GGD-98-194.htm)
- [WP 2020–2021 versions retrieved; journal venue not verified in this pass] Barber, Huang, Odean & Schwarz, "Attention-Induced Trading and Returns: Evidence from Robinhood Users," Robinhood herding: top stocks bought by Robinhood users each day have average 5-day abnormal returns of −3% (−6% for most extreme herding); ~−5% over the next month (−9% extreme); more pronounced post-COVID — [Paper PDF (IIMB copy)](https://iimb.ac.in/sites/default/files/inline-files/attention-induced-trading.pdf); [UC Davis summary](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)

**Retail options (US)**
- [PR] Bryzgalova, Pavlova & Sikorskaya (2023, *JF* 78(6):3465–3514): retail investors prefer cheaper, short-dated (weekly) options with average bid-ask spread of 12.6% and lose money on average; with PFOF, retail reached >60% of total options market volume; nearly 90% of PFOF comes from three wholesalers; more calls left suboptimally unexercised — [LBS Research Online](https://lbsresearch.london.edu/id/eprint/2827); [NBER WP](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f163907/f163907.pdf)
- [PR] de Silva, Smith & So, "Losing is Optional" (Review of Finance, 2025): retail option buyers bid up options ahead of earnings announcements with high expected volatility; average losses 5–9% around announcements, 10–14% for high-expected-volatility announcements; wide spreads and slow exit after volatility collapses; market makers are the main beneficiaries; ~US$3bn aggregate losses is a secondary-source figure — [Stanford GSB listing](https://www.gsb.stanford.edu/faculty-research/publications/losing-optional-retail-option-trading-expected-announcement); [Quantpedia summary](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/)
- [WP] Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda (2023), "Retail Traders Love 0DTE Options… But Should They?": retail lost >US$70m over ~2 years in 0DTE S&P 500 options; average $184k/day overall, $358k/day after daily expirations began (May 16, 2022); ~75% of retail S&P 500 option trades were 0DTE; transaction costs ~60% of losses; buyers pay the variance risk premium; short positions profitable even after fees — [BNN Bloomberg](https://www.bnnbloomberg.ca/day-traders-lose-358-000-per-day-gambling-on-zero-day-options-1.1910634); [WealthManagement](https://www.wealthmanagement.com/equities/retail-investors-are-in-love-with-same-day-options); [CXO summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)

**Regulators**
- [REG] ESMA (March 2018 product intervention): national regulators' analyses show 74–89% of retail CFD accounts typically lose money, average losses per client €1,600–€29,000; leverage limits 30:1 to 2:1, negative-balance protection, mandatory risk warning — [ESMA press release](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)
- [REG] SEBI (India) F&O study, FY25 (July 2025): >91% of individual equity-derivatives traders lost money; net losses rose ~41% to ~₹1,05,603 crore (vs ₹74,812 crore FY24) after transaction costs; sample = top 13 brokers, ~96 lakh traders — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html); FY22–FY24 study: 93% lost money — [Multibagg summary](https://www.multibagg.ai/market-pulse/articles/sebi-fno-losses-income-data-cmt4ret9aqnj80zql88lhpocl); [Policy Edge](https://www.policyedge.in/p/nearly-9-in-10-individual-fo-traders-lost-money-as-participation-fell-18-sebi-studies)
- [REG/SEC] SEBI FY26 study (reported Sept 2026): 87.7% of F&O traders in SEBI's broker sample incurred net losses in FY2025–26, down from 90.9% — [Finnovate blog (secondary)](https://www.finnovate.in/learn/blog/sebi-fno-trader-losses-fy26-individual-derivatives)
- [REG] SEBI intraday equity cash study (July 2024, FY23): >70% of individual intraday traders lost money; ~7 million traders covered — [Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/markets/news/over-70-intra-day-traders-incur-losses-during-fy23-reveals-sebi-study-124072401110_1.html); [News On AIR](https://www.newsonair.gov.in/sebi-study-over-70-of-intra-day-equity-traders-face-losses-in-2022-23-fiscal)

### Inferences
- Base rate for a new retail day trader: P(predictably profitable after costs) ≈ 1% (Taiwan, Brazil); P(lose money in a given year) ≈ 70–97% depending on instrument and leverage. Leverage and options amplify the loss rate (CFDs, F&O, 0DTE) mainly via costs and volatility-premium payments.
- What separates winners in the data is not indicator use but (a) structural microstructure edges (stale quotes, inside-spread limit orders, rebates) in the US evidence, and (b) persistent forecasting skill concentrated in few stocks, with willingness to short, in Taiwan. A retail-latency algo has no stale-quote edge today; the remaining route is a genuine short-horizon forecast that beats costs, which the Taiwanese top-500 show is possible for <1%.
- Cost is the single largest swing factor: Taiwan round trip ~40 bps; retail options spreads ~12.6%; 0DTE losses ~60% costs. For SPY the spread is ~1 bp, which is why the few surviving SPY patterns (intraday momentum) only survive there.

### Gaps
- No US regulator (SEC/FINRA) dataset on the share of profitable pattern day traders was found; US evidence is broker-sample or prop-firm based and dated (1990s–2000).
- Could not verify the ~US$3bn retail options loss figure in de Silva et al. from the paper itself.
- The SEBI FY26 figure (87.7%) is from a secondary blog; primary SEBI report not retrieved.
- No 2024–2026 peer-reviewed update to the Taiwan or Brazil day-trader studies was found; no newer 0DTE retail P&L study than Beckmeyer et al. (2023) was confirmed.
- ESMA figures date from 2018; I did not retrieve current broker-disclosed loss percentages under the ESMA regime.

## 6. Behavioral and statistical traps (overtrading, disposition effect, illusion of control, small samples, survivorship in strategy claims)

### Takeaway
The literature's most consistent lessons are: high turnover destroys returns; traders persist despite strong negative feedback (biased learning/overconfidence); and published or marketed "strategy" Sharpe ratios are inflated by data snooping, missing spread/slippage, leverage, and favorable sample windows. With intraday R² of 1–2% and hit rates of ~54%, distinguishing skill from luck requires years of data and explicit multiple-testing correction.

### Cited Findings
- [PR] Overtrading: most active households earned 11.4%/yr vs 17.9% market; ~75% annual turnover; attributed to overconfidence (Barber & Odean 2000) — [Hedge Fund Alpha summary](https://hedgefundalpha.com/strategies/common-stock-investment-performance-individual-investors/)
- [WP] Biased learning: Gervais & Odean (2001) model — successful traders over-attribute success to ability and become overconfident; empirically, experienced loss-making Taiwanese day traders continue at nearly the same rate as profitable ones (95.3% vs 96.4%), inconsistent with rational learning — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- [WP] No learning among persistent Brazilian day traders — [SSRN Chague et al.](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)
- [PR] Retail option traders are slow to close positions after volatility collapses post-announcement (a hold-the-loser pattern) — [Quantpedia summary of de Silva et al.](https://quantpedia.com/how-retail-losses-money-in-option-trading/)
- [PR] Attention-driven herding produces negative subsequent abnormal returns (Robinhood) — [UC Davis summary](https://gsm.ucdavis.edu/news/what-effect-brokerage-robinhood-having-stock-market)
- [PR] Data snooping: 163 of 7,846 intraday SPY rules significant at 5% before correction, zero after (Marshall et al. 2008); best rules not selectable ex ante (Bajgrowicz & Scaillet 2012) — [CXO summary](https://cxoadvisory.com/technical-trading/technical-analysis-anathema-to-the-academic-world); [CFA digest](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2013/02/technical-trading-revisited-false-discoveries-persistence-tests-and-transaction-costs-digest)
- [BLOG] Cost omission + leverage + sample choice: the ORB paper's ~33%/yr is attributed by the replicator to zero spread/slippage, 4× leverage on a ~24% hit-rate system, and a 2016–2023 window that skips weaker years (2015–2017 negative in all markets) — [MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)
- [WP] Survivorship/selection in performance measurement: round-trip-only analysis biases day-trader performance upward because winners are more likely to be closed (critique of Garvey & Murphy) — [Barber et al. WP](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf); Brazilian study conditions on 300+ days, and most entrants quit early (Taiwan: >75% within two years) — [Berkeley PDF](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)
- [WP] Parameter-optimization "improvements" to published intraday momentum rules (Maróy) illustrate post-publication overfitting risk — [Bear Bull Traders forum](https://forums.bearbulltraders.com/topic/4002-improvements-to-intraday-momentum-strategies-using-parameter-optimization-and-different-exit-strategies/)
- [PR] Statistical weakness of real signals: intraday momentum's success rate is 54.37% vs 50.42% baseline; R² 1–2% — [Gao et al. WP PDF](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)

### Inferences
- Sample-size arithmetic (derived, not sourced): the t-statistic of a Sharpe ratio is approximately SR × √(years). A true annualized SR of 1.0 needs ~4 years to reach t ≈ 2; SR 0.5 needs ~16 years — before any multiple-testing haircut. A month or two of live P&L says almost nothing about skill.
- Low-hit-rate, high-payoff systems (ORB: ~20–24% winners, 10R targets) have long losing streaks and are very sensitive to per-trade cost measured in R; costs of 6–12% of risk per trade were enough to erase the ORB edge.
- Red flags for any public strategy claim: no OOS period; commissions-only cost model; leveraged-ETF headline returns (TQQQ); author sells education/signals; parameters tuned after publication; Sharpe annualized on a sleeve that is in the market a fraction of the day.
- Algo design implications: pre-register a small hypothesis set; use FDR/SPA-style corrections when searching indicator/feature spaces; cost-model at mid + half-spread + slippage + fees; run walk-forward OOS; cap turnover; size by forecast volatility.

### Gaps
- Did not retrieve primary evidence on the disposition effect among day traders specifically (e.g., Odean 1998, "Are Investors Reluctant to Realize Their Losses?") or on illusion of control in trading; these are flagged but not quantitatively sourced here.
- No systematic study of survivorship bias in commercial/retail "strategy" marketplaces (e.g., backtest-vs-live decay on QuantConnect or signal-selling platforms) was found; the closest general evidence is post-publication anomaly decay literature, not retrieved in this pass.
