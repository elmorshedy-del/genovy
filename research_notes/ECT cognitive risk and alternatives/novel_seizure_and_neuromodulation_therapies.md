# Novel and Alternative Brain-Stimulation Treatments for TRD (Convulsive and Non-Convulsive): Efficacy and Cognitive Profile vs ECT — status as of October 2026

Scope notes for the report writer: these notes cover alternatives to standard ECT, not standard ECT parameter choices and not drugs, ketamine or psychedelics, which other researchers cover. The patient context is a young adult with about 10 years of TRD who has failed all antidepressant classes, is very side-effect prone (psychomotor slowing, brain fog), and had no response to standard rTMS. Each finding is labelled with its evidence type: **[RCT-sham]** sham-controlled RCT, **[RCT-active]** RCT against an active comparator such as ECT, **[OL]** open-label or uncontrolled, **[PR]** company press release or marketing, **[REG]** regulatory or payer document, **[MA]** meta-analysis. The Scite literature tool was unavailable during this research because it requires a paid plan, so peer-reviewed papers were checked through PubMed, PMC, publisher pages and Exa/Tavily extracts instead.

## 1. Magnetic seizure therapy (MST): efficacy vs ECT, cognitive outcomes, status

### Takeaway
MST is the alternative to ECT with the strongest evidence. The CREST-MST trial (Lancet Psychiatry, 2026; n=239, double-blind) showed that MST was non-inferior to right unilateral ultra-brief (RUL-UB) ECT on remission, at the pre-set 15% margin. MST also caused much less autobiographical-memory worsening: 2.7% vs 17.3%. Absolute remission rates in both arms were modest (22.5% vs 27.8%), and pooled data suggest ECT may be slightly stronger on continuous depression scores. MST is still investigational: no country has approved it as of October 2026, so it is available only through trials.

### Cited Findings
- **CREST-MST (primary trial)** [RCT-active, double-blind, non-inferiority]. Blumberger DM, McClintock SM, Thorpe KE, Tamminga CA, Foley K, Husain MM, …, Daskalakis ZJ. "Confirmatory efficacy and safety trial of magnetic seizure therapy versus right unilateral ultra-brief electroconvulsive therapy in depression (CREST–MST): a randomised, double-blind, non-inferiority trial in Canada and the USA." *Lancet Psychiatry* 2026;13(5):376–386. doi:10.1016/S2215-0366(26)00060-X. NCT03191058. Funded by NIMH. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext); [PubMed 41997695](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41997695/); [UTSW Pure record with volume and pages](https://utsouthwestern.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/confirmatory-efficacy-and-safety-trial-of-magnetic-seizure-therap-3/)
  - Design: 3 academic centres (CAMH Toronto, UT Southwestern Dallas, UC San Diego). Adults ≥18 with non-psychotic MDD. Treatment continued until remission or up to 21 sessions. MST used a twin coil in a midline frontal position. Co-primary outcomes were HRSD-24 remission and worsening of autobiographical memory on the AMT, defined as a ≥25% drop (about 1 SD). The non-inferiority margin was 15% absolute. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext)
  - 292 enrolled and 239 randomised (June 2018–March 2024); 3 withdrew before treatment. Mean age about 45 (range 18–78). Enrolment stopped short of the target: 219 had an adequate trial vs the planned 260. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext)
  - Remission in the completer sample: RUL-UB ECT 27.8% vs MST 22.5% (difference 5.3% favouring ECT; non-inferiority p=0.048; CI −4.4 to 14.9). Modified ITT: 25.2% vs 21.4% (p=0.021; CI −5.2 to 12.9). — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext)
  - Response: 52/108 (48%) with ECT vs 52/111 (47%) with MST, non-inferior. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext); the CAMH/UCSD press release likewise reports "nearly half (48 per cent)" responding in both arms — [EurekAlert, April 15 2026](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1124191)
  - Cognition: autobiographical-memory worsening in 17.3% with ECT vs 2.7% with MST (p=0.0003). MST was also better on global cognition, verbal learning, verbal fluency, attention and executive function, and patients reported fewer cognitive complaints. Withdrawals for non-serious adverse events were 12 with ECT vs 3 with MST. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext); a university summary also notes faster reorientation with MST — [UTHealth McGovern blog](https://med.uth.edu/psychiatry/2026/04/30/magnetic-seizure-therapy-vs-ect-a-new-era-in-convulsive-treatments-for-depression)
  - A published correction (July 7 2026) changed the CI wording to a one-sided 95% / two-sided 90% CI and corrected blinding-index values. It did not change the conclusions. — [Lancet Psychiatry correction](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00206-3/fulltext)
  - Caveats noted by secondary commentators: remission rates in both arms were lower than in older ECT trials, psychotic depression was excluded, and durability of remission and of cognitive protection through maintenance treatment is not yet established. — [UTHealth blog](https://med.uth.edu/psychiatry/2026/04/30/magnetic-seizure-therapy-vs-ect-a-new-era-in-convulsive-treatments-for-depression); [Clinical Trial Vanguard commentary](https://www.clinicaltrialvanguard.com/article/psych-pulse/mst-matches-ect-efficacy-and-leaves-memory-intact-the-crest-mst-trial-just-changed-the-convulsive-therapy-conversation)
- **Deng et al. 2024 (JAMA Psychiatry)** [RCT-active, double-blind; 3 sites, 2007–2012]. Deng ZD, Luber B, McClintock SM, Weiner RD, Husain MM, Lisanby SH, et al. "Clinical Outcomes of Magnetic Seizure Therapy vs Electroconvulsive Therapy for Major Depressive Episode: A Randomized Clinical Trial." *JAMA Psychiatry* 2024 (online Dec 6 2023). n=73 (35 MST, 38 UB-RUL ECT). ITT response 51.4% vs 42.1%; ITT remission 37.1% vs 26.3%. Among completers, response 58.6% vs 62.5% and remission 44.8% vs 41.7%; no significant differences. MST needed more sessions to remit (mean 9.0 vs 6.7; p=0.003), and ECT reached remission faster. Benefit was sustained over 6 months of follow-up in both groups. ECT had longer time to orientation and worse subjective cognitive and physical adverse effects. — [JAMA Psychiatry](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2812444); [Ovid abstract](https://www.ovid.com/01714644-202403000-00007)
- **Meta-analyses** [MA]:
  - Chen M, Yang X, Liu C, et al. *Transl Psychiatry* 2021 (10 studies, n=285). No difference in antidepressant effect (SMD −0.13 [−0.78; 0.52]). MST had shorter reorientation time (MD −14.67 min [−27.96; −1.41]) and shorter recovery time (MD −5.67 min), and less impairment on immediate and delayed word recall, visuospatial memory and verbal fluency. — [Nature/Transl Psychiatry PDF](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-021-01560-y.pdf); [Exa record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/ndk1qwx92vv)
  - Systematic review of 4 RCTs (n=86, search to July 2021), J Pers Med 2023;13(3):449. Response RR 1.36 (0.78–2.36), remission RR 1.17 (0.61–2.23), no significant differences; cognitive findings were mixed. — [MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2075-4426/13/3/449)
  - Can J Psychiatry 2024–25 (24 studies, n=377, all indications). Across 3 RCTs, no significant difference between MST and ECT (g=0.207 toward ECT, p=0.232). The authors describe MST's cognitive side effects as "modest". — [Can J Psychiatry](https://sage.cnpereading.com/doi/10.1177/07067437241301005)
  - Frontiers in Psychiatry, June 2026 (13 studies, n=607, including Chinese databases). Response RR 1.10 (0.94–1.27) and remission RR 1.04 (0.72–1.50), both comparable. MST was better on cognition (SMD 1.19), reorientation (MD −16.72 min) and overall adverse events (OR 0.23). — [Frontiers 2026a](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1873016/full)
  - Frontiers in Psychiatry, July 2026 (6 RCTs, n=429, search to March 2026). Post-treatment depression scores were higher (worse) with MST (SMD 0.329, 95% CI 0.119–0.539), meaning MST was slightly less effective. MST was better on verbal fluency (SMD 0.75), immediate memory (0.68), delayed memory (0.61) and verbal learning (0.57). — [Frontiers 2026b](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1886955/full)
  - Mutz et al., BMJ 2019 network meta-analysis (113 trials, n=6,750). MST vs sham response OR 5.55 (1.06–28.99), compared with bitemporal ECT 8.91, high-dose RUL ECT 7.27, bilateral rTMS 4.92, iTBS 3.20, HF-left rTMS 3.17 and tDCS 2.65. The authors called for sham-controlled MST trials. — [PMC6435996](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435996/)
- **Regulatory status** [PR]: "MagVenture Magnetic Seizure Therapy (MST) Systems are not approved or cleared for clinical use in any market worldwide. CAUTION, INVESTIGATIONAL DEVICE." MagVenture says it is "actively pursuing regulatory clearance of MST in multiple markets" and supporting research in Canada, the US, Brazil, China, Japan and Europe (April 17 2026). — [MagVenture press release](https://magventure.com/us/new-lancet-psychiatry-publication-reports-results-from-large-randomized-trial-of-magnetic-seizure-therapy-in-depression). CREST-MST ran under FDA IDE G170127 and a Health Canada ITA. — [Lancet Psychiatry](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext)
- **Recruiting MST trials (ClinicalTrials.gov, October 2026)**:
  - NCT04080778, UTHealth Houston (PI João de Quevedo). MST vs ECT in bipolar depression and TRD. Recruiting; ages 22–85; TRD defined as ≥2 failed antidepressants plus HDRS >19. Excludes antiepileptics; lithium must be tapered; target n=60; primary completion December 2027. — [CT.gov NCT04080778](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04080778)
  - NCT06409325, Shanghai Mental Health Center. MST vs ECT, n=210, recruiting, primary completion December 2026. — [CT.gov NCT06409325](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06409325)
  - NCT07473648 and NCT07533773, Second Hospital of Anhui Medical University. ECT vs MST mechanistic studies, recruiting. — [CT.gov NCT07473648](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07473648); [CT.gov NCT07533773](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07533773)

### Inferences
- For someone whose main fear is lasting cognitive impairment, MST is the convulsive option with the best-documented cognitive advantage over the most cognitively gentle ECT form, RUL-UB. That advantage is shown on a co-primary, pre-registered outcome. The trade-off is probably slightly lower or slower efficacy: MST needed about 2 more sessions to remit in Deng 2024, and two 2026 meta-analyses favour ECT on continuous scores.
- CREST-MST's absolute remission (about 22–28%) is much lower than the 60–80% often quoted for ECT. The trial population was referred for ECT, often after multiple failures, and excluded psychotic depression. Neither arm should be read as a near-certain route to remission.
- MST is reachable in the US only through trials. The only US-recruiting MST trial found is the single-site UTHealth Houston study.
- Mechanistically, MST is a "focal seizure" treatment and is unrelated to standard rTMS. Failing rTMS should not predict failing MST, though no data address this directly.

### Gaps
- No CREST-MST data found on longer-term (≥6-month) autobiographical memory or relapse, or on young-adult subgroups.
- The "Chen 2017" and "Zhang 2021" meta-analyses named in the brief could not be identified exactly. The nearest found were Chen 2021 (Transl Psychiatry) and the 2023 J Pers Med review; others may exist.
- Exact CREST-MST reorientation times (minutes) were not in the extracted abstract or text.
- No data on MST in patients who failed rTMS specifically.

## 2. Focal and low-amplitude electrical seizure therapies (FEAST, LAP-ST/iLAST, individualized-amplitude ECT) and non-convulsive electrotherapy (NET)

### Takeaway
These approaches all aim to reduce the strength or spread of the electric field while keeping, or deliberately dropping, the seizure. The evidence is small: open-label or pilot RCTs with n≈8–62. Reorientation is consistently faster, but efficacy is unproven, and at least one dose-ranging RCT found that 600 mA amplitude lost efficacy. None is approved as a distinct therapy. They are delivered only in trials, although amplitude reduction within FDA-cleared ECT devices (500–900 mA) is technically feasible.

### Cited Findings
- **FEAST** (focal electrically administered seizure therapy: unidirectional current, asymmetric electrodes, right orbitofrontal/SMA placement). Background on the method — [J ECT 2016 (Sahlem et al.)](https://journals.lww.com/ectjournal/fulltext/2016/09000/expanded_safety_and_efficacy_data_for_a_new_method.11.aspx?Ppt=Article%7Cectjournal%3A2016%3A09000%3A00011%7C10.1097%2Fyct.0000000000000328%7C)
  - Sahlem GL et al., J ECT 2016;32(3):197 [OL, n=20]. 65% response and 55% remission on HRSD-24. Reorientation in 4.4±3.0 minutes. No neuropsychological deterioration. 25% switched to conventional ECT. — [PubMed 27379790](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27379790/)
  - Sahlem GL, McCall WV, …, Sackeim HA. *Brain Stimul* 2020;13(5):1416–1425 [OL, non-randomized, 2 sites, n=39]. Response FEAST 65% vs RUL-UBP 57.9%; remission 35% vs 47.4%. Time to reorientation 6.6 vs 8.8 minutes (d=0.41, not significant). CUAMI-SF consistency 69.2% vs 63.9% (not significant). — [PubMed 32735987](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/32735987/)
  - The same cohort showed equivalent reductions in suicidal ideation — [Youssef et al., J ECT (PMC8606010)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8606010/)
  - Upcoming trial: NCT07857122, "FEAST" multicentre RCT (Centre Hospitalier Sainte-Anne, Paris; Sigma ECT/FEAST device vs RUL vs bilateral ECT). n=108, NOT YET RECRUITING, start November 2 2026. — [CT.gov NCT07857122](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07857122)
- **Low-amplitude seizure therapy (LAP-ST, about 500–600 mA vs the standard 800–900 mA)**:
  - Proof-of-concept [OL, n=22]. All patients had seizures at the first session. Median time to reorientation 4.5 minutes. "Efficacious" for depressive and psychotic symptoms. NCT02928159. — [PubMed 28667887](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28667887/)
  - Pilot double-blind RCT, 0.5 A LAP-ST vs UB-RUL [RCT-active, n=8]. Reorientation 6.8 vs 15.5 minutes. MADRS fell from 41.0 to 8.0 (LAP-ST) vs 39.0 to 9.5 (RUL). Authors: "Current amplitudes below 0.5 A are unlikely to be effective in humans." — [Brain Sci 2020;10(12):979](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/10/12/979)
  - Suicidality pilot RCT (n=7). Suicidal ideation remitted by session 3–4 in both arms. — [Brain Sci 2019;9(5):99](https://www.mdpi.com/2076-3425/9/5/99)
- **Individualized or reduced-amplitude ECT (Abbott CC, UNM; Peterchev/Deng/Lisanby)**:
  - Abbott CC et al., Am J Geriatr Psychiatry 2021;29(2):166–178 [RCT, 600 vs 700 vs 800 mA RUL, ages 50–80, n=62 analysed]. "The lowest amplitude group (600 mA) … showed significantly less antidepressant effect." There was no difference on the primary cognitive outcome (HVLT-R retention), but 800 mA was worse on verbal fluency, colour-word interference and dot counting. — [AJGP abstract](https://www.ajgponline.org/article/S1064-7481(20)30107-X/abstract); the investigators describe this as a "trade-off between cognitive safety (improved with 600 mA arm) and antidepressant response (improved with 700 and 800 mA arms)" — [Mol Psychiatry 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41380-021-01380-y)
  - Amplitude-determined seizure threshold (STa) study, Neuropsychopharmacology 2024 (n=29, ages 50–80). STa and E-field per unit current varied widely between patients. Higher STa (>~330 mA) predicted inadequate response to 800 mA RUL, and higher E-field per mA predicted category-fluency decline. The authors propose individualizing amplitude through STa×~2.5 or E-field modelling. — [Neuropsychopharmacology 2024](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-023-01780-4)
  - Nonhuman primate foundation: amplitude-titrated seizure thresholds were 112–174 mA for ECT, far below the conventional 800–900 mA. — [Peterchev et al., Neuropsychopharmacology 2015](https://www.nature.com/articles/npp2015122)
  - Ongoing: NCT05699226 (UNM, R33). Individualized amplitude (500–900 mA, set by STa/E-field) vs fixed 800 mA RUL in older adults, n=50, RECRUITING, primary completion January 2027. — [CT.gov NCT05699226](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05699226)
  - NCT06529029 (Michigan State). LAP-ST vs UB-RUL for remission of suicidal ideation, n=30, RECRUITING, primary completion October 2026. — [CT.gov NCT06529029](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06529029)
  - The NIMH (Lisanby) first-in-human "iLAST" trial, which planned a multi-electrode array with E-field modelling and amplitude titration, is listed as WITHDRAWN with 0 enrolled. — [CT.gov NCT03895658](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03895658)
- **Non-convulsive electrotherapy (NET: ECT-style bifrontal stimulation at about 1/8 of the expected seizure threshold, no seizure)**:
  - Regenold WT et al., Brain Stimul 2015;8(5):855–861 [OL, n=13; 11 with seizure-free data]. HDRS-17 fell from 20.3 to 8.6. Response 73%, remission 55%. No decline on MMSE or the autobiographical memory inventory. — [PubMed 26187603](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26187603/); [CT.gov NCT01065597](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT01065597)
  - Chinese replication [OL, n=20, 6 sessions]. HAMD-17 fell from 26.2 to 10.4. Response 60%, remission 10%. Wisconsin Card Sorting Test performance improved. — [Springer Medicine summary](https://www.springermedicine.com/antidepressant-drugs/mood-disorders/a-preliminary-study-of-adjunctive-nonconvulsive-electrotherapy-f/21735280)

### Inferences
- Taken together, these data suggest that much of ECT's cognitive burden comes from field strength and spread. They do not yet show that efficacy is preserved. The only properly randomized amplitude dose-ranging trial found an efficacy loss at 600 mA.
- For this patient these are research-only options. FEAST's French RCT and the UNM and MSU amplitude trials are the access routes. NET is intriguing as a seizure-free, anesthesia-based option but has only about 33 open-label patients.

### Gaps
- The "ECT-AIM" trial named in the brief could not be identified by that name. The closest match is UNM's R61/R33 amplitude-titration RCT (NCT05699226). No published results for it were found.
- No RCT of FEAST vs ECT has reported results. The MUSC "FEAST two-site" study (NCT02535572) is listed as completed, and its published output appears to be the non-randomized Sahlem 2020 paper.
- No data on any of these in young adults specifically; most low-amplitude work is in ages 50–80.

## 3. Accelerated, fMRI-connectivity-guided iTBS (SAINT/SNT) and other accelerated or targeted protocols

### Takeaway
SAINT/SNT has two sham-controlled RCTs showing large effects: 1-month remission of 46.2% vs 0% (n=29) and 50.0% vs 20.8% (n=48). It is FDA-cleared (2022), has Medicare hospital-outpatient payment codes (stable for 2026), and has no measurable cognitive harm. **Both RCTs excluded anyone with prior rTMS exposure.** The only data in prior-rTMS non-responders are 6 patients in the open-label 2020 study, 5 of whom (83%) responded. Without maintenance, about one-third of all treated patients (47% of initial remitters) stay in remission at 12 weeks.

### Cited Findings
- **Cole et al. 2020 (open-label SAINT)** [OL]. Cole EJ, Stimpson KH, Bentzley BS, …, Williams NR. *Am J Psychiatry* 2020;177(8):716–726. doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720. Remission (MADRS <11) in 19 of 21 completers (90.5%), or 19 of 22 by ITT (86.4%). "Neuropsychological testing demonstrated no negative cognitive side effects." — [AJP](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720)
  - **Prior rTMS non-responders (n=6)**: they needed more days to respond (mean 3.0 vs fewer; p=0.037). None responded after day 1, but "83% of these prior rTMS nonresponders did respond by the end of the 5-day protocol." The 4-week MADRS difference from other patients was not significant. — [AJP 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720)
  - The same paper cites prior work showing that 61% of rTMS non-responders responded with additional sessions. — [AJP 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720); that figure comes from Yip et al., Brain Stimul 2017 (continued twice-weekly deep TMS in non-responders), cited in [Hanlon et al. 2026](https://exa.ai/library/publication/t5zbc1xlt8n)
- **Cole et al. 2022 SNT RCT** [RCT-sham, double-blind, n=29: 14 active, 15 sham]. *Am J Psychiatry* 2022;179(2):132–141 (online October 2021). doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.20101429. Participants averaged 5 prior antidepressant trials. **Exclusions: "prior exposure to rTMS, nonresponse to ECT."** Remission at the immediate, 1-week and 4-week assessments: 57.1% vs 0%, 66.7% vs 10.0%, and 46.2% vs 0%. Any remission within 4 weeks: 78.6% vs 13.3%; any response: 85.7% vs 26.7%. Cohen's d 1.4–1.8. — [AJP 2022](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.20101429)
- **Kratter et al. 2026 replication RCT** [RCT-sham, double-blind, n=48: 24/24]. "Stanford neuromodulation therapy for treatment-resistant depression: a randomized controlled trial confirming efficacy…" *World Psychiatry* 2026;25(1):105–116. doi:10.1002/wps.70032. Remission at 1 month (primary): 50.0% vs 20.8% (p=0.035, NNT 3.4). Response: 54.2% vs 25.0% (p=0.039). Again excluded "prior exposure to rTMS, non-response to ECT", plus depth-adjusted dose >65% of maximum output. Baseline left-ACC beta power on EEG predicted benefit. — [PMC12805067](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805067/); [PubMed 41536095](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41536095)

  - Retreatment after relapse gives reproducible benefit: Geoly et al., *Am J Psychiatry* 2024;181(1):71–73 — cited in [Feyder et al. 2026](https://exa.ai/library/publication/280qhdw699h)

Magnus describes SAINT as the first mental-health therapy to receive NTAP and New Technology APC [PR] — [Magnus PR](https://www.magnusmedical.com/press-release/2026-payment-stability)
- **Other accelerated, non-fMRI protocols**:
  - Pragmatic accelerated TBS RCT, São Paulo [RCT-sham, triple-blind, n=100]. 3 iTBS sessions a day for 15 weekdays (45 sessions), craniometric targeting with no MRI. HDRS-17 reduction 54.7% active vs 31.87% sham (d=0.65, p<0.001). Scalp pain 17.4% vs 4.4%. — [PubMed 40042840](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40042840/)
  - Accelerated iTBS with the H1 (deep) coil vs standard H1 [RCT-active non-inferiority, n=104; BrainsWay-affiliated authors incl. Hanlon and Roth]. Non-inferior at week 6. Response/remission 87.8%/78.0% vs 87.5%/87.5%. Time to remission 21 vs 28 days. Excluded no prior-TMS group by design; patients had 1–4 failed antidepressants. — [Hanlon et al. 2026](https://exa.ai/library/publication/t5zbc1xlt8n)
  - FDA clearances of accelerated protocols [PR]: BrainsWay accelerated Deep TMS (6-day acute phase), September 16 2025 — [BrainsWay](https://www.brainsway.com/news_events/brainsway-receives-fda-clearance-for-accelerated-deep-tms-protocol-for-non-invasive-treatment-of-major-depressive-disorder-mdd); MagVenture accelerated rTMS and iTBS protocols, May 26 2026 — [MagVenture](https://magventure.com/us/magventure-receives-fda-clearance-for-accelerated-tms)
- **fMRI connectivity targeting alone did not beat standard targeting**: BRIGhTMIND [RCT-active, 5 centres, n=255; Morriss et al., *Nat Med* 2024]. Connectivity-guided iTBS (insula→DLPFC) vs structural-MRI F3 rTMS, 20 sessions each. No difference (adjusted mean −0.31, p=0.689). At 26 weeks, 32.5% responders, 22.8% remitters and 20% sustained responders. — [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-023-02764-z)
- **Recruiting or upcoming accelerated-TMS trials**:
  - NCT07329153 (UT Southwestern, UCSD and Cornell): neuronavigated vs non-navigated aiTBS vs sham, n=247, not yet recruiting (start July 2026). **Excludes ≥15 rTMS/iTBS sessions in the current episode.** — [CT.gov NCT07329153](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07329153)
  - NCT05377177 (UCSD COMBAT-SI): bilateral vs unilateral accelerated TBS for suicidal ideation in TRD, recruiting. — [CT.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05377177)
  - NCT07758010 (McLean OcTOPUS): one-day precuneus cTBS for TRD, recruiting from September 2026. — [CT.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07758010)
  - NCT04982757 (Weill Cornell): accelerated TMS for depression/OCD, enrolling by invitation, n=500. — [CT.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04982757)

### Inferences
- SAINT is probably the highest-yield next step that is non-convulsive, needs no anesthesia, and has no cognitive downside. However, its RCT effect sizes come from rTMS-naïve patients. In this patient the response probability is uncertain and probably lower than the headline 79%. The only direct signal (5 of 6 prior rTMS non-responders responding, open-label, small) is encouraging but weak.
- The SAINT hypothesis is that standard rTMS under-doses. It delivers about 90,000 pulses in 5 days vs 18,000 for a standard iTBS course over 6 weeks ([Cole 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720)). That gives a mechanistic reason why rTMS failure might not predict SAINT failure, but this has not been tested in an RCT.
- Relapse within about 3 months is common, so a plan for retreatment or maintenance should be part of any SAINT decision.
- A practical trial-access point: several new academic aiTBS RCTs exclude people with substantial prior rTMS in the current episode.

### Gaps
- No RCT or large cohort of SAINT/SNT in documented prior-rTMS non-responders. No published per-subgroup real-world outcomes for prior-TMS failures. Magnus has not publicly reported a "prior TMS failure" subgroup that I could find.
- Independent replication (outside Stanford/Magnus) of SNT's sham-controlled efficacy has not been published. The UTSW/UCSD/Cornell trial will test generic aiTBS but has not started reporting.
- Detailed neuropsychological data beyond "no negative cognitive side effects" (Cole 2020) were not retrieved.

## 4. Deep TMS (H1), bilateral TMS and theta-burst: evidence in patients who failed prior rTMS

### Takeaway
Very little controlled evidence addresses re-treating rTMS failures with a different TMS variant. Large meta-analyses show iTBS is equivalent to standard rTMS. Bilateral and deep TMS have similar or modestly higher pooled effects than left-HF rTMS. Response to rTMS falls as medication failures accumulate. Extending the course and increasing the dose (more sessions, accelerated schedules) has the best support for initial non-responders.

### Cited Findings
- **Deep TMS (H1)**: the pivotal sham-controlled trial (Levkovitz 2015) reported 32.6% HDRS remission at 5 weeks — [Hanlon 2026 citing the pivotal trial](https://exa.ai/library/publication/t5zbc1xlt8n). Response 38.4% vs 21.4% and remission 32.6% vs 14.6% vs sham [secondary source: clinic blog] — [Nushama](https://nushama.com/blog/saint-protocol-tms-evidence-ranked-severe-trd)
- Post-marketing deep TMS registry [OL, 21 sites, 1,351 analysed; BrainsWay-associated authors; mixed rating scales]. After 30 sessions, 81.6% response and 65.3% remission; deep-TMS iTBS 72.4%/69.2%. "Initial non-responders and non-remitters benefit from extended treatment." — [Tendler et al., Psychiatry Res 2023](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165178123001300)
- In the BrainsWay pivotal trial, "61% of unmedicated treatment resistant depression patients who did not respond to acute TMS treatment responded after four weeks of twice weekly deep TMS" (Yip et al., Brain Stimul 2017;10(4):847–849). This was continuation within the same course, not retreatment after a failed course. — cited in [Hanlon 2026](https://exa.ai/library/publication/t5zbc1xlt8n)
- **Bilateral DMPFC iTBS in patients who failed both antidepressants and a DLPFC rTMS/piTBS course** [RCT-sham, n=34, three arms]. No difference in overall HDRS-17 change among standard iTBS, prolonged iTBS and sham. Prolonged iTBS was better on a secondary depression scale (DSSS) and on anxiosomatic symptoms. — [Cheng et al., Eur Arch Psychiatry Clin Neurosci 2023;273:1433–1442](https://link.springer.com/content/pdf/10.1007/s00406-022-01523-4.pdf)
- **Treatment-resistance burden predicts poorer rTMS response**:
  - Registry analysis: mean 8 prior lifetime antidepressant trials; 37% responded or remitted. Fewer prior antidepressants predicted greater PHQ-9 improvement. Coil type (H1 vs figure-8) and protocol did not predict response. — [Chaikind et al. 2025](https://exa.ai/library/publication/l8rzlft3kxv)
  - FOUR-D secondary analysis (late-life): remission 43.9% with ≤1 failed medication trial vs 26.5% with 2 and 24.6% with ≥3. Bilateral TBS and bilateral rTMS did not differ. — [PMC10895496](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10895496/)
  - The SNT RCT authors note that conventional rTMS produces remission in about 17% of patients who failed 3 antidepressants. — [Cole 2022 AJP](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.20101429)
- TBS vs standard rTMS meta-analysis: response RR 1.02 (0.85–1.23), i.e. equivalent. — [Voigt et al., Transl Psychiatry 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-021-01441-4.pdf)
- Network meta-analysis response ORs vs sham: priming TMS 6.02, bilateral rTMS 4.92, bilateral TBS 4.44, LF-right rTMS 3.65, iTBS 3.20, HF-left rTMS 3.17. — [Mutz et al., BMJ 2019 (PMC6435996)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435996/)

### Inferences
- Simply switching coil type (figure-8 to H1) or pattern (10 Hz to iTBS) is unlikely to change the outcome for a complete rTMS non-responder, because pooled efficacy is similar. A change in total dose and density (accelerated, SAINT-like) or in target (fMRI-individualized, DMPFC, right-sided or bilateral) is the more plausible lever, but no RCT supports this in prior non-responders.
- Factors that may have undermined the first course are worth checking with the treating team (for example targeting method, intensity, number of sessions, concurrent benzodiazepines). The data above show extending sessions rescues a large share of early non-responders.

### Gaps
- No RCT was found that randomizes documented full-course rTMS non-responders to deep TMS, bilateral TMS or accelerated TMS vs sham.
- The primary Levkovitz 2015 paper was not retrieved directly; its response figures come from a secondary clinic page.

## 5. Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS)

### Takeaway
VNS is an FDA-approved (2005) implant for chronic TRD with ≥4 failed treatments. Its effect is slow and cumulative. The RECOVER RCT (n=493, extremely refractory patients) missed its primary MADRS endpoint, but secondary CGI-I, QIDS-SR and QIDS-C measures favoured active stimulation. A 5-year non-randomized registry showed cumulative response of 67.6% vs 40.9%. VNS has no known cognitive harm, and small cohorts suggest it improves cognition. Medicare still covers it only under Coverage with Evidence Development; LivaNova's June 2025 reconsideration request had produced no new national coverage decision as of 2026.

### Cited Findings
- **RECOVER unipolar primary paper** [RCT-sham, double-blind, 12 months, 84 sites]. Conway CR, Aaronson ST, Sackeim HA, George MS, …, Rush AJ. "Vagus nerve stimulation in treatment-resistant depression: A one-year, randomized, sham-controlled trial." *Brain Stimul* 2025;18(3):676–689. doi:10.1016/j.brs.2024.12.1191. NCT03887715. Sponsored by LivaNova, designed with CMS.
  - n=493 randomized; 88.4% completed. Mean 13.3 lifetime failed treatments and 29.4 years depressed; 70.6% had prior interventional treatments.
  - Primary outcome, mean percent time in MADRS response over months 3–12: 18.9% vs 16.3% (p=0.137, not significant).
  - Secondary outcomes: CGI-I time in response 26.7% vs 18.2% (p=0.004); QIDS-SR 25.2% vs 19.8% (p=0.049); CGI-I partial response 53.8% vs 39.8% (p<0.001); QIDS-C partial response 39.6% vs 30.7% (p=0.006).
  - Dyspnea was more common with active stimulation (p=0.035). No new adverse events. Suicidal ideation worsened in 10.8% (active) vs 15.3% (sham).
  - Sources: [WashU Digital Commons full text](https://digitalcommons.wustl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7421&context=oa_4); [Brain Stim symposium abstract](https://www.brainstimjrnl.com/article/S1935-861X(24)00270-5/fulltext)
- RECOVER quality of life and function: modest gains on Mini-Q-LES-Q and WPAI item 6 (p=0.05); no difference on WHODAS or EQ-5D. — [Exa abstract record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/4lmqk117dk2). Payer critique: secondary benefits were "inconsistent across outcome instruments and of uncertain clinical magnitude"; the 18–24-month durability reports (Conway et al., Int J Neuropsychopharmacol 2026;29(1):pyaf080) come from open-label phases. — [Anthem CG-SURG-120](https://www.anthem.com/medpolicies/abc/active/gl_pw_e002906.html)
- A suicidal-ideation secondary analysis found active VNS reduced suicidal ideation vs sham in patients with baseline ideation. — [Hristidis et al., J Clin Psychiatry 2026;87(3):25m16296](https://www.psychiatrist.com/jcp/vagus-nerve-stimulation-suicidal-ideation-treatment-resistant-major-depression-recover)
- **5-year registry** [OL, non-randomized, 61 sites, n=795; ≥4 failed treatments including ECT]. Aaronson ST et al., *Am J Psychiatry* 2017;174(7):640–648. doi:10.1176/appi.ajp.2017.16010034. Cumulative response 67.6% vs 40.9% and cumulative first remission 43.3% vs 25.7% (VNS+TAU vs TAU; both p<0.001). In ECT non-responders, response was 59.6% vs 34.1%. Median time to remission was 49 vs 65 months. — [AJP](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2017.16010034); [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/MED/28359201)
- **Cognition**: in an open-label cohort (n=14) followed for 2 years, learning and memory improved within 1 month and other domains improved gradually. 9 of 14 returned to skilled work. — [Desbeaumes Jodoin et al., J ECT 2018 (PDF)](https://francoisricher.uqam.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/136/Desbeaumes-Jodoin-VNS-cognition-2018-1.pdf). An earlier 10-week study of cognitive performance also exists (Sackeim et al., Neuropsychiatry Neuropsychol Behav Neurol 2001;14:53–62) — reference only, [cited in the same PDF](https://francoisricher.uqam.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/136/Desbeaumes-Jodoin-VNS-cognition-2018-1.pdf)
- **Regulatory and coverage** [REG/PR]:
  - FDA approved VNS in 2005 for adjunctive treatment in adults with ≥4 failed antidepressant treatments. CMS issued a 2007 national non-coverage decision, reconsidered it in 2018–2019, and finalized Coverage with Evidence Development in 2019. LivaNova submitted a reconsideration request in June 2025. The 2026 annual report says it will "advance its engagement with CMS as it works toward clarity on national reimbursement." — [LivaNova annual report](https://investor.livanova.com/static-files/40ebd931-674b-430f-85fd-f10686eb68fa); [LivaNova PR, June 4 2025](https://investor.livanova.com/news-releases/news-release-details/livanova-initiates-process-us-centers-medicare-and-medicaid)
  - A CMS transmittal effective July 1 2026 shows only coding maintenance of NCD 160.18, with "No policy … being changed." — [CMS Transmittal 13760](https://www.cms.gov/files/document/r13760otn.pdf)
  - The VA Community Care criteria (effective August 1 2026) cover VNS for TRD after ≥4 adequate trials. — [VA CDI 00073](https://department.va.gov/vha/community-care/cdi-templates/cdi-00073)

### Inferences
- VNS has essentially no cognitive cost and a long-horizon benefit, but its onset is slow (months) and its controlled effect is small. It is a "long game" add-on, not a route to a strong response within the next year before medical school. Surgery, cost and coverage are barriers.

### Gaps
- Bipolar arm results and the formal 24-month RECOVER paper were not fully extracted.
- No current CMS proposed decision memo on VNS for TRD was found as of October 2026.

## 6. Deep brain stimulation (SCC, VC/VS, MFB) and closed-loop or personalized DBS

### Takeaway
DBS is investigational for depression in the US. Abbott's sham-controlled TRANSCEND pivotal trial (SCC target; n=100; 25 US sites) is recruiting, with primary completion expected April 2027. Pooled long-term open-label data show response of about 46% at 12 months and 55% at 24 months, but the earlier pivotal RCTs (BROADEN, RECLAIM) failed at their blinded endpoints. Cognition appears stable or improved. Closed-loop DBS has striking single-patient results and only n≈12 feasibility trials. Brain surgery for a young adult planning medical school is a last-line consideration.

### Cited Findings
- **Pooled SCC DBS** (5 studies including BROADEN, 172 implanted, 5 countries; single-manufacturer data) [OL long-term follow-up]. Himes LM, Mayberg HS, Husain MM, Holtzheimer PE, Lozano AM, Kennedy SH, et al. *Brain Stimul* 2025;18(5):1632–1640. doi:10.1016/j.brs.2025.08.017.
  - 88% had prior ECT. MADRS fell 43% at 12 months and 53% at 24 months. Response 46% and 55%; remission 24.7% and 39.7%.
  - Suicide incidence 0.55 per 100 patient-years. Major attrition: 23% had data at 5 years.
  - Sources: [Europe PMC](https://europepmc.org/article/med/40854479); [Exa record](https://exa.ai/library/publication/565pwn51g7p)
- **Crowell et al., Am J Psychiatry 2019;176(11):949–956** [OL, n=28, 4–8 years]. Response ≥50% and remission ≥30% maintained in years 2–8. No suicides. — [AJP](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.18121427)
- **Failed pivotal RCTs**: BROADEN (SCC, n=90) had 6-month response of 20% with active stimulation, short of its 40% target, rising to 49% at 24 months open-label. RECLAIM (VC/VS, n=30) had 20% response at 16 weeks. — [Johnson et al., Mol Psychiatry 2024 review](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348289). Crowell 2019 reports BROADEN's 6-month figures as 17% active vs 22% sham — [AJP 2019](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.18121427). (Note: the two sources give different BROADEN percentages, likely from different response definitions or time points.)
- Meta-analysis of 14 open-label studies and 3 RCTs (n=233): 56% response, 35% remission. Long-term responders across targets (SCC, VC/VS, vALIC, MFB) ranged 32–80%. — [Johnson et al., Mol Psychiatry 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348289)
- **Cognition**: a randomized sham-controlled crossover trial (3-month periods) found no cognitive deterioration with SCG-DBS. A systematic review found no cognitive change up to 6 months and some improvement in memory, attention and processing speed, and executive function at 6–18 months. — [BMJ Mental Health 2025;28:e301408](https://mentalhealth.bmj.com/content/28/1/e301408). Long-term SCC DBS neuropsychological safety up to 42 months: Bogod et al., J Neuropsychiatry 2014 — [cited in Johnson 2024](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11348289)
- **TRANSCEND (Abbott, NCT06423430)** [ongoing pivotal RCT-sham]. Double-blind, delayed-stimulation/sham-controlled, 12 months; bilateral SCC white matter; Infinity DBS system. n=100; ages 22–70; unipolar non-psychotic MDD; ≥4 failed antidepressant treatments; episode ≥12 months or ≥3 lifetime episodes. RECRUITING at about 25 US sites (Emory, Mount Sinai, MGH, BIDMC, MUSC, UTSW, Baylor, WashU, Penn, UCLA, UC Davis, Mayo, others). Primary completion April 2027. — [CT.gov NCT06423430](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06423430). FDA Breakthrough Device designation [PR] — [Abbott press release, September 4 2024](https://abbott.mediaroom.com/2024-09-04-Abbott-Initiates-Clinical-Study-to-Evaluate-the-Use-of-Its-Deep-Brain-Stimulation-System-to-Manage-Severe-Depression). UTSW: "optimistically, this therapy could become more widely available within five years" — [UTSW MedBlog](https://utswmed.org/medblog/deep-brain-stimulation)
- **Closed-loop DBS (UCSF)**: Scangos KW et al., *Nat Med* 2021;27:1696–1700 [n=1]. A 36-year-old woman unresponsive to multiple drugs and ECT received the NeuroPace RNS device (VC/VS stimulation triggered by an amygdala gamma biomarker). MADRS went from 33 to 14 at 12 days and below 10 months later. — [PMC11219029](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11219029/). PReSiDio (NCT04004169), n=12, is recruiting. It requires an episode ≥2 years with "4 adequate trials (including ECT)." — [CT.gov NCT04004169](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT04004169). PReSiDio-BP (bipolar) NCT07127913 — [UCSF](https://clinicaltrials.ucsf.edu/trial/NCT07127913)
- Other recruiting DBS studies (small, mostly mechanistic): Mayberg Percept PC LFP study (NCT05773755); Northwell directional DBS (NCT06096207); UCLA tractography-guided SCC (NCT03952962); Baylor (NCT05418894). — [CT.gov search results via NCT links: NCT05773755](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05773755); [NCT06096207](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06096207); [NCT03952962](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT03952962)

### Inferences
- DBS does not appear to harm cognition and may give the most durable benefit in extreme refractoriness. But it carries surgical risk, response takes months, and controlled efficacy is unproven. For this patient it belongs after less invasive options (SAINT, MST, possibly ECT variants) have been tried.
- PReSiDio appears to require prior ECT in the treatment history. TRANSCEND does not mandate ECT but requires age ≥22.

### Gaps
- TRANSCEND enrollment progress was not publicly reported. No interim data.
- Alagapan et al. 2023 (Nature) outcome numbers were not extracted.
- No quantitative MFB-DBS RCT results were retrieved in this pass.

## 7. Transcranial focused ultrasound (tFUS/LIFU) and temporal interference (TI) stimulation

### Takeaway
These are non-invasive ways to reach deep targets such as the subcallosal cingulate, amygdala and internal capsule without surgery. Human data are early-phase: a small sham-controlled crossover (n=22), small RCTs (n=23) and open-label pilots, with rapid but short-term mood effects. None is approved, and all are years from clinical availability. Early studies report no cognitive harm, but measurement has been limited.

### Cited Findings
- **SCC tFUS (Utah)** [RCT-sham, crossover, n=22 TRD]. Riis TS, Feldman DA, …, Mickey BJ, Kubanek J. *Biol Psychiatry* 2025;97:825–834. fMRI showed reduced SCC activity during stimulation (p=0.028). In the per-protocol sample (n=19), real stimulation beat sham on HDRS-6 at 24 hours and on sadness (d>1). Only trends in ITT. HDRS-6 changes at 1 and 7 days were −55% and −52% (active) vs −22% and −29% (sham). — [PubMed 39396736](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39396736/); commentary [Neuropsychopharmacology 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-025-02194-0)
- **Metalens tFUS (Paris)** [OL, n=5 TRD; 25 sessions over 5 days targeting SCC tracts]. Depression scores fell 60.9% on average; 4 of 5 responded and 2 remitted; no serious adverse events. — [Attali et al., Brain Stimul 2025;18:1004–1014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1935861X25000993). The follow-on trial NCT06085950 (Sainte-Anne) is RECRUITING, n=32. — [CT.gov NCT06085950](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06085950)
- **DLPFC tFUS (Korea)** [RCT-sham, n=23 MDD]. MADRS fell from 28.5 to 14.8 at 2-week follow-up (active) vs 29.2 to 24.8 (sham); group×time interaction p=0.003. No clinically significant change on CANTAB cognitive testing. — [Oh et al., Psychiatry Investig 2024 (PMC11321877)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11321877/)
- **DMN (amPFC) tFUS (Arizona)** [OL, n=20]. HDRS response 45% and remission 35%. — [Schachtner et al., Front Psychiatry 2025](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2025.1451828/full)
- **ALIC LIFU** [RCT-sham crossover target engagement, n=21]. Functional disconnection d=−1.55 and increased positive emotion d=0.6. — [Neuropsychopharmacology 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41386-025-02252-7)
- **Recruiting or upcoming tFUS trials**: Sunnybrook dome-helmet neuromodulation for TRD (NCT07094789, n=20); Laureate Institute LIFU in TRD (NCT07166289, n=140); Attune ATTN201 head-worn FUS dose-finding (NCT07851038, n=300, not yet recruiting); Utah personalized tFUS R61 (NCT06902298, active, not recruiting); Stanford LIFU for post-TBI depression (NCT07828483). — [CT.gov NCT07094789](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07094789); [NCT07166289](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07166289); [NCT07851038](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07851038); [NCT06902298](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06902298). (Separately, ablative MR-guided FUS capsulotomy trials such as NCT07108257 are lesioning procedures, not neuromodulation — [CT.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07108257).)
- **Temporal interference stimulation**:
  - Amygdala TIS [OL single-arm, n=32 MDD, 5 days]. HAMD-17 response 59.4% after treatment and 50.0% at 1 month. Mild adverse events (sleepiness 24.1%). — [BMC Psychiatry 2026](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1186/s12888-026-08439-6)
  - Multicentre sham-controlled RCT (NCT06477276), active-arm secondary analysis. Response 44% at week 8. — [J Affect Disord 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0165032726005112)
  - Bipolar depression, NAc tTIS [OL, n=25 completers]. Response 28%. Memory and executive function improved. — [Mol Psychiatry 2025](https://preview-www.nature.com/articles/s41380-025-03292-7)
  - A registered RCT of TI-tACS for MDD (NCT07712159). — [CT.gov](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT07712159)

### Inferences
- tFUS targeting the SCC is the most conceptually promising "non-invasive DBS" approach. Realistically it is 3–7+ years from routine clinical use, and access now is only through small US/Canadian/French trials.
- TI data come mainly from Chinese centres and are early; the sham-arm comparison was not available in the extracted text.

### Gaps
- No tFUS trial has reported durability beyond about 1–2 weeks in TRD.
- No company has announced an FDA submission for therapeutic tFUS in depression.
- Sham-arm response rates for the TI RCT (NCT06477276) were not extracted.

## 8. Home tDCS (Flow FL-100) and convulsive vs non-convulsive comparisons

### Takeaway
Home tDCS (Flow FL-100) was FDA-approved by PMA on December 8 2025 and launched in the US on about September 17 2026. Its label explicitly excludes patients "considered treatment refractory to medication." The pivotal RCT showed HDRS remission of 44.9% vs 21.8% in a mostly non-refractory population. It has a benign cognitive profile but likely limited power for this patient. Across modalities, ECT remains the strongest acute antidepressant in network meta-analysis. Non-convulsive options trade some efficacy for no meaningful cognitive cost, with SAINT/SNT the possible exception on efficacy.

### Cited Findings
- **Woodham et al. 2024** [RCT-sham, fully remote, UK/US, n=174]. Woodham RD, Selvaraj S, Lajmi N, …, Young AH, Fu CHY. *Nat Med* 2024 (online October 21 2024). NCT05202119. 10 weeks of tDCS (2 mA, anode left DLPFC, 30 minutes per session).
  - Primary HDRS improvement: 9.41 vs 7.14 points (95% CI 0.51–4.01; p=0.012; d=0.37).
  - Response/remission: HDRS 58.3% vs 37.8% and 44.9% vs 21.8%; MADRS 64.2% vs 32.3% and 57.5% vs 29.4%.
  - No serious device-related adverse events; no mania. In the open-label extension, about 90% kept their response from week 10 to week 20.
  - Sources: [Nature Medicine](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41591-024-03305-y); [PubMed 39433921](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39433921/); [Supplement](https://media.springernature.com/original/springer-static/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41591-024-03305-y/MediaObjects/41591_2024_3305_MOESM1_ESM.pdf)
- Woodham et al. note that a large clinic-based adjunctive tDCS trial (Burkhardt et al.) found no effect over 6 weeks. An earlier individual-patient-data meta-analysis found response of 30.9% vs 18.9% (NNT 9) and remission of 19.9% vs 11.7% (NNT 13). — [Woodham 2024 full text](https://digitalcommons.library.tmc.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=3515&context=uthmed_docs)
- **FDA**: PMA P230024 approved December 8 2025; Breakthrough designation May 31 2022. Indication: "moderate to severe major depressive disorder (MDD) in the current episode, either as monotherapy or as an adjunctive treatment, in patients 18 years and older who are not considered treatment refractory to medication." — [FDA SSED P230024](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/P230024B.pdf); [Bikson, Brunoni, George, Brain Stimul 2026;19(1):103021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/41475514)
- **Launch** [PR]: "Flow Neuroscience launches FL-100 in the US … September 17, 2026 … available by prescription only" — [BioSpace](https://www.biospace.com/press-releases/flow-neuroscience-launches-fl-100-in-the-us-the-first-fda-approved-at-home-tdcs-treatment-for-depression). Expected retail price $500–800 — [Psychiatric Times, December 2025](https://www.psychiatrictimes.com/view/fda-approves-first-at-home-brain-stimulation-device-for-treatment-of-depression). Personalized HD-tDCS has also been tested in an RCT (Jog et al., JAMA Netw Open 2025;8(9):e2531189), cited in [Bikson et al. 2026](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1935861X25004231)
- **Convulsive vs non-convulsive (network meta-analysis)**: response ORs vs sham were bitemporal ECT 8.91 (2.57–30.91), high-dose RUL ECT 7.27, priming TMS 6.02, MST 5.55, bilateral rTMS 4.92, bilateral TBS 4.44, iTBS 3.20, HF-left rTMS 3.17 and tDCS 2.65. In head-to-head network estimates, bitemporal and high-dose RUL ECT were associated with higher response than the other active treatments. — [Mutz et al., BMJ 2019 (PMC6435996)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6435996/)

### Inferences
- Flow is cheap, home-based and cognitively benign, but it is labelled for non-refractory patients and has a modest effect. For this patient it is at most a low-risk adjunct or bridge, not a primary strategy.
- No SAINT vs ECT RCT exists, and the network meta-analysis predates SAINT/SNT. Any statement that SAINT is "as good as ECT" is unproven.

### Gaps
- No head-to-head trial of SNT/SAINT vs ECT or MST was found (none registered in the searches run).
- Flow has no published data in documented TRD with ≥4 failures or after rTMS failure.

## 9. Other emerging modalities: epidural cortical stimulation, minimally invasive skull-mounted implants, photobiomodulation

### Takeaway
Several "between TMS and DBS" implants are entering first-in-human trials in 2025–2026: Motif DOT under an FDA IDE (April 2026) and Inner Cosmos (early-feasibility completion, company-reported). Epidural cortical stimulation has small long-term series. Transcranial photobiomodulation has mixed or negative controlled trials. None is near approval.

### Cited Findings
- **Epidural prefrontal cortical stimulation (EpCS)**:
  - Nahas et al., Biol Psychiatry 2010;67:101–109 [OL, n=5]. 54.9% HRSD-24 improvement at 7 months; 3 of 5 remitted. "No … reports of emergent cognitive deficits." — [PMC2863140](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2863140/)
  - Five-year follow-up: 3 of 5 (60%) still in remission. Five serious adverse events (1 infection, 4 device malfunctions with suicidal ideation or hospitalization). — [Williams et al., Brain Stimul 2016;9(6):897–904](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27443912/)
  - Kopell et al. 2011, left-DLPFC EpCS [RCT single-blind, n=12]. No difference vs sham in the controlled phase; improvement only in the open-label phase. — [Exa record (Neurosurgery 2011)](https://exa.ai/library/publication/fmcbmr5k0n7)
- **Motif Neurotech DOT** (wireless, blueberry-sized, embedded in skull bone above the dura, does not contact brain tissue) [PR]. FDA approved an IDE in April 2026 for the RESONATE early-feasibility trial in TRD. Sites: Baylor, Mass General Brigham, Emory, UTHealth Houston, Iowa, Utah, NYU. — [Rice University news, April 28 2026](https://news.rice.edu/news/2026/brain-computer-interface-based-rice-research-wins-fda-approval-first-clinical-trial); [Patient Worthy](https://patientworthy.com/2026/04/28/motif-neurotech-gains-fda-clearance-to-launch-first-in-human-trial-of-implantable-bci-for-depression)
- **Inner Cosmos** (sub-scalp implant, home stimulation) [PR, company-reported, not peer-reviewed]. Announced completion of an FDA early-feasibility study (November 2025). Claims "Patient 2 achieved an 83% improvement from baseline … a 60% gain over their best prior TMS outcome." — [Inner Cosmos](https://innercosmos.ai/2025/11/26/clinical-trial-update-fda-early-feasibility-study-completion); [secondary summary](https://www.neurofounders.co/articles/inside-inner-cosmos-digital-pills-for-the-mind)
- **Transcranial photobiomodulation (near-infrared)**:
  - ELATED-2 [RCT-sham pilot, n=21]. d=0.90 (BOCF). HAM-D response 50% vs 27% (not significant). — [PMC7864111](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7864111/)
  - ELATED-3 [RCT-sham, n=49]. No difference vs sham. — [Europe PMC 35950904](https://europepmc.org/article/med/35950904)
  - Wearable self-administered tPBM [RCT-sham, n=48]. No antidepressant effect; sleep improved. — [J Affect Disord 2024](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0165032724020548)

### Inferences
- Skull-mounted cortical stimulators (Motif, Inner Cosmos) are the most interesting "chronic home TMS-equivalent" concept for someone who responds to cortical stimulation but relapses. They are first-in-human only. Inner Cosmos's comparison with prior TMS outcomes is anecdotal and company-reported.
- Photobiomodulation is not ready, and its controlled evidence is largely negative at the tested doses.

### Gaps
- No peer-reviewed efficacy data for Motif DOT or Inner Cosmos were found.
- Personalized EEG/MRI-targeted ECT has no clinical-outcome trials beyond the E-field amplitude work in Section 2.

## 10. Cross-modality synthesis: which options best fit "strong response with near-negligible lasting cognitive impairment" after standard rTMS failure

### Takeaway
On current evidence, two options look best for this patient. **SAINT/SNT** offers a high acute remission probability (in rTMS-naïve patients), no cognitive cost and FDA clearance, but evidence in rTMS failures is weak and relapse is common. **MST**, available only in trials, offers ECT-class (slightly lower) efficacy with much less memory impact. VNS and DBS have no cognitive cost but are slow, invasive and modest or unproven in controlled trials. FEAST, low-amplitude ECT, NET, tFUS, TI and skull-mounted implants are research-only.

### Cited Findings
- SAINT/SNT: 1-month remission 46.2% vs 0% and 50.0% vs 20.8% in two sham RCTs, both excluding prior rTMS — [Cole 2022](https://ajp.psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2021.20101429); [Kratter 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12805067/). 5 of 6 prior rTMS non-responders responded (open-label) — [Cole 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720). 33% of all treated patients in remission at 12 weeks without maintenance — [Geoly 2025](https://exa.ai/library/publication/q65chnk0rlf). No negative cognitive effects — [Cole 2020](https://psychiatryonline.org/doi/10.1176/appi.ajp.2019.19070720)
- MST: remission 22.5% vs 27.8% (non-inferior); autobiographical-memory worsening 2.7% vs 17.3% — [CREST-MST](https://www.thelancet.com/journals/lanpsy/article/PIIS2215-0366(26)00060-X/fulltext). Not approved anywhere — [MagVenture](https://magventure.com/us/new-lancet-psychiatry-publication-reports-results-from-large-randomized-trial-of-magnetic-seizure-therapy-in-depression)
- VNS: RECOVER primary endpoint negative; secondary measures positive — [Conway 2025](https://digitalcommons.wustl.edu/cgi/viewcontent.cgi?article=7421&context=oa_4). Cognition stable or improved — [Desbeaumes Jodoin 2018](https://francoisricher.uqam.ca/wp-content/uploads/sites/136/Desbeaumes-Jodoin-VNS-cognition-2018-1.pdf)
- DBS: 55% response at 24 months open-label, pooled — [Himes 2025](https://europepmc.org/article/med/40854479). No cognitive deterioration in a sham crossover — [BMJ Ment Health 2025](https://mentalhealth.bmj.com/content/28/1/e301408). TRANSCEND recruiting, ages 22–70 — [NCT06423430](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT06423430)
- Home tDCS: label excludes medication-refractory patients — [FDA SSED](https://www.accessdata.fda.gov/cdrh_docs/pdf23/P230024B.pdf)
- rTMS-family benefit shrinks with more prior failures — [Chaikind 2025](https://exa.ai/library/publication/l8rzlft3kxv); [FOUR-D secondary](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10895496/)

### Inferences
- A plausible evidence-ordered sequence for the report writer to weigh (my inference, not a guideline):
  1. SAINT/SNT, with a pre-agreed retreatment or maintenance plan. Ask the provider directly about outcomes in prior-rTMS non-responders.
  2. If SAINT fails, or a convulsive option is needed, MST via a trial (UTHealth Houston NCT04080778; or international sites), weighed against RUL-UB ECT, which another researcher covers.
  3. VNS as a long-horizon adjunct if relapsing.
  4. DBS (TRANSCEND) only after convulsive options.
  5. Experimental tFUS, FEAST, low-amplitude and implant trials as opportunistic additions.
- "Near-negligible chance of lasting cognitive impairment" is best supported for SAINT/TMS, tDCS, VNS and DBS (no evidence of cognitive harm). MST shows a large reduction in memory harm vs RUL-UB ECT on a 2.7% vs 17.3% autobiographical-memory measure, but not zero. Its long-term cognitive outcomes beyond the treatment course were not found.
- Psychomotor slowing and brain fog in this patient may partly reflect anesthesia and post-ictal effects with convulsive therapies. Non-convulsive options avoid both. MST still requires anesthesia and seizures, though reorientation is about 15 minutes faster in pooled data ([Chen 2021](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41398-021-01560-y.pdf); [Frontiers 2026a](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychiatry/articles/10.3389/fpsyt.2026.1873016/full)).

### Gaps
- No head-to-head data comparing SAINT with MST or ECT.
- No age-stratified (young-adult) cognitive outcome data for MST or SAINT.
- Scite could not be used (no paid plan), so citation-integrity checks such as retraction and correction screening rely on publisher pages. One correction was found, for CREST-MST, and it does not alter conclusions.
