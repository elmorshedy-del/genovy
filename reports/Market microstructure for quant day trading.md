# Tiny Scalps Feed the Fastest Traders

The evidence rejects the premise that many small predicted trades are easier than one big move, at least at the horizons it implies: single ticks, cents and seconds. It supports a reworked version: many modest bets that are as independent of each other as possible, held for minutes to hours in the cheapest instruments. Trading costs are roughly fixed per trade, but the typical price move grows only with the square root of holding time. Under a simple random-walk model, an ES signal traded with market orders needs a correlation with the next move of about **0.8 at one second, 0.10 at one minute and 0.013 at one hour**. On micro contracts, exchange and broker fees alone come to **about one tick per round trip on MES and 2.4 ticks on MNQ**, before any spread is paid ([Topstep fee table](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/8284213-topstepx-commissions-and-fees)). The traders on the other side of sub-minute trades are colocated firms that react within tens of microseconds. They earned a **median Sharpe ratio of 4.3 in E-mini futures**, and small traders lost the most to them per contract ([Baron, Brogaard, Hagströmer & Kirilenko](https://www.cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/@economicanalysis/documents/file/oce_riskandreturn0414.pdf)). Leverage scales profits and costs together and leaves expectancy per unit of risk unchanged. Buying 0DTE options to scalp small moves means paying roughly **$190–$260 an hour in time decay on one at-the-money SPX contract**, plus the spread. That is a bet that realized volatility will beat implied volatility, and implied usually wins. Population data agree. **97% of Brazilians who day-traded index futures for 300+ days lost money** ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)), and **under 1% of Taiwanese day traders were predictably profitable after fees** ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://www.escholarship.org/uc/item/7k75v0qx)). The approaches that survive are slower and depend on market conditions: intraday momentum into the close (R² of 1–2%), position sizing scaled to forecast volatility, dealer gamma as a filter for the volatility regime, a schedule built around market events, and possibly breakouts in news-driven "stocks in play". Every one of these is fragile to slippage. So the first part of the algorithm to build is a cost-and-fill simulator with a validation harness, not a signal.

## Wholesalers, one-tick queues and microsecond races shape the plumbing

### Half of US stock volume never touches a lit exchange

US equities traded **17.6 billion shares ($1.1 trillion) a day in 2025**, and 2025 was the first full year in which off-exchange volume passed half the total. **50.6% of consolidated volume printed to trade reporting facilities**: 81.3% of it executed by principal dealers and 18.7% on alternative trading systems ([Cboe 2025 U.S. Equities Year in Review](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)). Multiplying through, about 41% of all volume is internalized by wholesalers and single-dealer platforms and about 9.5% trades in dark pools. That leaves roughly 49% for lit exchanges, and part of that is hidden or midpoint liquidity, which was 17.2% of volume on Cboe's own venues. The split is sharper in the names a day trader watches. **59.47% of volume in the 20 most-active stocks executed off-board in July 2026** ([Rosenblatt, July 2026](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-july-2026)). The venue map is still growing: the Texas Stock Exchange began live trading on July 10, 2026 ([Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/publications/2026/07/texas-stock-exchange-opens-for-trading)). The trading day is getting longer too. Pre-market volume rose 111% in 2025 to 5.91% of the total ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/2025-u-s-equities-year-in-review)), and Nasdaq plans near-24-hour weekday trading from December 6, 2026, pending SEC approval and SIP readiness ([Cooley](https://governancebeat.cooley.com/24-hour-trading-nasdaqs-faqs-and-secs-roundtable/)).

The off-exchange majority comes from the economics of retail order flow. The SEC's most recent detailed data are from Q1 2022. Brokers then sent **more than 90% of individual investors' marketable orders to six wholesalers**. The wholesalers internalized 90.44% of that dollar volume and gave price improvement on 83.17% of those orders, against 8.78% on exchanges. Adverse selection, the price moving against the liquidity provider after a fill, was only **1.26 bps for these orders at wholesalers versus 4.40 bps on exchanges** ([SEC Order Competition proposal, 34-96495](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/proposed/2022/34-96495.pdf)). That gap explains the whole arrangement: wholesalers pay for and improve retail orders because those orders are, on average, uninformed. The SEC withdrew its proposal to auction those orders before internalization in June 2025 ([National Law Review](https://natlawreview.com/article/sec-formally-withdraws-fourteen-rule-proposals)), so the structure will persist. For an algorithm this cuts both ways. A small marketable order sent through a retail broker will usually be filled by a wholesaler, with a fraction of a cent of improvement. An order routed directly to an exchange trades where the remaining flow is most toxic.

Regulation sets the grid all of this runs on. Regulation NMS (2005) has three core rules. Rule 611 forbids trading through protected quotes. Rule 610 caps exchange access fees at 30 mils ($0.003) per share. Rule 612 sets a one-cent minimum quoting increment for stocks priced above $1 ([SEC adopting release](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/34-51808.pdf)). In September 2024 the SEC adopted three changes: a half-penny tick for stocks whose time-weighted average quoted spread is 1.5 cents or less, a 10-mil cap on access fees, and a requirement that fees be determinable at the time of execution ([SEC fact sheet](https://www.sec.gov/files/34-101070-fact-sheet.pdf)). The D.C. Circuit upheld the rule in October 2025. The SEC's June 11, 2026 order then pushed compliance for the new tick and fee caps to the **first business day of November 2027** ([SEC Order 34-105656](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-11997.pdf)). As of October 2026 the tick is still a penny and the cap is still 30 mils.

Two other changes are already live. The round-lot redefinition took effect on November 3, 2025. Stocks priced $250.01–$1,000, a band that currently holds SPY and QQQ, now have 40-share round lots, and SIPs report quote sizes in shares ([CTA Round Lot FAQ](https://www.ctaplan.com/publicdocs/ctaplan/CTA_Round_Lot_Changes_FAQ.pdf)). The amended Rule 605 began collecting execution-quality data on August 1, 2026, with the first reports due at the end of September ([Federal Register 2025-19316](https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2025-19316.pdf)). That gives retail traders broker-level public price-improvement statistics for the first time. Any equity backtest spanning November 2025 mixes two round-lot regimes. Any logic tuned to today's one-cent tick will need recalibration once half-penny ticks shorten queues in tick-constrained names.

### Index futures put price discovery in a single FIFO book

ES is $50 times the S&P 500 futures price, with a 0.25-point tick worth $12.50 ([CME E-mini FAQ](https://www.cmegroup.com/trading/equity-index/eminifaq.html)). MES is one-tenth the size, $5 per point with a $1.25 tick ([CME MES specs](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/equities/sp/micro-e-mini-sandp-500.contractSpecs.html)). NQ is $20 times the Nasdaq-100 with a $5.00 tick ([CME NQ specs](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/equities/nasdaq/e-mini-nasdaq-100.contractSpecs.html)), and MNQ is $2 per point with a $0.50 tick ([CME MNQ specs](https://www.cmegroup.com/markets/equities/nasdaq/micro-e-mini-nasdaq-100.contractSpecs.html)). All four trade Sunday to Friday, 5:00 pm to 4:00 pm Central, with a daily 60-minute halt ([CME E-mini overview](https://www.cmegroup.com/education/lessons/product-e-mini-s-and-p)). They expire quarterly at the third-Friday open against a Special Opening Quotation and halt together with the equity-market circuit breakers ([CME Rulebook Ch. 358](https://www.cmegroup.com/rulebook/CME/IV/350/358/358.pdf)). The daily settlement price is itself a VWAP of lead-month trades between 14:59:30 and 15:00:00 CT ([CME settlement procedure](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/E4DaBw)).

ES and NQ outrights match on pure price-time priority (first in, first out), as does about 70% of all CME volume ([Databento](https://databento.com/blog/cme-matching-algorithms-explained)). An order loses its place in the queue if its price changes, its quantity increases or its account changes ([CME developer documentation](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EPICSANDBOX/pages/457218479)). Retail participation has surged through the micros. Micro E-mini average daily volume (ADV) set a record above 3.4 million contracts in Q2 2025 ([CME](https://www.cmegroup.com/newsletters/quarterly-equity-index-recap/2025-july-equity-index-recap.html)). In July 2026 micros made up 54% of CME's record 8.2 million contracts of equity-index ADV ([TradeInformer](https://www.tradeinformer.com/institutional-trading/cme-group-sets-july-volume-record-as-equity-index-trading-jumps-48)).

Futures lead the cash market. Using 2000 data, Hasbrouck found the E-mini carried roughly 90% of S&P 500 price discovery, and NQ over 80% for the Nasdaq-100 ([Hasbrouck](https://archive.nyu.edu/jspui/bitstream/2451/27374/2/FIN-00-046.pdf)). In 2005–2011 data, **91% of ES–SPY arbitrage opportunities were started by a move in ES**, even though the two instruments' returns correlated at only 0.09 at a 10-millisecond horizon ([Budish, Cramton & Shim](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)). No primary estimate after 2015 was found, so treat the 90% figure as directionally right but dated.

The printed cash index is a weighted average of 500 last trades, so it lags. Futures fair value follows cost of carry, F ≈ S·e^((r−q)T), where r is the financing rate, q the dividend yield and T the time to expiry, and the basis decays to zero at expiry. Intraday signals should therefore be computed on ES or SPY quotes, not the index print. Tick size relative to price matters a great deal here. At an S&P level of 6,000–7,000, one ES tick is about 0.36–0.42 bp, and the spread is almost always one tick. That makes ES the textbook **large-tick, deep-queue** contract. An NQ tick at Nasdaq-100 levels of 20,000–25,000 is only about 0.1 bp, so NQ moves more ticks per unit of time and has thinner queues.

### The order book pays for queue position, not insight

Under price-time priority, the order at the front of a queue fills first, and it fills mostly against small, uninformed orders. The order at the back fills mainly when a large aggressive order sweeps the whole price level, and that tends to happen just before the price moves through. Moallemi and Yuan calibrate this for large-tick Nasdaq stocks. They find the value difference between the front of the queue and an average position is about **0.21–0.26 ticks, the same order of magnitude as the spread** ([Moallemi & Yuan](https://drupalgsb-test.paas.cc.columbia.edu/sites/default/files-efs/pubfiles/25461/queue-value-2016.pdf)). This is why passive "earn the spread" strategies are queue games. It is also why a backtest that assumes a resting limit order fills whenever the price touches it overstates performance.

The order types an engine needs follow from this. Limit orders earn the spread but are adversely selected. Marketable limit orders pay the spread. IOC and FAK orders avoid resting exposure. Post-only orders guarantee you are the passive side. Midpoint pegs trade at the mid without being displayed. Intermarket sweep orders are a broker-dealer privilege that exists because of Rule 611: they let a router take liquidity on one venue while sweeping better-priced quotes elsewhere ([SEC Reg NMS release](https://www.sec.gov/rules/final/34-51808.pdf)).

Market data come in three levels. L1 is top of book plus trades. L2 is depth aggregated by price, such as CME's 10-deep market-by-price feed. L3 is market-by-order: every add, modify, cancel and execution with order IDs, as in Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH or CME MBO. Only L3 lets you reconstruct your own place in the queue. It is also the only data that shows failed race attempts ([Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29011/w29011.pdf)).

The most useful single way to classify an instrument is its spread-to-tick ratio. **Large-tick** assets almost always have a spread of exactly one tick. **Small-tick** assets have spreads of several ticks ([Eisler, Bouchaud & Kockelkoren](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/0904.0900v3); [Dayri & Rosenbaum](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1207.6325)). In large-tick books such as ES, SPY and most low-priced stocks, the price changes only when a queue is used up. Queue sizes and imbalance therefore carry most of the short-horizon information, and crossing the spread costs a large share of a typical move. In small-tick books such as high-priced stocks, quotes revise inside the spread and queues are short, so the state of the spread and the deeper levels matter more. Which regime you are in shapes both the choice of signals and the execution tactics.

### Spread theory says you pay for other people's information

Microstructure theory splits the spread into three costs that a liquidity provider must recover. The first is order processing. The second is inventory risk ([Ho & Stoll 1981](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2881%2990020-9)). The third is adverse selection. In Glosten and Milgrom's model, a competitive dealer sets the ask at the asset's expected value given that someone is buying, and the bid at its expected value given a sale. The spread therefore widens with the share of informed traders, and transaction prices follow a martingale ([Glosten & Milgrom](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-405X%2885%2990044-3)). Kyle's model gives the linear version. Price moves by λ for each unit of net order flow, with λ = σ_v/(2σ_u), where σ_v is the size of the informed trader's information and σ_u the volume of noise trading, and the informed trader hides inside the noise ([Kyle 1985](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913210)). λ can be estimated intraday by regressing mid-price changes on signed volume; it rises at the open and around news.

Roll's estimator backs the effective spread out of bid-ask bounce as 2√(−Cov(Δp_t, Δp_{t−1})), and it is undefined when returns trend ([Roll 1984](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1984.tb03897.x)). Huang and Stoll found a large order-processing component, with smaller but significant adverse-selection and inventory components ([Huang & Stoll 1997](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v10y1997i4p995-1034.html)).

The working tool is the realized spread: the effective spread minus the mid-price move over a fixed window after the fill. A passive strategy that earns a positive effective spread but a negative realized spread is being picked off. Measuring this markout on every fill is the cleanest diagnostic a small trader can run.

Avellaneda and Stoikov's standard market-making model quotes around a reservation price r = s − q·γ·σ²·(T−t), where s is the mid, q the inventory, γ risk aversion and σ volatility, so quotes lean against the inventory held. The total spread is γσ²(T−t) + (2/γ)·ln(1 + γ/k), where k measures how fast fill probability falls with distance from the mid ([Avellaneda & Stoikov](https://www.math.nyu.edu/~avellane/HighFrequencyTrading.pdf)). In a one-tick book like ES the model's continuous-price assumption fails. It reduces to decisions to join, skip or cancel at fixed levels, which again makes queue position the variable that matters most.

Empirical regularities fill out the picture. The price impact of a large order executed in pieces follows a square-root law, Δ ≈ Y·σ·√(Q/V), where Q is order size, V daily volume and Y of order one. Across markets the exponent ranges from 0.4 to 0.7 ([Tóth et al.](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/1105.1694)), although one study finds a logarithm fits more of the size range ([Zarinelli et al.](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/1412.2152)). For a trade of 0.01% of ADV in a stock with 2% daily volatility, impact is about 2 bps. Small traders therefore operate where spread, fees and adverse selection dominate and impact is secondary.

The signs of successive trades have long memory: their autocorrelation decays as ℓ^(−γ), with γ ≈ 0.5 in stocks and 0.8 in futures, because large orders are split into many small ones ([Lillo, Mike & Farmer](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0412708)). Each trade's impact then decays in a way that offsets that memory, so prices stay close to a random walk ([Bouchaud et al. propagator model](https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/0307332)). That is the deep reason order flow is predictable while prices are not.

The best short-horizon estimate of fair value is Stoikov's micro-price: the expected future mid given the current imbalance and spread. It is a martingale by construction and predicts better than either the mid or the simple size-weighted mid ([Stoikov](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=2970694)). Volume and volatility follow a U-shape through the day ([Admati & Pfleiderer](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/1.1.3)). The closing auction absorbed about 9% of daily volume in 2024 and about 20% on index-rebalance and expiry days ([BMLL](https://www.bmlltech.com/news/market-insight/into-the-close-unpacking-u-s-closing-auction-dynamics-and-the-impact-of-the-russell-reconstitution)); in July 2026 it was 7.93% ([Rosenblatt](https://www.rblt.com/market-structure-reports/us-securities-volumes-july-2026)). For a deeper treatment, the standard modern text is [Bouchaud, Bonart, Donier & Gould, *Trades, Quotes and Prices*](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316659335).

### Microsecond races set the floor everyone else trades above

The best measurement of high-frequency competition comes from London Stock Exchange message data. **Latency-arbitrage races account for about 22% of volume.** They happen about once a minute per FTSE 100 stock. The modal winning margin is 5–10 microseconds, and the fastest observed reaction time is 29 microseconds. The top six firms take over 80% of race wins. The races cost about 0.42 bp on all volume traded and make up a third of the effective spread ([Aquilina, Budish & O'Neill](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w29011/w29011.pdf)). In ES–SPY, the median arbitrage opportunity lasted 97 ms in 2005 and 7 ms in 2011, while profit per opportunity stayed constant. In the authors' words, the arms race "just continually raises the bar" ([Budish, Cramton & Shim](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)). Microwave links now carry data from CME's Aurora data center to Nasdaq's Carteret facility in 3.982 ms one way ([McKay Brothers/Quincy](https://mckay-brothers.com/quincy-latency-reductions-nearing-perfection-on-aurora-nj-data-network)).

The winners include Citadel Securities, which says it executes 35%+ of US retail volume ([Citadel Securities](https://www.citadelsecurities.com/news-and-insights/from-the-desk-of-jim-esposito/)), and Jane Street, at more than 10% of North American equity volume in 2024 ([Global Trading](https://www.globaltrading.net/?p=38181)). A retail algorithm that is not colocated is one to four orders of magnitude slower. Vendors claim a Chicago VPS reaches CME in about a millisecond. A home connection plus a broker API takes tens to hundreds of milliseconds. Interactive Brokers' default API data are aggregated snapshots every 250 ms for futures ([IBKR TWS API](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/top_data.html)). Every resting order at the best price is therefore a free option sold to faster traders. Any signal that exists only below about 10 milliseconds, such as ES leading SPY or a headline hitting the tape, is out of reach.

## Fixed costs and square-root moves sink the tiny-scalp premise

### Fees alone eat a tick on every micro round trip

The cheapest complete 2026 fee schedule found is Topstep's. It charges **$3.78 per round turn on ES and NQ and $1.22 on MES and MNQ**, exchange and NFA fees included ([Topstep](https://help.topstep.com/en/articles/8284213-topstepx-commissions-and-fees)). Broker-tier estimates run from $3.30 to $5.30 for ES and from $0.60 to $1.10 for micros ([damnpropfirms](https://damnpropfirms.com/trading-guides/futures-commissions-explained-what-you-actually-pay/)). NinjaTrader's free plan alone charges $1.29 per side on standard contracts and $0.39 on micros before exchange fees ([NinjaTrader](https://ninjatrader.com/pricing/)). IBKR's lowest-volume futures tier is $0.85 per contract per side plus exchange fees ([IBKR](https://www.interactivebrokers.com.sg/en/index.php?f=49560)). Converting these fees into ticks shows why the choice of contract dominates scalping economics.

| Contract | Multiplier | Tick value | Round-trip fees | Fees in ticks | Fees plus crossing a 1-tick spread |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| ES | $50/pt | $12.50 | $3.78 | 0.30 | 1.30 ticks ($16.28) |
| MES | $5/pt | $1.25 | $1.22 | 0.98 | 1.98 ticks ($2.47) |
| NQ | $20/pt | $5.00 | $3.78 | 0.76 | 1.76 ticks ($8.78) |
| MNQ | $2/pt | $0.50 | $1.22 | 2.44 | 3.44 ticks ($1.72) |

The tick is the same size in index points, but a micro tick is worth one-tenth as much in dollars. As a result, **micros cost about 3.2 times more than full-size contracts per dollar of notional exposure**. An MNQ trader who crosses the spread both ways starts every round trip 3.4 ticks behind. At 20 market-order round trips a day, the yearly cost per contract is about $82,000 for ES and $12,400 for MES.

Day-trade margins make the problem worse psychologically. NinjaTrader asks $500 of intraday margin for ES and $50 for MES ([NinjaTrader](https://ninjatrader.com/pricing/)), against roughly $11,800 of overnight maintenance margin at TradeStation ([TradeStation](https://www.tradestation.com/?p=42775)). With SPX near 7,700, one ES contract controls about $385,000, so a $500 margin is about 770 times leverage and a single tick is 2.5% of the margin posted.

### Bracket geometry, not skill, sets the win rate

Take a trade with a profit target of T ticks, a stop of S ticks and a round-trip cost of C ticks. Its expectancy is E = p(T − C) − (1 − p)(S + C), so the **breakeven win rate is p\* = (S + C)/(T + S)**. The table applies this formula to the costs above. Its first row shows the win rate a bracket achieves on pure noise.

| Breakeven win rate | 1:1 | 2:2 | 4:4 | Target 1 / Stop 4 | Target 4 / Stop 2 | Target 1 / Stop 8 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Random-walk win rate, S/(T+S) | 50.0% | 50.0% | 50.0% | 80.0% | 33.3% | 88.9% |
| ES, fees only (limit orders in and out) | 65.1% | 57.6% | 53.8% | 86.0% | 38.4% | 92.2% |
| ES, fees plus spread (market orders) | >100% | 82.6% | 66.3% | >100% | 55.0% | >100% |
| MES, fees only | 98.8% | 74.4% | 62.2% | 99.5% | 49.6% | 99.7% |
| MES, fees plus spread | >100% | 99.4% | 74.7% | >100% | 66.3% | >100% |
| NQ, fees only | 87.8% | 68.9% | 59.5% | 95.1% | 45.9% | 97.3% |
| MNQ, fees only | >100% | >100% | 80.5% | >100% | 74.0% | >100% |

The most important lesson about scalping follows from this formula. If prices follow a random walk with no drift, a bracket hits its target first with probability S/(T + S). Substituting that into the expectancy gives **E = −C exactly**. A 1-tick target with an 8-tick stop wins 88.9% of the time on pure noise. An ES trader using that bracket needs 92.2% just to cover fees, and 93.0% if the stop slips one tick. A high win rate is a mechanical result of where the bracket sits, not evidence of skill. Only the excess win rate above S/(T + S) pays, and that excess has to exceed C/(T + S).

Detecting that excess is statistically expensive. Telling a true 60% win rate apart from the 57.6% breakeven of an ES 2:2 bracket at two standard errors takes about 1,600 independent trades, and at 60% the Sharpe ratio per trade is only about 0.05. Ruin calculations look reassuring only if the edge is known. If the true win rate is 57% rather than an estimated 60%, expectancy is negative and ruin is eventually certain, whatever the account size.

### Required forecast accuracy explodes as the holding period shrinks

Costs are fixed per trade, but the standard deviation of the price move over a holding period h grows like √h. Call a forecast's correlation with the move that follows its IC (information coefficient). A directional forecast earns roughly IC × 0.8σ_h per trade, so it covers costs only if IC > C/(0.8σ_h). The thresholds below are for ES, assuming 1% daily S&P volatility, a random walk and an even 6.5-hour session.

| Holding horizon | Typical move σ (ES ticks) | Market-order cost as % of σ | IC needed, market orders | IC needed, limit orders |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 second | 2.0 | 65% | 0.81 | 0.19 |
| 10 seconds | 6.4 | 20% | 0.26 | 0.06 |
| 1 minute | 15.6 | 8.3% | 0.10 | 0.024 |
| 5 minutes | 34.9 | 3.7% | 0.047 | 0.011 |
| 15 minutes | 60.4 | 2.2% | 0.027 | 0.006 |
| 1 hour | 120.8 | 1.1% | 0.013 | 0.003 |
| 1 day | 308 | 0.4% | 0.005 | 0.001 |

Published research shows what size of correlation actually exists. The best short-horizon microstructure signal, order-flow imbalance, explains about **3% of the mid-price change over the next 200 ms**, with ideal data and no latency ([Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov slides](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Kukanov.pdf)). That is an IC near 0.17, at a horizon where market-order execution needs about 0.8. The best-documented slower signal, market intraday momentum, explains 1–2% of the variance in the last half-hour's return ([Gao, Han, Li & Zhou](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)). That is an IC near 0.1–0.14, at a horizon where the threshold is about 0.02. Real signals with small ICs cover costs at long horizons and fail at short ones. The limit-order column is optimistic, because passive fills are adversely selected, as shown above.

Grinold's fundamental law, IR ≈ IC × √breadth, is the formal case for many small bets, where IR is the information ratio and breadth the number of independent bets. It holds within its assumptions ([New Frontier Advisors](https://newfrontieradvisors.com/media/1744/fundamental-law-september-2019.pdf)). An IC of 0.02 on one bet a day gives an information ratio of about 0.32. The same IC on 100 independent bets a day gives about 3.2. But Clarke, de Silva and Thorley show two catches. Effective breadth for correlated bets is N/[1 + (N − 1)ρ], where ρ is the average correlation between bets. And implementation frictions add a "transfer coefficient" below one ([Clarke, de Silva & Thorley](https://joim.com/wp-content/uploads/emember/downloads/p0158.pdf)). Suppose those 100 bets have a pairwise correlation of 0.2, which is typical when intraday index signals all track the same market move. Effective breadth then shrinks to about 4.8 bets a day, and the information ratio falls to about 0.70.

Meanwhile, costs grow linearly with the number of bets, while gross IR grows only with its square root. With gross edge e, per-trade volatility s, cost c and n trades a year, net Sharpe ≈ ((e − c)/s)·√n. At e = 2 bp, s = 20 bp and n = 2,500, a gross Sharpe of 5.0 becomes 2.5 after 1 bp of cost and zero after 2 bp. A sound design rule is to keep cost at or below half the expected edge, and to stress-test at 1.5–2 times estimated costs.

### Leverage and 0DTE convexity amplify costs along with profits

Leverage multiplies profit, loss and cost by the same factor, so it leaves expectancy per unit of risk, E/σ, unchanged. It only raises the chance of hitting a fixed loss limit. The appeal of 0DTE options is different: convexity, meaning a position whose delta grows as the price moves in its favor. One at-the-money SPX 0DTE option has a delta of about 0.5 × $100 = $50 per index point, the same exposure as one ES contract, while risking only the premium. Late in the day its gamma is about three times its morning level. Working from the median premium path below, a 2-point SPX move at 15:30 changes an ATM option's premium by about 17%, against about 5.5% at 10:00.

The cost comes in three parts. The first is the spread plus fees. The median ATM SPXW spread from January 2022 to August 2026 was **0.20 index points ($20 per contract)** ([Concretum Research](https://concretumgroup.substack.com/p/spx-0dte-options)). Cboe charges customers $0.45 plus a $0.14 SPXW surcharge ([Cboe Fees Schedule](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/cboe/2026/34-105669-ex5.pdf)). Add a typical $0.65 commission, and a round trip that crosses the spread costs about $22.50. That equals 0.45 SPX points of movement in delta terms, against 0.33 points for ES with market orders.

The second part is time decay (theta). The median ATM premium falls from about 24 bp of the strike price at 10:00 to 8 bp at 15:30. At a 7,700 strike that comes to roughly **$231 an hour from 10:00 to noon, $192 from noon to 14:00 and $257 from 14:00 to 15:30** per contract, so a 15-minute hold needs about 1.6 points of movement just to cover theta. The third part is that the market charges a premium for convexity. The table below is a Black-Scholes illustration, not market data. It assumes SPX at 6,000, an ATM call bought with four hours left at 15% implied volatility (about $17.70), and zero interest rates.

| Outcome | ATM 0DTE call P&L | One ES contract P&L |
|---|---|---|
| +5 points within 15 minutes | about +$206 | +$250 |
| −5 points within 15 minutes | about −$295 | −$250 |
| Flat for one hour | about −$238 | $0 |
| +5 points, but after one hour | about +$26 | +$250 |
| +20 points within 15 minutes | about +$1,128 | +$1,000 |

Under Black-Scholes, gamma profits beat theta only if the realized move over the holding window is larger than about σ_implied × S × √Δt. That is roughly **22 SPX points per hour of session time at 15% implied volatility with SPX at 6,000**. Implied volatility usually exceeds what is then realized. Since 2004, the VIX-implied daily move has exceeded the S&P 500's actual absolute daily move about 80% of the time ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2020/06/08/fear-and-volatility-1)), and VIX1D significantly overestimates the volatility that follows ([TU Dresden](https://fis.tu-dresden.de/portal/en/publications/a-new-star-is-born-does-the-vix1d-render-common-volatility-forecasting-models-for-the-us-equity-market-obsolete%28cdf81654-bf7d-41b6-a165-af0168c8b016%29.html)). Buying 0DTE options to scalp 3–5-point moves is a structurally losing trade unless the trader can forecast realized volatility above implied. Simple 0DTE structures entered at 10:00 and held to the close, with no further conditions, had negative Sharpe ratios after costs from 2016 to January 2026: −0.51 for straddles and −0.96 for iron butterflies and condors (Vilkov, via [FirmTape](https://firmtape.com/research/what-the-0dte-literature-claims)).

SPY and XSP options cut the size by ten but not the friction. A one-cent spread plus $0.65 per side costs $2.30 per round trip, which is 15% of the premium on a $0.15 option. Taxes do favor index products. Futures and broad-based index options such as SPX and XSP are Section 1256 contracts: gains are taxed 60% as long-term and 40% as short-term, about 26.8% at the top blended federal rate, against 37% for SPY and QQQ options ([TaxSlayer Pro](https://support.taxslayerpro.com/hc/en-us/articles/360040712794); [Beancount](https://beancount.io/blog/2026/05/14/section-1256-contracts-60-40-tax-treatment-futures-broad-based-index-options-regulated-forex-form-6781-mark-to-market-traders-guide)).

### Population data show who wins at small horizons

It is clear who wins at short horizons. In two years of E-mini data, high-frequency traders earned a median Sharpe ratio of 4.3. Revenue was concentrated in a few firms, new entrants were less profitable and more likely to exit, and "differences in relative latency account for large differences" in performance ([Baron et al., CFTC/JFQA](https://www.cftc.gov/idc/groups/public/@economicanalysis/documents/file/oce_riskandreturn0414.pdf)). An earlier draft found that small traders "suffer the highest loss to HFTs on a per contract basis" ([Sethi summary](https://rajivsethi.substack.com/p/risk-and-reward-in-high-frequency-12-12-07)).

It is just as clear who loses. Of 19,646 Brazilians who began day-trading mini-index futures in 2013–2015, 1,551 kept going for more than 300 days. **97% of them lost money**, only 1.1% earned more than the minimum wage, and there was no sign that they learned ([Chague, De-Losso & Giovannetti](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=3423101)). In complete Taiwanese data from 1992 to 2006, fewer than 1% of day traders were predictably profitable after fees. The top 500 earned 37.9 bps a day after fees in the following year. They did it by trading aggressively, with 71% of their orders marketable, on short-term forecasts in a few stocks, not by providing liquidity ([Barber, Lee, Liu & Odean](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/Day%20Trading%20Skill%20110523.pdf)). More than three-quarters quit within two years, yet experienced losers kept trading at nearly the same rate as winners, 95.3% against 96.4% ([Barber et al., on learning](https://faculty.haas.berkeley.edu/odean/papers/day%20traders/day%20trading%20and%20learning%20110217.pdf)).

In 0DTE, retail traders lost about **$358,000 a day after daily expirations began in May 2022, and more than 60% of that was transaction costs** ([CXO summary of Beckmeyer, Branger & Gayda](https://cxoadvisory.com/individual-investing/retail-0dte-option-trader-performance)). Trader-level journal data for 2020–2022 show 0DTE trades losing 4.6% per trade, while option *selling* earned 20% ([Bogousslavsky & Muravyev](https://www.lsu.edu/business/files/event-files/2025-finance-mardi-gras/retail_option_trading_v2.pdf)). Regulators elsewhere report the same pattern. 74–89% of EU retail CFD accounts lose money ([ESMA](https://www.esma.europa.eu/node/84933)), and over 91% of Indian individual equity-derivatives traders lost money in FY25 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/markets/news/net-losses-of-traders-in-fo-widens-in-fy25-sebi-study-125070701221_1.html)).

Prop-firm evaluations are no shortcut. Topstep's 2025 disclosure shows 33.3% of traders who reached the funded level received any payout, and 0.71% reached a live account ([Topstep](https://www.topstep.com/risk-disclosure)). Vendor data across ten firms put the share of traders ever paid at about 7% ([Finance Magnates](https://www.financemagnates.com/forex/exclusive-only-7-of-300000-prop-trading-accounts-achieved-payouts/)). Profitable US day traders in older data relied on market-structure edges that no longer exist: trading against stale Nasdaq dealer quotes ([Harris & Schultz](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v50y1998i1p39-62.html)), and posting limit orders inside dealer spreads on ECNs ([Garvey & Murphy](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v12y2005i5p629-649.html)). No US study comparable to the Brazilian or Taiwanese datasets exists for retail ES and MES traders, which is the biggest gap in this evidence.

### The verdict: right about breadth, wrong about horizon and instrument

The premise deserves its strongest case. Breadth does raise risk-adjusted returns. High-frequency firms do make money on huge numbers of tiny trades. And short-horizon order flow is more predictable than daily direction, because the signs of successive trades have long memory. Even so, four findings break the premise as stated.

First, the fixed cost per trade grows relative to the typical move as the horizon shrinks, so the forecast accuracy needed rises from about 0.01 at one hour to about 0.8 at one second. Second, the short-horizon predictability that does exist (persistent order flow, queue imbalance, lead-lag between venues) is harvested by firms reacting in microseconds, three to five orders of magnitude before a retail system sees it. Their edge comes from speed, queue priority and rebates, not from predicting with public data. Third, breadth collapses when bets share a common driver: a hundred intraday index trades are worth about five independent bets. Fourth, leverage and option convexity scale costs along with profits. Micros cost a tick or more in fees alone, and buying 0DTE premium means paying theta, which is usually a losing trade because implied volatility typically exceeds realized.

The defensible version of the premise is to make **many modest bets, each held long enough that round-trip cost is only a few percent of the typical move** (about 4% at five minutes in ES with market orders), each conditioned so the bets are as independent of one another as possible, and each with an expected edge of at least twice its cost. They should be placed in the cheapest instruments: ES rather than MES or MNQ where capital allows, SPY rather than single stocks for equity-index exposure, and SPX rather than SPY for options.

## Order flow explains the present far better than it predicts the next minute

### Futures sign trades exactly; equities guess

"Order flow" covers several distinct measurements. Signed trade volume is aggressive buys minus aggressive sells, which retail platforms show as "delta" and "cumulative volume delta" (CVD). Order-flow imbalance (OFI) is the net change in the queues at the best bid and offer, and multi-level imbalance extends the same idea deeper into the book. Static queue imbalance compares the bid and ask queue sizes at one moment, and volume-clock "toxicity" measures such as VPIN, covered below, track imbalance over buckets of equal volume. CME publishes which side was the aggressor directly. The MDP 3.0 Trade Summary message carries tag 5797, with values for buy, sell or no aggressor. "No aggressor" occurs at the open, after pauses, and when customer orders trade against implied liquidity ([CME Client Systems Wiki](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/spaces/EPICSANDBOX/pages/457227733)).

In US equities the side has to be inferred, and accuracy against true signs is mediocre. On Nasdaq trades, the tick rule gets 78.62% right. Lee-Ready gets 86.88% with one-second timestamps and 93.57% with millisecond timestamps ([Carrion & Kolay](https://doi.org/10.1111/fire.12218)). Bulk volume classification does worse than the tick rule on equities, at 64.3% with one-second bars ([Chakrabarty, Pascual & Shkilko](https://dee.uib.eu/digitalAssets/234/234006_Pascual.pdf)). Even in futures, applying the tick rule to aggregated bars degrades quickly. Misclassification is 24.8% at one-second aggregation and 43.1% at 300 seconds, "barely improving on random" ([Andersen & Bondarenko](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_43.pdf)). Odd lots made up a median 24% of trades and 35% of price discovery in O'Hara, Yao and Ye's sample, and were then missing from the consolidated tape entirely ([O'Hara, Yao & Ye](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12185)).

Signed-flow features built from consolidated equity data therefore carry roughly 7–23% sign error per trade, which weakens any measured relationship. Direct feeds such as ITCH avoid the problem, because they show the side of the resting order that was hit.

### Imbalance explains most of a price change only after it has happened

Cont, Kukanov and Stoikov define OFI as the net change in the bid and ask queue sizes from limit orders, cancellations and trades at the best quotes. A market sell and a cancelled bid count the same, because both shrink the bid queue ([Cont, Kukanov & Stoikov](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.6402v3)). Over 10-second intervals in 50 S&P 500 stocks, mid-price changes are linear in OFI, with **an average R² of 65%**. The slope is inversely proportional to depth, and deeper levels play only a second-order role. Combining several levels into an "integrated" OFI pushes the in-sample R² for the same interval to **87%** ([Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2023.2236159)).

Prediction is a different matter. Regressing the mid change over the 200 ms after each execution on OFI over the 200 ms before it gives an **average R² of 2.93%**, a little stronger at 50–100 ms ([CKS slides](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2016/05/Kukanov.pdf)). Order-book event correlations "typically vanish after 10 seconds" ([CKS](https://arxiv.org/html/1011.6402v3)). Lagged OFI from other assets improves one-minute-ahead forecasts, but that predictability "decays quickly" ([Cont, Cucuringu & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.13213)). Deep models fed order-flow inputs beat models fed raw book snapshots, the architecture matters little, and the forecastable signal peaks about two average price changes ahead ([Kolm, Turiel & Westray](https://doi.org/10.1111/mafi.12413)). In one-second E-mini data, order-flow shocks "dissipate almost entirely within a second" ([Takahashi](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.06788v4)).

None of these papers reports results after spread, fees and latency. The headline R² values describe how prices are formed, not what a slow trader can forecast. Realistic uses of OFI are execution timing (whether to post or cross, rest or cancel) and minute-scale features drawn from related assets.

### Queue imbalance predicts the next tick, which costs a tick to capture

Best-level queue imbalance is I = (bid size − ask size)/(bid size + ask size). It is a strong predictor of the direction of the next mid-price move. Across ten Nasdaq stocks, out-of-sample AUC was **0.75–0.81 for large-tick stocks and 0.58–0.64 for small-tick stocks**. When imbalance neared one, the probability of an up-move reached 0.8–0.9 in large-tick names ([Gould & Bonart](https://arxiv.org/html/1512.03492)). Deep-learning work across tick-size classes confirms accuracy above 0.7, rising above 0.9 when only high-probability predictions are kept. Yet the same work concludes that "high forecasting power does not necessarily correspond to actionable trading signals" ([Briola, Bartolucci & Aste](https://www.tandfonline.com/doi/full/10.1080/14697688.2025.2522911)).

The tension is built in. Imbalance is most predictive exactly where the tick is large, so the predicted move is one tick, and crossing the spread to capture it costs one tick plus fees. Its value lies in managing passive orders: don't rest on the thin side, and stay in the queue on the thick side. The micro-price works best as a fair-value mark, not as a signal on its own. ES should behave like Gould and Bonart's large-tick stocks, though no peer-reviewed ES measurement was found.

### VPIN failed its independent tests, and footprint tools were never tested

VPIN is an absolute order imbalance measured over buckets of equal volume. Its authors promoted it as a real-time "toxicity" gauge that peaked before the 2010 flash crash ([Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhs053)). It did not survive independent scrutiny. Andersen and Bondarenko show it peaked after the crash, not before, and that its predictive content comes mechanically from trading intensity. With accurate trade classification it "behaves in a diametrically opposite way". Once volume and volatility are controlled for, it adds no predictive power ([Andersen & Bondarenko, JFM 2014](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S1386418113000189); [CREATES RP 2013-43](https://repec.econ.au.dk/repec/creates/rp/13/rp13_43.pdf)). An independent equity study also favored tick-rule versions over bulk-classified ones ([Chakrabarty et al.](https://dee.uib.eu/digitalAssets/234/234006_Pascual.pdf)).

The practitioner toolkit (footprint charts, delta and CVD, "absorption", "exhaustion", stacked imbalances, DOM heatmaps) displays signed trade flow and visible depth. Vendors claim, for example, that absorption "often precedes a reversal", while also warning that "a bright zone on the map is a hypothesis, not a fact" ([ATAS](https://help.atas.net/en/support/solutions/articles/72000660059-heatmap); [Bookmap](https://bookmap.com/blog/how-cumulative-volume-delta-transform-your-trading-strategy)). **No peer-reviewed, out-of-sample, after-cost test of these rules was found.** The related evidence is unfavorable. CVD uses trades only, and imbalance built from trades alone is a noisier driver of price than OFI ([CKS](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/12/1/47/816163)). "Absorption" matches the CKS observation that high OFI without a price change comes before a move, but that is a sub-second effect with an R² near 3%. These tools generate hypotheses; they are not proven edges.

### Displayed depth lies through icebergs and spoofing

Displayed size misstates real supply in both directions. In 2011, iceberg orders were about **9% of E-mini book volume but matched 12% of traded volume**, and the average iceberg's total size was 12 times the average order ([Christensen & Woodmansey](https://doi.org/10.3905/jot.2013.8.3.068)). Exchange-managed icebergs can be detected with certainty in MBO data, because their order IDs persist; synthetic icebergs run by trading platforms can only be inferred ([arXiv 1909.09495](https://arxiv.org/html/1909.09495v1)).

Spoofing distorts depth the other way. Navinder Sarao's layered E-mini sell orders sat three to four levels from the best price. On May 6, 2010 they were modified more than 81,000 times, only 81 contracts were executed, and they made up **20–29% of the entire sell side of the book** ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/7486-16)). JPMorgan's record $920.2 million settlement described genuine orders filling milliseconds after layered spoof orders, which were cancelled within a second ([CFTC](https://www.cftc.gov/PressRoom/PressReleases/8260-20)).

The layer of the book that heatmap "walls" and deep-book imbalance read is exactly the layer spoofers manipulate, and the fills and cancels happen too fast for a human to tell apart in real time. Information close to the best price is both more predictive and harder to fake. Weighting displayed size by order age, using MBO order IDs, is the principled countermeasure. That is an inference; no published test was found.

### Deep learning on the book collapses on fresh data

DeepLOB, a convolutional-plus-LSTM network applied to raw order-book snapshots ([Zhang, Zohren & Roberts](https://arxiv.org/abs/1808.03668)), reported an F1 score of 78.9 on the FI-2010 benchmark dataset. The LOBCAST benchmark tested 15 models. It reproduced DeepLOB at 71.4, measured **59.5 on fresh 2021–2022 Nasdaq data**, and found every model falling to 48–61% ([LOBCAST](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.01915v2)). LOBCAST also found three problems with FI-2010: it is pre-normalized, it cannot be turned back into a raw book, and its labels were chosen to balance classes rather than for financial meaning. Its backtests show profitability "far from guaranteed", and no study found shows deep order-book predictions surviving realistic costs. Since most of the gain comes from stable order-flow features rather than the architecture ([Kolm et al.](https://doi.org/10.1111/mafi.12413)), linear or gradient-boosted models on OFI-type features are the right baseline.

### Big prints and retail flow are slow signals

The idea that "big print equals smart money" is not supported. In NYSE audit-trail data, medium-sized trades produced about 79% of cumulative price change, while large trades produced about 25% ([Chakravarty](https://econwpa.ub.uni-muenchen.de/econ-wp/fin/papers/0201/0201003.pdf)). Trades in round sizes have more impact ([Alexander & Peterson](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/223/An%20analysis%20of%20trade-size%20clustering%20and%20its%20relation%20to%20stealth%20trading.pdf)), and informed traders hide in odd lots ([O'Hara, Yao & Ye](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.12185)).

Retail order imbalance, identified from off-exchange prints with sub-penny price improvement, predicts about **10 bps of outperformance over the following week** ([Boehmer, Jones, Zhang & Zhang](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/jofi.13033)), which makes it a multi-day signal. A field test using 85,000 known retail trades found that method identifies only 35% of retail trades and gets the direction wrong on 28%. A fix based on the quote midpoint reaches about 95% accuracy ([Barber et al. 2024](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/full/10.1111/jofi.13334)). No rigorous evidence was found for intraday "unusual volume" or dark-print alerts.

### Doing this properly requires order-level data

Research on queues and OFI needs L3 data, or at least L2, with exchange timestamps. Databento's December 2024 price sheet put historical CME MBO data at about $1.80 per GB and MBP-10 at $0.50 per GB ([Databento unit pricing](http://databento-media.s3-website-us-east-1.amazonaws.com/media/unit-pricing-2024-12-11.pdf)). Live CME data moved to subscription plans, with the Standard plan at **$199 a month from June 22, 2026** ([Databento](https://databento.com/blog/updates-to-subscription-pricing)). For equities, Nasdaq TotalView-ITCH is Databento's only real-time L2/L3 feed ([Databento equities](https://databento.com/equities)). LOBSTER's academic reconstruction of the Nasdaq book costs £7,499 a year per institution ([LOBSTER](https://data.lobsterdata.com/info/docs/legal/LOBSTER_priceList.pdf)). Massive (formerly Polygon) publishes trades and NBBO quotes but no depth for any asset class ([Massive](https://massive.com/knowledge-base/article/does-massive-offer-level-2-data)). One-minute bars cannot evaluate tick-level scalping at all.

## Dealer gamma sets the volatility regime, not price levels

### Hedging flows dampen or amplify depending on the dealer's sign

An options market maker who hedges delta trades against the market when net long gamma, buying dips and selling rallies. When net short gamma, the market maker trades with the market. How strong the effect is depends on the size of dealer gamma relative to liquidity in the underlying, and on whether dealers actually rebalance ([Amaya, Garcia-Ares, Pearson & Vasquez](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf)). In theory, hedging moves prices only if the underlying market cannot absorb the hedges without price changes ([Barbon & Buraschi](https://abarbon.com/papers/gamma-fragility)). Gamma peaks at expiration, which makes 0DTE options "the single most potent source of intraday variation in market makers' hedging needs" ([Adams et al. 2026](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)).

Vendors have built a vocabulary on top of this sound mechanism. "Call walls" are the strikes with the largest call gamma, described as resistance and magnets. "Put walls" are described as support. There are also "zero-gamma flips", and OPEX windows said to be driven by vanna and charm (how delta changes with implied volatility and with time). SpotGamma itself calls put-wall support "conditional" and warns that walls "can give way quickly" in deeply negative gamma ([SpotGamma](https://spotgamma.com/call-wall-put-wall-explained/)). Practitioners describe vanna and charm flows as "estimates, not measurements" ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/vanna-charm-flows/)). The math of vanna and charm is real, since delta does depend on implied volatility and time. But whether those flows move prices depends on dealer inventory that nobody outside the exchange can see.

Pinning is the best-documented effect tied to specific price levels. In 1996–2002 data, optionable stocks' closing prices clustered at strike prices on expiration days, shifting returns by at least 16.5 bp per expiration ([Ni, Pearson & Poteshman](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jfinec/v78y2005i1p49-87.html)). 13.56% of S&P futures settled within $0.25 of a strike at serial expirations, against 10% expected by chance (Golez & Jackwerth, via [FirmTape](https://firmtape.com/research/what-the-0dte-literature-claims)). No modern estimate specific to SPX was found.

### Public GEX gets the sign wrong where it matters most

The standard gamma-exposure (GEX) formula sums gamma × open interest × 100 across strikes. Calls count positive and puts negative, on the assumption that dealers are long all calls, short all puts, and hedge exactly to delta ([SqueezeMetrics](https://slopeofhope.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/11/GEX-white_paper.pdf)). A common dollar version multiplies by S² × 0.01 to express the hedging required per 1% move.

Two of the inputs are not observed. Open interest says nothing about who holds a contract, and OCC reports it only at the end of the day ([FlashAlpha](https://flashalpha.com/articles/0dte-gamma-exposure-pin-risk-intraday-options-analytics)). The best ground truth is Cboe trade records coded by whether the trader is a customer or a market maker. They show that SPX market makers' net gamma was **positive in 88% of 10-minute intervals from 2018 to 2024**. They also show that customers start each day **short at-the-money 0DTE straddles and long far out-of-the-money wings**, the shape of an iron condor or butterfly ([Adams et al. 2026](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)). The naive convention therefore gets the sign wrong at the heaviest strikes nearest the current price on expiration days. That makes naive "flip" levels near spot especially unreliable.

Stale open interest matters less than often claimed. Most dealer 0DTE gamma comes from positions built before expiration day: 0.39% of S&P 500 shares per 1% move, against 0.16% for positions opened the same day ([Adams et al.](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)). Calculations based only on SPX also miss offsetting positions in SPY options, ES options and over-the-counter structures ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/volatility-insights-evaluating-the-market-impact-of-spx-0-dte-options)). A homemade GEX is a different variable from the trader-type-coded gamma used in the strongest academic studies, so their findings do not automatically carry over.

### The best evidence: hedging usually dampens intraday volatility, modestly

The most careful recent study is a July 2026 draft that merges work by two research teams. It uses the days before 2022 when Tuesday and Thursday expirations did not yet exist as a natural experiment. On days when 0DTEs expired, **10-minute S&P 500 realized volatility was 61 bp lower (annualized), about 7% of its 9.10% average**, and each extra basis point of hedging against the trend went with about 4 bp lower realized volatility. A one-standard-deviation rise in 0DTE net gamma lowers volatility over the next 10 minutes by about 23 bp annualized, an effect that lasts about an hour. High dealer gamma also strengthens reversals of order flow for about 30 minutes, and hour-to-hour momentum turns into reversal when hedging needs are high ([Adams, Dim, Eraker, Fontaine, Ornthanalai & Vilkov](https://www.jean-sebastienfontaine.com/papers/0dte-options-volatility.pdf)).

Amaya et al. find that volatility falls as dealer gamma in the previous interval rises. The median effect is only −0.08 percentage points of daily volatility, but it reaches **+3.3 points of annualized daily volatility and +6.4 points of 30-minute volatility when dealer gamma is negative** ([Amaya et al.](https://cdn.cboe.com/resources/education/research_publications/gammasqueezes.pdf)). The dissenting study is by Brogaard, Han and Won. They find that a one-standard-deviation rise in 0DTE trading raises volatility by about 9.1% of its average, and attribute it to speculative retail trading ([SSRN 4426358](https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=4426358)). Adams et al. dispute how they identify the effect. Cboe, which is not neutral, puts net dealer gamma hedging at "at best, just 0.2% of the SPX daily liquidity" ([Cboe, May 2025](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)).

The most tradable result comes from before daily expirations existed. Across 60+ futures markets from 1974 to 2020, the return over the rest of the day predicts the return in the last 30 minutes. In S&P 500 futures, the effect exists only when the previous day's naive dealer gamma is negative (coefficient 6.63, t = 4.78) and vanishes when it is positive (coefficient 0.82, t = 1.03). A timing strategy earned a Sharpe of 1.73 before costs and stayed positive with a one-tick cost ([Baltussen, Da, Lammers & Martens](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). Three caveats limit this. It uses the naive sign convention, its sample ends in May 2020, and Adams et al. find reversal rather than momentum when hedging needs are high in 2018–2024. Whether it persisted after 2022 is not established.

The consensus as of October 2026 has three parts. Dealer hedging on average dampens intraday volatility, because customers sell at-the-money premium. Negative-gamma states are a minority of episodes and raise volatility and momentum. And the effects are small enough that gamma is a **variable describing the volatility regime, with modest explanatory power, not a generator of price targets**.

### 0DTE in 2026: dominant, tighter, still costly for buyers

0DTE options made up about 59% of SPX volume in 2025, at 2.3 million contracts a day (Cboe, via [Mondo Visione](https://m.mondovisione.com/news/cboe-global-markets-reports-trading-volume-for-december-and-full-year-2025-202616)). Their share hit a record 66.2% in July 2026 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/market-metrics-that-matter-derivatives-july-2026-volume-highlights)), and volume hit a record 3.4 million contracts a day in September 2026 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/market-metrics-that-matter-derivatives-september-2026-volume-highlights)). Daily expirations now exist for SPX/SPXW (completed in 2022), for SPY and QQQ (from November 2022) ([MIAX](https://miaxglobal.com/alert/2022/11/14/miax-exchange-group-options-markets-listing-tuesday-and-thursday-weekly)), and for XSP, the one-tenth-size, cash-settled SPX option ([Cboe XSP specs](https://www.cboe.com/tradable_products/sp_500/mini_spx_options/specifications)). In January 2026 the SEC approved Monday and Wednesday expiries for the largest single stocks ([Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2026-01-16/mag-7-stock-options-get-sec-nod-for-monday-wednesday-expiries)).

Estimates of who trades 0DTE conflict. Cboe estimates retail at 50–60% of SPX 0DTE volume ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)), while one academic footnote implies about 94% is institutional ([Almeida, Freire & Hizmeri](https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Almeida.pdf)). SEC economists explain the gap: a large volume of retail-sized orders arrives as non-marketable limit orders resting on the book, not through price-improvement auctions ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf)). Over 95% of 0DTE trades have limited risk, and only 4% are naked short positions ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/0-dt-es-decoded-positioning-trends-and-market-impact)).

Costs have fallen sharply. The at-the-money SPXW quote was one tick wide only 0.5% of the time in July 2020, but 64.7% of the time in July 2023. Customer limit orders at the midpoint filled 50–62% of the time. Trying a limit order first cost about $0.021–0.028 all-in, against $0.05 for a marketable order ([SEC DERA](https://www.sec.gov/files/dera-hope-reasonable-prc-2503.pdf)). Spreads relative to price are narrowest between 10:00 and 14:00 and widest at the open and close ([Almeida et al.](https://www.fma.org/assets/docs/Derivatives2025/Almeida.pdf)). The same SEC paper shows 89.3% of OPRA trades in July 2020 carried quote timestamps out of sequence. That data error biases the cost estimates in earlier studies of retail losses.

Even so, the 0DTE mispricings Almeida et al. documented before 2022 "dissipate after the daily availability of 0DTEs", and unconditional strategies lose money after costs. Any surviving edge should be expected to be conditional, small and limited in capacity.

### Mechanical flows concentrate at the close

Leveraged and inverse ETFs must rebalance in the direction of the day's move, near the close. The required trade is about L(L−1) × daily return × assets, where L is the leverage multiple, so it grows with both leverage and the size of the move ([Baltussen et al.](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/intramom.pdf)). Estimates have grown from about $1.04 billion per 1% S&P move in 2011 ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201348/)) to almost $7 billion per 1% move, as leveraged-ETF assets hit $117 billion in 2024 (Morgan Stanley, via [Bloomberg](https://www.itiger.com/news/2466019752)). Combined with negative dealer gamma, this is the mechanical setup for continuation in the last hour on big-move days.

Volatility-control and CTA flows are larger but slower. Nomura estimated about $108 billion of net equity selling from early March to early April 2026, from a pool above $1 trillion ([Kitco/Reuters](https://www.kitco.com/news/off-the-wire/2026-04-08/volatility-linked-funds-put-march-us-stock-selling-spree-rearview)). These flows play out over several days and are estimated with models, so they cannot be timed intraday. The end-of-day reversal in single stocks, 3.78–6.86 bp a day, "cannot be explained by liquidity- or gamma-hedging effects" ([Baltussen, Da & Soebhag](https://academicweb.nd.edu/~zda/EOD.pdf)). Everyone knows these flows exist, so any edge has to come from better estimates of their size and timing.

### Using gamma in an algorithm

The evidence supports using gamma as a regime input to sizing and strategy choice. When dealer gamma is strongly positive, favor fading moves, mean reversion and premium selling. When it is negative, favor breakout and momentum logic, with smaller size and wider stops. The evidence does not support call walls, put walls or zero-gamma flips as hard entry levels. Confirmation bias is the known trap: walls "work" on quiet days, which are most days, and fail on the large-move days that dominate P&L.

If you compute GEX from public open interest, check its sign against an independent proxy. One option is the realized autocorrelation of 5–10-minute returns, which should be negative in true long-gamma states. And treat the 0DTE market mainly as a source of information (expected move, event pricing, regime) rather than as the instrument for small directional scalps. For those, ES is more efficient.

## VWAP, volume and indicators work as state variables, not triggers

### One formula with many windows

Every VWAP variant computes Σ(P·V)/ΣV. They differ only in the time window, the price used and which trades are included. **Session VWAP** resets at the open and, on bars, usually uses the typical price (H + L + C)/3 ([StockCharts](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/volume-weighted-average-price-vwap.md)). Institutional definitions distinguish full VWAP, VWAP excluding your own trades, non-block VWAP (excluding trades of 10,000 or more shares) and OHLC approximations ([Madhavan](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)). **Anchored VWAP**, popularized by Brian Shannon, starts the sum at a chosen event such as a swing high or low, an earnings release or a gap ([StockCharts AVWAP](https://chartschool.stockcharts.com/table-of-contents/technical-indicators-and-overlays/technical-overlays/anchored-vwap.md)). Weekly and monthly VWAPs are simply anchored VWAPs that start at the beginning of the period, rolling VWAP and VWMA use a fixed lookback, and "buyer" and "seller" VWAPs split trades by aggressor side, so they need trade classification. Standard-deviation bands use the volume-weighted variance σ² = ΣV(P − VWAP)²/ΣV, which can be computed from running sums. The bands are unstable early in the session ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/indicators/vwap-standard-deviation-bands)). Futures have at least three defensible daily VWAPs: one for the Globex session from 6:00 pm ET, one for regular hours from 9:30 ET, and one anchored at settlement. ES's own settlement price is a 30-second VWAP ([CME](https://cmegroupclientsite.atlassian.net/wiki/x/E4DaBw)).

Clean data matter more than the formula. Odd lots count in consolidated volume but not in a bar's high and low ([SEC 34-70898](https://www.sec.gov/files/rules/sro/nyse/2013/34-70898.pdf)), so a VWAP built from every trade can sit outside a bar's displayed range ([Alpaca forum](https://forum.alpaca.markets/t/historical-vwap-data-very-wrong/14272/2)). With more than half of volume trading off-exchange, consolidated volume is the only valid input. A VWAP used for signals should exclude auction prints, late-reported trades and block trades priced at VWAP, and multi-day anchors across futures rolls need back-adjusted prices.

### The benchmark creates the flows the signal tries to exploit

VWAP became the dominant execution benchmark because it gives an unbiased estimate of the price a non-strategic trader faces during the day ([Berkowitz, Logue & Noser](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.1988.tb02591.x)). Madhavan warns that its "uncritical use ... can promote trading behavior that actually increases costs and risk", and that it can be gamed ([Madhavan](https://www.smallake.kr/wp-content/uploads/2014/07/TP_Spring_2002_Madhavan.pdf)).

Algorithms measured against benchmarks leave footprints. ES volume clusters in the first seconds of each minute because VWAP and TWAP algorithms slice orders on the minute, and "a mildly sophisticated HFT algorithm" trades ahead of them. In 2010–2011, 51.56% of ES trades were for one contract ([Easley, López de Prado & O'Hara, "The Volume Clock"](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)). Equilibrium theory shows that TWAP and VWAP targets create predictable intraday price pressure ([Choi, Larsen & Seppi](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.08336)). Across stocks, returns continue at half-hour lags that are exact multiples of one trading day, and the pattern lasts about 40 days ([Heston, Korajczyk & Sadka](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1540-6261.2010.01573.x)).

Two plausible mechanisms follow, though neither has been measured. "VWAP-or-better" algorithms slow down above VWAP and speed up below it, which creates a soft pull back toward VWAP. Large one-sided programs spread over the day create a persistent drift that keeps price on one side of VWAP. Which one dominates on a given day depends on the net size of benchmarked flow relative to liquidity. That size is correlated with relative volume, gaps and news, which argues for a VWAP rule that depends on the regime rather than a single fixed rule.

### The best VWAP and breakout backtests are fragile to a cent of slippage

The best-known public test is Zarattini and Aziz's rule: long QQQ above session VWAP and short below, flipping on one-minute closes, flat overnight. It reported **a Sharpe of 2.1, a 671% total return and a 9.4% maximum drawdown from 2018 to September 2023** over 21,967 trades, with a 17% hit rate and an average gain-to-loss ratio of 5.67 ([Zarattini & Aziz](https://concretumgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Volume-Weighted-Average-Price.pdf)). It assumed zero slippage and a commission of $0.0005 a share, it had no out-of-sample period, and the authors run a research firm and a day-trading education business. A quick check shows how fragile this is. The strategy traded 13.09 million shares for about $167,700 of profit. Half a cent of slippage per share would cost about $65,000, or 39% of profit; a full cent would cost about $131,000, or 78%. A QuantConnect reproduction with matched settings got a Sharpe of 1.75, but other public replications reported "far lower numbers" ([QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/terminal/cache/embedded_backtest_b115d0894231d55994b1068202f6c0ae.html)). The profits came from a few big winners on trend days, in a sample that contains COVID and the 2022 bear market, so the rule should also be tested on 2010–2017 and 2024–2026.

The same research group's five-minute opening-range breakout on QQQ reported about 33% a year after commissions. An independent 2026 replication on index CFDs found a gross edge of +0.131R per trade on NQ, where R is the amount risked per trade. After spread and slippage that fell to **+0.002R (t = 0.0)**. The net result was negative in four of five markets, and negative in every market in 2015–2017 ([MQL5 replication](https://www.mql5.com/en/blogs/post/776235)). The more promising variant limits breakouts to "stocks in play": the 20 US stocks with the highest opening volume relative to normal, usually driven by news. It reports a Sharpe of 2.81 for 2016–2023, and a community recreation got about 2.4 ([Zarattini, Barbon & Aziz](https://ideas.repec.org/p/chf/rpseri/rp2498.html); [QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/forum/discussion/18444/opening-range-breakout-for-stocks-in-play/)). It has a plausible economic basis in post-news drift, but it has not been verified by peer review and is sensitive to fills in fast-moving news stocks. No peer-reviewed test was found of trading reversion toward VWAP or fading moves to ±2σ bands.

### Volume profile and volume oscillators lack intraday evidence

Market Profile was introduced by CBOT trader J. Peter Steidlmayer in 1985. It organizes trading activity into a point of control (POC), the most-traded price, and a value area containing 70% of activity ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Market_profile); [CQG](https://help.cqg.com/cqgic/25/Documents/marketprofilevalueareasmpva.htm)). High- and low-volume nodes, and the "80% rule" for when price re-enters the previous value area, are standard practitioner lore without published statistics ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/concepts/80-percent-rule)). There is a plausible rationale: high-volume prices are where two-sided liquidity cleared. That makes distance to the POC and position relative to the previous value area legitimate features to test, against placebo levels placed at random.

The classic volume indicators (OBV, accumulation/distribution, Chaikin Money Flow, MFI, price-volume trend, Klinger, ease of movement, force index, PVO) are fixed formulas applied to OHLCV data. What they share is the signed return, a volume z-score and where the close sits in the bar's range, (2C − H − L)/(H − L). Rigorous evidence exists only at monthly horizons, where moving-average rules on OBV forecast the equity premium both in and out of sample ([Neely, Rapach, Tu & Zhou](https://doi.org/10.1287/mnsc.2013.1838)). The best-supported intraday volume feature is relative volume normalized by time of day: today's volume in a window divided by its average in the same window over previous days. The stocks-in-play work uses it as its universe filter.

### Volume predicts volatility well and direction only conditionally

The robust facts about volume concern volatility. Under the mixture-of-distributions hypothesis, volume and price variability are both driven by the arrival of information ([Clark](https://doi.org/10.2307/1913889); [Tauchen & Pitts](https://doi.org/10.2307/1912002); [Karpoff](https://doi.org/10.2307/2330874)). The number of trades, rather than their size, carries most of the volatility information ([Jones, Kaul & Lipson](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/7.4.631)).

For direction, volume changes the sign of autocorrelation. High-volume daily returns tend to reverse when trading is driven by liquidity needs ([Campbell, Grossman & Wang](https://doi.org/10.2307/2118454)) and to continue when trading is speculative ([Llorente, Michaely, Saar & Wang](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/15.4.1005)). SPY and ES are the most liquid instruments, with the least information asymmetry. For them this predicts that high-volume moves are more likely to reverse at daily horizons, the opposite of the folk rule that "volume confirms the move". This can coexist with the one-minute continuation relative to VWAP seen intraday. An intraday regression, r_{b+1} = c₀ + c₁r_b + c₂·RVOL_b·r_b, tests which pattern holds.

Volume itself can be forecast. Gradient-boosted models pooled across stocks reached an out-of-sample R² of 0.62 for 15-minute volume bins, against 0.27 for the classic CMEM decomposition ([Cucuringu, Li & Zhang](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.08180v1); [Brownlees, Cipollini & Gallo](https://doi.org/10.1093/jjfinec/nbq024)). Sampling in volume time removes most intraday seasonality and partly restores normal-looking returns ([The Volume Clock](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/the_volume_clock_-_insights_into_the_high_frequency_paradigm.pdf)). But an FX test found that these better statistical properties do not, on their own, produce better strategies ([MQL5](https://www.mql5.com/en/articles/23310)).

### Indicator rules die under data-snooping correction

RSI, stochastics, MACD, Bollinger %B, ATR, OBV and stochastic RSI are all nonlinear functions of the same price and volume history. Stochastic RSI applies the stochastic formula to RSI: %K = 100 × (RSI − min RSI)/(max RSI − min RSI) over a lookback window ([TA-Lib](https://ta-lib.org/functions/stochrsi/)).

Research on these indicators' predictive power follows a consistent arc. Brock, Lakonishok and LeBaron found that moving-average and range-break rules beat random-walk benchmarks on the Dow from 1897 to 1986 ([BLL 1992](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v47y1992i5p1731-64.html)). White's Reality Check test, which corrects for the number of rules tried, showed the best rule was insignificant in the following decade ([Sullivan, Timmermann & White](https://www.fmg.ac.uk/sites/default/files/2020-11/dp303.pdf)). Controlling the false-discovery rate showed investors "would never have been able to select ex ante the future best-performing rules", and that even in-sample performance "is completely offset by the introduction of low transaction costs" ([Bajgrowicz & Scaillet](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v106y2012i3p473-491.html)). Significant rules survive only in "young" markets such as the Russell 2000, not in the S&P 500 ([Hsu & Kuan](https://ideas.repec.org/p/sin/wpaper/04-a003.html)).

Intraday results are no better. Of **7,846 rules tested on 5-minute SPY data, 163 were significant at 5% before correcting for data snooping and none after** ([Marshall, Cahan & Cahan](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/empfin/v15y2008i2p199-210.html)). No candlestick rule beat buy-and-hold after costs on 5-minute Dow stocks ([Duvinage, Mazza & Petitjean](https://ideas.repec.org/a/taf/quantf/v13y2013i7p1059-1070.html)). In Nikkei stocks, intraday technical rules and book-imbalance rules showed some predictability, but none beat buy-and-hold ([Yamamoto](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v36y2012i11p3033-3047.html)). No intraday test of RSI or stochastic RSI on SPY or ES after 2010, corrected for data snooping, was found. The right use for indicators is as candidate features in a model, held to the same multiple-testing discipline that killed them as standalone rules.

### A handful of intraday regularities survive

| Pattern | Evidence quality | Horizon | Size | Status after costs and out of sample |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Market intraday momentum (first half-hour or rest-of-day predicts last half-hour) | Multiple peer-reviewed papers, many markets | Last 30 minutes | Out-of-sample R² ~1–2%, hit rate ~54% | Survives in SPY at ~1 bp spreads; depends on regime; weaker after 2012 |
| Gamma-conditioned momentum | Peer-reviewed (JFE 2021) | Last 30 minutes | Only when dealer gamma is negative | Sample ends 2020; persistence unknown |
| "Noise-area" breakout in SPY | Working paper by authors with commercial ties | Intraday | Net Sharpe 1.33 claimed | Not replicated |
| Stocks-in-play opening-range breakout | Working paper by authors with commercial ties, plus a recreation | Intraday | Backtest Sharpe 2.4–2.8 | Sensitive to slippage; unverified |
| Opening-range breakout on index ETF or future | Working paper plus independent replication | Intraday | ~0.13R gross | About zero after costs |
| VWAP cross on QQQ | Working paper by authors with commercial ties | Intraday | Sharpe 2.1 claimed | Assumed zero slippage; replications weaker |
| Post-news drift | Peer-reviewed (JFE 2021) | Hours to days | Large abnormal returns | Survives costs in single stocks |
| Intraday seasonality across stocks | Peer-reviewed (JF 2010) | Same half-hour on earlier days | Small | Useful for execution timing |
| Pre-FOMC drift | Peer-reviewed plus updates | 24 hours before the announcement | ~50 bp originally | Gone after 2015 |
| Standalone indicator rules | Peer-reviewed, corrected for snooping | Any | None after correction | Fail |

The strongest effect is **market intraday momentum**. SPY's return from the previous close through 10:00 predicts the return in the last half-hour, with an out-of-sample R² of about 1.2%. A timing strategy that traded only the final 30 minutes earned 6.67% a year with a Sharpe of 1.08 and a 54.37% hit rate from 1993 to 2013. Using real bid-ask quotes cut returns after decimalization to 4.46% a year ([Gao, Han, Li & Zhou](https://c.mql5.com/forextsd/forum/173/intraday_momentum_-_the_first_half-hour_return_predicts_the_last_half-hour_return.pdf)). The effect is insignificant on the lowest-volatility third of days and strongest on news days, with an R² of 11% on days when FOMC minutes are released. It appears across 16 developed markets, in and out of sample ([Li, Sakkas & Urquhart](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/finmar/v57y2022ics138641812100001x.html)). But it vanishes out of sample in one variant based on the overnight return ([Rosa](https://ideas.repec.org/a/wly/jfutmk/v42y2022i12p2218-2234.html)), and it was weaker in a 2012–2014 replication, with an R² of 0.014 ([CXO Advisory](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=25481)). A practitioner extension enters when price breaks out of a "noise area" around the open, sized from the average move over the previous 14 days. It reported a Sharpe of 1.33 after costs from 2007 to early 2024 ([Zarattini, Aziz & Barbon](https://www.sfi.ch/de/publications/n-24-97-beat-the-market-an-effective-intraday-momentum-strategy-for-s-p500-etf-spy)) and has not yet been independently replicated. News-driven price moves in single stocks keep drifting for days without reversing and stay profitable after costs ([Jiang, Li & Wang](https://ideas.repec.org:443/a/eee/jfinec/v141y2021i2p573-599.html)), which is the economic basis for the stocks-in-play idea.

Below 30 minutes the sign of the effect flips. Order imbalances predicted returns up to 30 minutes ahead in 1996, but only 5 minutes ahead by 2002 ([Chordia, Roll & Subrahmanyam](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/cfa-digest/2005/11/evidence-on-the-speed-of-convergence-to-market-efficiency-digest-summary)). Intraday autocorrelations are mostly negative, with the strongest reversal at about 15 minutes ([Baule, Schlie & Zhou](https://optionmetrics.com/?p=2611)). Serial correlation falls further when volatility is forecast to be high ([Bianco, Corsi & Renò](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0810.4912)). The horizon map for SPY and ES therefore has four bands. From ticks to one minute, returns reverse, but mostly through bid-ask bounce that someone crossing the spread cannot trade. At 5–15 minutes, temporary liquidity imbalances produce reversal that rewards passive execution. In the last 30 minutes there is momentum, conditional on volatility, news and gamma. Over several days, end-of-day moves partly reverse.

## Implied volatility, the clock and the calendar define what kind of day it is

### The options market prices the day's range, with a premium

Dividing the VIX by √252, about 15.9, gives the market's one-standard-deviation daily move: a VIX of 16 implies about ±1% ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/what-the-vix-and-vix-1-d-indices-attempt-to-measure-and-how-they-differ)). VIX1D, built from SPXW options expiring today and tomorrow and with history back to May 2022, prices today's event risk directly. It is computed in business minutes, freezes the near-term volatility in the last hour, and converges to the next day's expiry by 4:00 pm (same source).

Two corrections make these numbers usable. First, implied volatility carries a premium over realized. Since 2004, the VIX-implied daily move has exceeded the actual absolute close-to-close move about 80% of the time. But the implied range contained the actual high-low range only 59% of the time ([Advisor Perspectives](https://www.advisorperspectives.com/commentaries/2020/06/08/fear-and-volatility-1)), so stops and targets keyed to the expected move should be calibrated on the distribution of ranges. The premium can vanish in stress years: in 2022 the VIX averaged about 23 and realized volatility about 24 ([Cboe](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/what-the-vix-and-vix-1-d-indices-attempt-to-measure-and-how-they-differ)).

Second, event days need their own numbers. VIX1D rose an average 61.4% the day before CPI and FOMC releases and fell 24.8% on the day ([Cboe VIX1D in 11 charts](https://www.cboe.com/insights/posts/how-the-vix-1-d-index-performance-compares-to-the-vix-index-and-other-volatility-indices-in-11-charts/)). Taking the difference in variance between consecutive daily expiries isolated a 0.62% implied move for the July 2026 CPI day, against 0.55–0.58% for midweek days ([ORATS](https://orats.com/blog/bank-earnings-cpi-straddles)). The at-the-money straddle gives the same information directly: under a normal distribution, the expected absolute move is about 0.8 times the straddle price.

The variance risk premium predicts equity returns at quarterly horizons ([Bollerslev, Tauchen & Zhou](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/732839)). The slope of the VIX term structure predicts returns on volatility assets, not index direction ([Johnson 2017](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cup/jfinqa/v52y2017i06p2461-2490_00.html)). VVIX gauges tail risk ([Park, Fed FEDS 2013-54](https://www.federalreserve.gov/pubs/feds/2013/201354)). **No source found shows the VIX level or term structure predicting intraday direction.** Their supported uses are sizing, bracket width and regime flags. A backwardated term structure and VVIX spikes mark stress; a high VIX1D/VIX ratio flags risk concentrated in today's events.

### Realized volatility is the one thing you can forecast well

Unlike direction, volatility clusters and can be forecast reliably. The heterogeneous autoregressive (HAR-RV) model regresses next-day realized variance on its daily, weekly and monthly averages, and it remains a hard benchmark for machine learning to beat ([Corsi](https://academic.oup.com/jfec/article/7/2/174/856522); ["HARd to Beat"](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2406.08041)). The intraday U-shape has to be modeled explicitly. It distorts high-frequency volatility dynamics and makes a naive GARCH model on intraday returns misleading ([Andersen & Bollerslev](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/academics-research/research/detail/1997/intraday-periodicity-and-volatility-persistence-in-financial-markets/)).

The working pipeline forecasts the day's volatility with a HAR model on log realized variance and blends that forecast with VIX1D, after a haircut for the variance premium. It then spreads the result across the session with a time-of-day profile, using separate profiles for 8:30 and 14:00 event days, and uses the resulting expected volatility for each bar to set stop distances and position size.

At monthly horizons, scaling exposure inversely to forecast variance raised the market portfolio's Sharpe ratio by about 25% ([Moreira & Muir](https://www.nber.org/papers/22208)). Whether that carries over intraday is plausible but untested in the sources found. Its main benefit is keeping risk per trade stable across days whose ranges differ by a factor of three, not adding alpha. The volatility state also tells you which strategy to run. Intraday momentum gets stronger when volatility is high, while serial correlation at very short horizons turns more negative (the LeBaron effect). A fixed "fade" or "breakout" rule is therefore misspecified.

### Regime classification is plausible but unvalidated

Several frameworks for classifying the type of day are popular: Market Profile's initial-balance taxonomy of normal, trend and double-distribution days, hidden Markov models of volatility states, Hurst exponents, and Kaufman's efficiency ratio, |P_t − P_{t−n}|/Σ|ΔP| ([TA-Lib](https://ta-lib.org/functions/er.html)). No rigorous out-of-sample intraday evidence was found for any of them, and the hidden-Markov material for this use consists of tutorials and blogs ([QuantStart](https://www.quantstart.com/articles/market-regime-detection-using-hidden-markov-models-in-qstrader/)). Hurst estimates are a specific trap. On 514 sessions of one-minute E-mini data from 2024 to 2026, a rescaled-range estimate gave H ≈ 0.66, which suggests trending. The authors attribute that to bias from short-range autocorrelation, while a different estimator (GPH) indicated close to a random walk ([SSRN 6809080](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=6809080)).

A defensible approach labels historical days after the fact, using the close's position in the day's range, the range relative to the expected move, and the efficiency ratio over the full session. It then trains a classifier on first-hour features only: the first-hour move relative to the expected move, the width of the initial balance (the first-hour range) relative to its 20-day average, the efficiency ratio, market internals, the sign of dealer gamma and an event-day flag. The classifier should be evaluated walk-forward. Expect early classification to be probabilistic rather than decisive: the first half-hour explains only 1–3% of the variance in the last half-hour.

### Market internals diagnose participation, not direction

Market internals are widely watched. $TICK is the number of NYSE stocks last trading on an uptick minus those on a downtick, with ±1,000 conventionally treated as "extreme" ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/tick-index/)); $ADD, $VOLD and TRIN measure advancing versus declining issues and volume. No peer-reviewed evidence was found that they predict intraday index returns, and TRIN's predictive power has reportedly "drastically weakened" since the 2000s ([AAII](https://www.aaii.com/journal/article/322803-using-the-arms-index-trin-for-market-breadth-analysis)). Concentration makes internals that count every stock equally structurally misleading for cap-weighted indices. The top 10 stocks reached a record 40.7% of the S&P 500 in December 2025 ([Walnut Invest](https://walnutinvest.com/stats/market-concentration-statistics)), so SPX can rise on negative breadth for long stretches. Cap-weighted internals, such as the share of index weight trading above VWAP or a tick measure for mega caps, are the relevant inputs for SPX and QQQ. Equal-count internals suit IWM and broad risk appetite. Both belong in a day-type classifier rather than in an entry rule.

### Cross-asset lead-lag is gone at tradable speeds

In the 1980s, S&P futures led the cash index by 20–45 minutes ([Kawaller, Koch & Koch](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/jfinan/v42y1987i5p1309-29.html)), mostly because stale component prices made the index lag. By 2011 the ES–SPY gap was a 7 ms race ([Budish et al.](https://conference.nber.org/conf_papers/f69665.pdf)). Treasury cash and futures markets "can effectively be seen as one" at millisecond offsets ([NY Fed](https://libertystreeteconomics.newyorkfed.org/2015/08/high-frequency-cross-market-trading-in-us-treasury-markets)). No rigorous evidence was found of an exploitable minute-scale lead from Treasuries, the dollar, oil, bitcoin or mega caps into index futures.

Other markets still matter as slower context. Use ES or NQ as the signal instrument when trading SPY, QQQ or SPX options, because futures carry the freshest price nearly 23 hours a day. On macro days, use yields, the dollar and VIX futures to tell whether the market is trading interest rates or growth, and use the NQ/ES ratio as a gauge of concentration.

### Scheduled events reprice in milliseconds, then trend

SPY and ES respond to macro surprises **within 5 milliseconds**. Trading intensity jumps more than 100-fold, and the profit available to the fastest traders is only about $19,000 per event in SPY and $50,000 in ES ([Chordia, Green & Kottimukkalur](https://ideas.repec.org/a/oup/rfinst/v31y2018i12p4650-4687..html)). Before releases, E-mini price impact rises while traders hold back orders, and shocks "dissipate almost entirely within a second" ([Takahashi](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06788v2)). In the April 2025 tariff turmoil, E-mini book depth collapsed by about 90% ([Global Trading](https://www.globaltrading.net/?p=38057)). An algorithm that is not colocated has no edge on headline speed and faces maximum slippage risk around releases. The rule is to be flat or small from about two minutes before to one to five minutes after tier-one releases: 8:30 ET for CPI, NFP and PCE, 10:00 data, and the 14:00 FOMC statement with its 14:30 press conference. After that, lean toward following the trend, because intraday momentum is stronger on news days.

Most calendar anomalies have decayed. The pre-FOMC drift accounted for more than 80% of the equity premium from 1994 to 2011 ([Lucca & Moench](https://www.newyorkfed.org/medialibrary/media/research/staff_reports/sr512.pdf)), but it "essentially disappeared after 2015" ([Kurov, Wolfe & Gilbert](https://www.skidmore.edu/economics/documents/KurovWolfeGilbert-TheDisappearingPre-FOMC-Announce-Drift-200914.pdf)). The even-week pattern in the FOMC cycle ([Cieslak, Morse & Vissing-Jorgensen](https://stern.nyu.edu/sites/default/files/assets/documents/cycle_paper_cieslak_morse_vissingjorgensen.pdf)), the turn-of-month effect ([McConnell & Xu](https://rpc.cfainstitute.org/research/financial-analysts-journal/2008/equity-returns-at-the-turn-of-the-month)), month-end "dash for cash" reversals ([Etula et al.](https://academic.oup.com/rfs/article/33/1/75/5494694)) and strength in options-expiration weeks ([Stivers & Sun](https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jbfina/v37y2013i11p4226-4240.html)) are all documented. None has a clean test after publication, so at best they justify small daily size tilts.

The most reliable event facts are mechanical. Closing auctions are huge on rebalance and expiry days, such as Nasdaq's record $334 billion closing cross at the Russell reconstitution on June 26, 2026 ([Nasdaq](https://ir.nasdaq.com/news-releases/news-release-details/record-trading-volume-nasdaq-closing-cross-during-june-2026)). The Russell indexes will reconstitute semiannually for the first time on December 11, 2026 ([LSEG](https://www.lseg.com/en/media-centre/press-releases/ftse-russell/2026/ftse-russell-announces-december-2026-russell-us-indexes-reconstitution-schedule)). Social-media sentiment offers weak, short-lived intraday predictability that often reverses within the same day ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/sentiment-indicators/high-frequency-investor-sentiment-and-stock-returns)).

### The clock: act at the open and the close, wait at lunch

Volume, range and spreads follow a U-shape through the day. **The first 30 minutes** carry the most information and the widest spreads, and many participants avoid the first 15–30 minutes entirely ([NYSE](https://nyse.com/data-insights/opening-auction-filling-behaviors-opportunities-early-in-the-day)). This is the window for measuring the day type, at reduced size: the gap against the expected move, the initial balance, internals, and whether price holds above or below VWAP. **Late morning to early afternoon** is the lull, suited to mean reversion or to standing aside, and 0DTE spreads are narrowest from 10:00 to 14:00. **The last 30 minutes** carry momentum tied to gamma and leveraged-ETF hedging; market-on-close imbalances are published near 15:50, and rebalance flows dominate on calendar days.

One structural fact constrains every design that is flat at the close. Historically the US equity premium was earned overnight, with daytime returns near zero ([Cliff, Cooper & Gulen, via arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.04481); [Lou, Polk & Skouras](https://personal.lse.ac.uk/polk/research/TugOfWar.pdf)), although the split has been unstable in recent periods ([CXO](https://www.cxoadvisory.com/?p=27932)). An intraday-only algorithm gives up that passive premium, so its edge must come from intraday structure, not from simply being long.

## A disciplined research pipeline matters more than the model

### Start from who pays you and why

Professional research starts from an economic mechanism: a risk premium, a behavioral bias, or a structural flow such as index rebalancing, leveraged-ETF and option hedging, or benchmarked execution. It then asks who is on the other side of the trade and why they accept a loss. The base rate for discoveries is sobering. Across 316 published factors, accounting for data mining implies a new finding should clear a t-statistic of 3.0 rather than 2.0 ([Harvey, Liu & Zhu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w20592)). Across 97 published predictors, returns were 26% lower out of sample and 58% lower after publication ([McLean & Pontiff](https://Www.Gwern.net/doc/economics/2016-mclean.pdf)).

Strategy families sort into three groups for this trader. Latency arbitrage and passive market making in large caps and ES are **not winnable**: they are speed and queue games that a trader without colocation cannot win. Passive liquidity provision in names with wider spreads, and alpha from book imbalance, are **marginal**, and both need queue simulation on L2/L3 data. The **viable** families, provided costs are honestly modeled, are intraday momentum and seasonality, event-driven trades at horizons where hundreds of milliseconds don't matter, mean reversion and pairs over minutes to hours, and trend or reversion strategies conditioned on volatility.

Daily pairs trading earned up to 11% a year from 1962 to 2002, with profits declining after 1988 ([Gatev, Goetzmann & Rouwenhorst](https://nber.org/papers/w7032)); no intraday pairs evidence was retrieved. Before testing, write down the universe, period, signal and success metric. Count every variant you try. Keep the most recent 6–12 months of data untouched until the end.

### Sample and transform data so statistics mean something

Bars that close after a fixed dollar amount, volume or number of trades give returns closer to normal than bars that close on the clock ([Alpaca](https://alpaca.markets/learn/alternative-bars-02)). A common sizing rule of about 1/50 of a day's dollar value gives roughly 50 bars a day ([mlfinpy](https://mlfinpy.readthedocs.io/en/latest/FinancialDataStructure.html)). Fractional differencing looks for the smallest amount of differencing that makes a series stationary while keeping as much of its memory as possible ([AFML summary](https://skillsmp.com/creators/nutdnuy/afml-book-skill/github-skills-lopez-de-prado-financial-ml)).

The feature families for intraday work are returns over several lookbacks scaled by volatility, plus the overnight gap; realized volatility and range-based estimators; volume and volatility normalized by time of day, without which a model simply "learns" the open and the close; distances to VWAP, the opening range and the previous day's levels; order-book features such as imbalance, OFI, micro-price and spread in ticks; context from related markets; and calendar flags. In large-scale machine-learning studies of return prediction, the features that matter are recent trends and reversals, liquidity and volatility ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)), the same ingredients. A legitimate intraday signal explains a few percent of variance at most. A backtest that shows much more should be presumed to contain a bug or look-ahead.

### Label trades the way they exit

Labels based on a fixed horizon ignore the price path and the volatility regime. The triple-barrier method labels each event by whichever of three barriers it touches first: a profit target scaled to volatility, a stop scaled to volatility, or a time limit. That mirrors a real bracket order. Meta-labeling then separates the direction of a trade from its size. A simple, interpretable primary rule proposes trades, for example the sign of the first half-hour return or an opening-range break. A secondary classifier predicts whether each proposed trade will hit its profit target first, and its probability is used to filter and size the trade ([fynance](https://fynance.readthedocs.io/en/latest/features.labels.html)). The economic hypothesis lives in the primary rule; machine learning only decides when to trust it.

Labels that overlap in time are not independent. Each sample should be weighted by its average uniqueness: over its lifetime, the average of one divided by the number of labels active at the same time ([fynance](https://fynance.readthedocs.io/en/master/generated/fynance.features.labels.uniqueness_weights.html)). The problem is severe intraday. If a new 30-minute label starts every minute, each bar is covered by about 30 labels, the effective sample is about 1/30 of the rows, and naive t-statistics are inflated about 5.5 times. Barriers should also be set at multiples of the round-trip cost, not just of volatility, or the labels will be dominated by bid-ask bounce.

### Use regularized trees first and be skeptical of complexity

Return prediction has a tiny signal-to-noise ratio. In Gu, Kelly and Xiu's monthly study, the out-of-sample R² was −3.46% for ordinary least squares with all predictors, 0.34% for boosted trees, and a peak of 0.40% for a three-layer neural network. The gains came from interactions between predictors, and even that small R² raised a market-timing Sharpe from 0.51 to 0.77 ([Gu, Kelly & Xiu](https://www.nber.org/papers/w25398.pdf)). Kelly, Malamud and Zhou argue that heavily over-parameterized models improve out-of-sample prediction ([SFI](https://www.sfi.ch/en/publications/the-virtue-of-complexity-in-return-prediction)). But their gains appear in monthly market timing, driven by predicting recessions, and the claim is contested.

For noisy tabular intraday features, a pragmatic modeling order starts with regularized linear or logistic models to check whether any signal exists. It moves next to gradient-boosted trees, kept shallow, with subsampling, monotonic constraints where theory supports them, and early stopping on purged folds. Deep sequence models come last, and only with very large tick datasets.

Reinforcement learning has credible results in execution, a narrow problem with clear benchmarks ([Nevmyvaka, Feng & Kearns](https://www.cis.upenn.edu/~mkearns/papers/rlexec)). But agents trained on replayed history "exhibit considerable overfitting which prevents them from real deployment" ([Zhang et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.11685)), because replay simulators cannot model the trader's own impact or adverse selection.

### Validate against the number of things you tried

A backtest's Sharpe ratio means nothing without three facts: how many variants were tried, how long the sample is, and the shape of the return distribution. Bailey and López de Prado show that the expected maximum Sharpe among N independent trials with zero skill is SR₀ = √V·[(1 − γ)Z⁻¹(1 − 1/N) + γZ⁻¹(1 − 1/(Ne))], where V is the variance of the trials' Sharpe ratios, γ is Euler's constant (about 0.577) and Z⁻¹ is the inverse normal distribution. Their deflated Sharpe ratio (DSR) tests the chosen strategy against that threshold, adjusting for skew and fat tails. In their example, an annualized Sharpe of 2.5 over five years of daily data, chosen from 100 trials with negative skew and fat tails, has a DSR of only 0.90, which is not significant at 95% ([Bailey & López de Prado](https://www.davidhbailey.com/dhbpapers/deflated-sharpe.pdf)). The same arithmetic sets a minimum backtest length. With five years of data, trying more than about 45 independent configurations makes an in-sample Sharpe of 1.0 the expected result of pure noise.

The probability of backtest overfitting (PBO) is estimated by repeatedly splitting the data into in-sample and out-of-sample halves. It measures how often the in-sample winner ranks below the out-of-sample median; above 0.5, the selection process is worse than random ([Bailey, Borwein, López de Prado & Zhu](https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=2326253)). Ordinary k-fold cross-validation leaks information through overlapping labels. Purging removes training samples whose labels overlap the test period, an embargo drops a buffer of samples right after it, and combinatorial purged cross-validation (CPCV) produces a distribution of out-of-sample paths rather than one ([LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/purged-cross-validation.md)). Intraday, keep whole days together in a fold and embargo across the overnight gap.

A useful rule of thumb: a Sharpe ratio's t-statistic is roughly the Sharpe times √(years of data). A true Sharpe of 1.0 therefore takes about four years to reach t ≈ 2 before any correction for multiple testing, and a month or two of live P&L says almost nothing.

Before committing capital, a strategy should pass a sequence of gates. It needs a stated economic hypothesis, then purged cross-validation or CPCV, then a DSR above 0.95 using an honest count of trials, and a PBO below about 0.2–0.3. Only then does it get one look at the untouched holdout, followed by paper trading and small live trading with slippage monitored. Sizing should assume about half the backtested edge will disappear.

### Most intraday backtests fail in the fills

Execution assumptions break more intraday backtests than signal math does. The common errors are deciding and filling on the same bar, and filling market orders at the mid or the close, which quietly adds back half the spread on each side and is often the entire edge. Others are treating a touch of a limit price as a fill, ignoring queue position and adverse selection, and ignoring retail latency of tens to hundreds of milliseconds. Bad prints create phantom mean-reversion profits, and auctions, halts, LULD price bands and futures rolls are routinely mishandled.

Tools like hftbacktest model feed and order latency, and model queue position with fill probabilities that follow a power law ([hftbacktest](https://hftbacktest.readthedocs.io/)). NautilusTrader runs the same event-driven code in simulation and in live trading ([Quantt comparison](https://www.quantt.co.uk/resources/backtesting-platforms-comparison)). A sound workflow screens ideas with fast vectorized bar backtests (for example vectorbt), then re-tests the survivors event by event with L2/L3 data and latency. Comparing the two measures how much the execution assumptions inflated the first result. Backtrader, still common in tutorials, has had no release since April 2023 (same source).

### Model costs as implementation shortfall and size with fractional Kelly

Implementation shortfall measures every cost against the price at the moment of decision: commissions and taxes, spread and impact, delay, and the opportunity cost of orders that never fill ([Quantitative Brokers on Perold](https://www.quantitativebrokers.com/blog/a-brief-history-of-implementation-shortfall)). For a small trader in ES or SPY, impact is negligible, and spread plus fees dominate. Turnover turns small per-trade costs into large annual drags: 10 round trips a day at 1 bp each costs 25% of capital a year at 1× exposure. Almgren-Chriss optimal execution, extended to square-root impact ([Fields Institute](https://www.fields.utoronto.ca/talks/Optimal-execution-nonlinear-cost-functions-and-trading-enhanced-risk)), matters mainly when scaling into thin names or into the close.

For sizing, the Kelly fraction for continuous returns is μ/σ². But full Kelly carries a 50% chance of a 50% drawdown at some point. Half-Kelly cuts that chance to 12.5% and keeps about 75% of the growth rate ([MacLean, Thorp & Ziemba](https://econpapers.repec.org/bookchap/wsiwsbook/7598.htm); [LuxAlgo](https://www.luxalgo.com/library/concept/kelly-criterion/)). Betting too much is far more costly than betting too little. Apply ¼ to ½ Kelly to a Sharpe that has already been deflated for the number of trials and haircut about 50% for decay. Volatility targeting sets position size to target volatility divided by forecast volatility, using volatility normalized by time of day.

Hard controls belong in code: a daily loss limit of about 2–3 times daily volatility, stops on each trade scaled to volatility, caps on gross and net exposure, and a kill switch for data or connectivity failures. Add a pause on any strategy whose live drawdown exceeds 1.5–2 times its backtested maximum, which doubles as a check for overfitting.

### "Quantum trading" is a mislabel, and quantum computing is irrelevant here

"Quantum trading" almost certainly means quant trading. As of October 2026, actual quantum computing has no demonstrated production advantage in trading. The headline HSBC–IBM trial of September 2025 reported a 34% improvement in predicting whether European corporate-bond requests for quote would fill, using features generated on IBM's Heron processor. It was a research result on fill probability, not live trading or profit ([IBM](https://www.ibm.com/quantum/blog/hsbc-algorithmic-bond-trading); [Markets Media](https://www.marketsmedia.com/hsbc-trials-quantum-enabled-algo-trading-with-ibm/)). The authors did not claim quantum advantage ([PostQuantum](https://postquantum.com/industry-news/hsbc-ibm-quantum-advantage/)), and Scott Aaronson said the claim had "more red flags than a People's Liberation Army parade" ([FI-Desk](https://www.fi-desk.com/quantum-computing-breakthrough-has-more-red-flags-than-a-peoples-liberation-army-parade/)). JPMorgan and Quantinuum's certified-randomness result in Nature (March 2025) is a cryptographic building block, not a trading tool ([JPMorgan](https://www.jpmorgan.com/technology/news/certified-randomness)). Cloud quantum hardware is too slow for intraday decision loops. A 34% improvement over unspecified baselines, with no test against simply injecting classical noise, is exactly the kind of claim the deflated-Sharpe discipline discounts. Retail products branded "quantum AI" deserve suspicion.

## A buildable plan for a non-colocated trader in 2026

### Infrastructure is cheap; the binding constraint is cost per trade

A working stack costs roughly $50–$400 a month. CME non-professional market data cost $1.55 a month for top of book and $12.10 for depth, per exchange, from June 2026, rising slightly in January 2027 ([CME fee list](https://www.cmegroup.com/market-data/files/june-2026-market-data-fee-list.pdf)). Databento's CME Standard plan for historical and live data is $199 a month ([Databento](https://databento.com/blog/updates-to-subscription-pricing)). Tradovate's API add-on is $25 a month, but CME sub-vendor licensing for data delivered through the API has reportedly cost $290–$500 a month ([Tradovate community](https://community.tradovate.com/t/is-cme-sub-vendor-requirement-for-api-access-is-290-per-month/6215)). Interactive Brokers' default API data are 250 ms snapshots, and its tick-by-tick data are capped by the number of market-data lines and unavailable for options ([IBKR](https://interactivebrokers.github.io/tws-api/top_data.html)). A Chicago VPS costs $40–80 a month, with vendor-claimed sub-millisecond latency to CME ([QuantVPS via Optimus](https://optimusfutures.com/quantvps.php)). For a trader doing 20 ES round trips a day, these fixed costs are trivial next to about $82,000 a year of trading costs per contract.

Regulation changed in 2026. The SEC approved removing FINRA's pattern-day-trader rule on April 14, 2026, effective June 4, 2026. A general standard for intraday margin deficits replaces the $25,000 requirement, and brokers may phase the change in until October 20, 2027 ([WilmerHale](https://www.wilmerhale.com/en/insights/client-alerts/20260423-sec-approves-amendments-to-finra-rule-4210-replacing-day-trading-margin-requirements-with-a-modernized-intraday-margin-standard)). Futures were never subject to the rule. Section 1256 contracts generally fall outside the wash-sale rule, while equity options remain subject to it ([NexusFi](https://nexusfi.com/a/regulation/wash-sale-rule-futures-section-1256)). That comes from secondary sources and is worth confirming with a tax professional. Prop-firm evaluations are not a funding shortcut: the economics are negative for most participants, the trading is mostly simulated, and trailing-drawdown rules interact badly with scalping strategies that have fat-tailed losses.

### An architecture that follows from the evidence

The evidence points to a layered design. The **instrument layer** trades ES where capital allows, or MES with the knowledge that its fees cost about a tick per round trip. It uses SPY for equity-index exposure, uses SPX or XSP only to express views on realized versus implied volatility, and takes ES and NQ as the signal source for everything. The **context layer** runs before and during the session. It holds a HAR-RV forecast blended with VIX1D, a time-of-day profile of volatility and volume, an event calendar with stand-aside windows, a gamma-regime estimate checked against realized short-horizon autocorrelation, cap-weighted internals and a first-hour day-type classifier.

The **signal layer** is a short list of primary rules, each with a stated economic mechanism and a holding period of minutes to hours. Candidates are intraday momentum into the close, noise-area or opening-range breaks conditioned on volatility and relative volume, continuation after scheduled releases, and possibly breakouts in single-name stocks in play. A **meta-labeling layer**, a regularized gradient-boosted classifier trained on context features with triple-barrier labels and uniqueness weights, decides whether to take each primary signal and how large to make it. The **execution layer** uses the micro-price, queue imbalance and OFI to decide whether to rest an order or cross the spread, measures markouts on every fill, and never leaves orders resting through scheduled releases. The **risk layer** applies volatility targeting, fractional Kelly on a deflated and haircut Sharpe, a daily loss limit, exposure caps and kill switches.

The **research harness** must exist before any of these layers is trusted. It holds MBO or MBP-10 data, an event-driven simulator with latency and queue models, purged cross-validation, DSR with an honest trial count, PBO, an untouched holdout, and live tracking of implementation shortfall. The instinct behind "many small trades" survives here in a disciplined form. Breadth comes from trading several independent, condition-dependent effects across instruments and times of day, each at a horizon where cost is a small share of the move. It does not come from slicing one prediction into tick-sized pieces.

## Conclusion

The thread running through every part of this field is that statistical predictability and the ability to profit from it move in opposite directions. Queue imbalance predicts the next tick with an AUC near 0.8, but the move it predicts is exactly one tick, which is what crossing the spread costs. Order-flow imbalance explains up to 87% of a price change in the same interval and about 3% of the next one. Deep order-book models reach over 90% accuracy on their most confident predictions and still produce no tradable signal. Indicator rules look significant by the hundreds until the number of rules tested is counted. For a trader who is not colocated, the useful question is therefore not "what will the next price be?" but "under what conditions is a small, well-understood effect strong enough to clear its cost?" Those conditions are volatility state, dealer positioning, event timing, relative volume and time of day. Read this way, the premise reverses: what is easier is not making many small price predictions but making many conditional decisions about when to apply a few modest, economically grounded bets, and how large to make them.

This has concrete consequences for the design phase that follows. Choose the holding horizon first: horizons where round-trip cost is no more than about 5% of the typical move, meaning minutes to hours in ES. Choose instruments second: ES over micros, SPX over SPY for options, futures quotes as the reference price. Make the first deliverable the cost-and-fill simulator and the evaluation harness, because any surviving edge will have an R² of a few percent and must be told apart from luck over 1,600 or more trades. Expect live results at about half the backtest.
